The Ash Ledger

Player Briefing · r002

Mission Context

On April 18, 1906, San Francisco is struck by a catastrophic earthquake. The disaster itself is not your target. Aletheia has identified a historical discontinuity in its aftermath: something that should survive the destruction does not.

You are inserting into a city of damaged streets, spreading fires, broken communications, injured civilians, improvised relief stations, and fragmented authority. Your greatest advantages are preparation, teamwork, and knowing that history contains a missing piece. Your greatest danger is behaving like people who do not belong there.

Apocryphon

At six past the falling hour, the woman with the blue pencil counts what the fire will erase. Brick remembers weight. Mud remembers water. A ledger crosses the bay twice, though no ferry carries it. When the third copy is made, seven men stop asking whether the city fell and begin asking why it stood.

What Aletheia Will Confirm

  • The earthquake itself must not be prevented.
  • The discontinuity begins in the disaster’s aftermath.
  • The Apocryphon is symbolic but operationally useful; you are not expected to solve it before insertion.
  • The mission may require preserving a person, object, practice, record, relationship, or some combination.
  • Extraction is scheduled for the Ferry Building south arcade at 23:40 local.

Before Insertion

Choose a cover

Surveyor, relief worker, insurance investigator, medical volunteer, telegraph technician, or another role your Vector can make believable through useful action.

Choose your red line

What future capability will your Vector refuse to expose unless a life or the mission clearly demands it?

Vector Questions

  • What do you initially think “the third copy” means?
  • When disaster creates innocent people who need help, how much mission time will your Vector spend helping them?
  • Does your Vector believe restoring history and changing history are meaningfully different?
