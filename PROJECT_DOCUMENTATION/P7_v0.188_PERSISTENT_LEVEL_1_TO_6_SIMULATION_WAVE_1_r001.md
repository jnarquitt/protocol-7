# Protocol 7 v0.188 — Persistent Level 1–6 Simulation Wave 1 r001

**Status:** FIRST ADVANCEMENT STRESS TEST — AMBER-GREEN

## Assumptions tested

This wave uses the current v0.188 candidates:
- Level 1 starts at 20 Skill Ranks and 16 BAR.
- Each new Level grants +4 Skill Ranks.
- BAR progresses 16 / 18 / 21 / 24 / 27 / 30.
- Ability/Core Growth opportunities occur at Levels 3 and 5.
- Skill Mastery becomes available at Level 4.
- A Mastery Die equals the current Skill die and only enters the pool through a qualifying Mastery VAM.
- Mastery VAMs usually cost 7–9 BAR.
- AP remains 3; normal Reaction remains 1.
- HP uses the fixed-18-plus-half-STR advancement model only as a simulation candidate, not canon.

## Persistent profiles

Six long-form advancement profiles were followed conceptually through the same Level 1→6 sequence:

1. **Field Operator** — combat/tactics specialist.
2. **Investigator** — evidence/information specialist.
3. **Diplomat** — social/cover specialist.
4. **Engineer** — technical specialist.
5. **Recon Generalist** — broad mobility/perception character.
6. **Non-Mastery Generalist** — deliberately refuses Skill Mastery and spends BAR on several veteran non-Mastery systems.

Each profile retained its original Level 1 identity while adding new authorization rather than replacing its starting VAM package wholesale.

## Level-by-level feel

### Level 1 — competent but bounded
The 16-BAR starting presets support clear identities. Most builds carry several inexpensive VAMs and must still make meaningful omissions.

**Result:** PASS.

### Level 2 — specialization starts
+4 Skill Ranks plus 2 BAR and Advanced I access creates a noticeable improvement. This level works best when a player crosses at least one Skill breakpoint or gains a strong signature VAM such as Counterintelligence, Deep Cover, Protective Intercept, Disarm Protocol, or equivalent.

**Risk:** if all four Skill Ranks become Progress Ranks and the chosen L2 VAM is passive, Level 2 can feel quieter than desired.

**Result:** AMBER-GREEN.

### Level 3 — first major integration jump
21 BAR, another +4 Skill Ranks, first Core Growth opportunity, and High-Integration VAMs produce a strong milestone. Character identity broadens without yet changing the normal practical dice ceiling.

**Result:** PASS.

### Level 4 — true watershed
24 BAR plus first Skill Mastery is the largest qualitative jump in the tested progression. The character can devote roughly one third of active capacity to a Mastery VAM while retaining approximately the entire Level 1 capacity for other modules.

This feels appropriately dramatic for the campaign midpoint-to-veteran transition.

**Result:** STRONG PASS, with power monitoring required.

### Level 5 — elite breadth
27 BAR plus second Core Growth opportunity and Elite VAM access makes the Vector substantially more flexible. The best Level 5 experience was not 'my Mastery die gets bigger again' but 'I can now maintain Mastery while also carrying advanced team, rescue, social, or technical systems.'

**Result:** PASS.

### Level 6 — capstone integration
30 BAR is high but did not automatically erase loadout tension in the provisional catalog because the most powerful systems cost 8–12 BAR. A Level 6 Vector can maintain one major capstone/Mastery architecture plus a meaningful collection of evergreen low-BAR VAMs, but still cannot load every attractive high-tier system.

**Result:** PASS FOR FURTHER TESTING.

## Dice-pool benchmark

Exact Protocol Dice distributions were calculated for representative competent pools. These are examples rather than universal character builds.

| Benchmark | Pool | Average score | DC 12 | DC 16 | DC 20 | DC 24 |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| L1 competent | d8 d8 d6 d8 d6 | 16.25 | 76.0% | 55.2% | 31.4% | 14.0% |
| L3 improved | d10 d8 d6 d10 d6 | 18.55 | 83.5% | 66.5% | 45.3% | 25.0% |
| L4 Mastery | d10 d8 d6 d10 d6 **+ d10 Mastery** | 23.45 | 93.1% | 83.7% | 69.0% | 50.3% |
| L6 Mastery example | d10 d10 d6 d12 d6 **+ d12 Mastery** | 26.80 | 96.0% | 90.1% | 79.9% | 65.1% |

## Major mathematical finding

**Skill Mastery is powerful enough to define Level 4.**

In the representative pool, adding a d10 Mastery Die raises average score from 18.55 to 23.45 and raises the chance of reaching DC 20 from about 45% to about 69%.

That is a large but desirable specialist jump if three safeguards remain:

1. the Skill must actually be Mastered;
2. a costly VAM must be loaded;
3. the VAM's condition must apply.

This also confirms that routine seven-die escalation would be dangerous. Six dice already make a veteran specialist extremely reliable.

## Mastery versus non-Mastery build

The deliberately non-Mastery Level 4–6 profile remained viable when its 7–9 BAR was instead invested in multiple strong VAMs covering action timing, investigation, protection, social control, or team support.

The Mastery build wins at repeated rolls in one chosen domain. The non-Mastery build wins in breadth and simultaneous problem coverage.

**Initial verdict:** desirable tradeoff. Do not make Mastery mandatory.

## BAR findings

### 16 BAR at Level 1
Healthy. Starting packages remain constrained and distinct.

### 18–21 BAR at Levels 2–3
Healthy. The extra space is noticeable without feeling excessive.

### 24 BAR at Level 4
Very strong fit for 7–9 BAR Mastery systems. This is the most convincing point in favor of the revised BAR curve.

### 27–30 BAR at Levels 5–6
Still creates choices because elite/capstone systems consume large chunks of capacity. However, optimized-loadout testing is still required before promotion.

## Low-tier retention

Every strong Level 5–6 test loadout retained at least some 1–3 BAR VAMs because they efficiently filled remaining capacity and provided narrow permissions that expensive VAMs did not replace.

**Result:** PASS. The catalog is currently avoiding the 'old powers become trash' problem.

## Enemy Mastery

Peer-level Opposition with one Mastered Skill creates a strong table signal. A six-die enemy is substantially more threatening without requiring inflated HP or universal bonuses.

Recommended use:
- rare or absent at Levels 1–3;
- introduced sparingly at Level 4;
- more common among named elite Opposition at Levels 5–6;
- ordinary historical adversaries generally remain below this ceiling.

## HP candidate finding

The fixed 18 HP + half current STR maximum per new Level remains plausible but is not yet cleared. It creates visible durability growth, but STR optimization must be tested separately because increasing STR before later level gains improves future HP awards.

Do not promote HP rules from this wave.

## Problems found

### 1. Level 2 can occasionally feel quiet
If no Skill breakpoint is crossed, Level 2 needs its new authorization choice to carry more emotional weight. This may be solved by presentation and VAM choice rather than more raw power.

### 2. Mastery language needs a precise pool rule
The final rules must explicitly distinguish:
- normal Skill Die;
- Skill Mastery Die;
- situational fifth die from gear/VAM/Advantage;
- the intended six-die practical ceiling.

### 3. Starting packages share several utility VAMs
This is mechanically healthy but may create visual sameness. The app should emphasize signature VAMs and recommended upgrade paths rather than merely listing package contents.

### 4. Command Link placement remains unresolved
It is thematic enough to tempt Level 1 placement but strong enough to function as an advancement reward. Test both placements before final catalog lock.

## Wave 1 verdict

**AMBER-GREEN.**

The proposed advancement architecture is working well enough to continue. No evidence currently supports reducing the 16/18/21/24/27/30 BAR curve. Skill Mastery is powerful but appears controllable at 7–9 BAR. Six dice are sufficiently strong that the design should continue treating them as the veteran ceiling.
