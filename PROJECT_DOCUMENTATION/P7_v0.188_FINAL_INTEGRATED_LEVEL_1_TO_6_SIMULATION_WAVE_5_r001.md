# Protocol 7 v0.188 — Final Integrated Level 1–6 Simulation Wave 5 r001

**Status:** FINAL INTEGRATED SIMULATION REPORT — GREEN with targeted revisions

## Scope

Wave 5 combines the candidate advancement architecture rather than testing each subsystem in isolation:

- 18 starting HP and STR-based later HP;
- BAR 16/18/21/24/27/30;
- +4 Skill Ranks each Level after Level 1;
- Ability growth at Levels 3 and 5;
- Level 2 Edge;
- first Skill Mastery at Level 4;
- second Mastered Skill at Level 6;
- rebuilt VAM authorization bands;
- one ordinary bonus-die slot shared by Feature/Assist/gear/situation sources;
- Mastery Die as the veteran exception;
- peer opposition using Mastery at upper Levels;
- Matched Blanks observed as a secondary critical-effect layer, not used to tune base success math.

## Persistent party

The simulation used recurring archetypal Vectors rather than isolated snapshots:

1. **Operator** — combat specialist; Driven Edge; ranged Mastery at L4, tactical/leadership Mastery at L6.
2. **Ghost** — infiltration/recon generalist; Inspired Edge; investigation/search Mastery at L4, social/cover Mastery at L6.
3. **Interpreter** — social/investigative specialist; Connected Edge; behavioral/social Mastery at L4, investigation Mastery at L6.
4. **Fabricator** — technical/medical specialist; Prepared Edge; technical Mastery at L4, medical Mastery at L6.
5. **Bulwark-Low-STR control** — deliberately fragile build taking Durable at L2.
6. **Bulwark-High-STR control** — high-STR build taking Durable at L2 to test stacking of innate durability plus Edge HP.

Each was advanced persistently through all six Levels, retaining earlier VAMs and making later loadout choices under the current authorization bands.

## Level-by-level experience

### Level 1 — GREEN
16 BAR remains sufficient to establish a strong identity without giving away veteran systems. The rebuilt Standard catalog is broad enough for combat, recon, social, technical, rescue, and command identities.

### Level 2 — GREEN after Edge addition
This was previously the quietest advancement. Edge fixes that problem. The character receives a small personal declaration in addition to ordinary Skill/BAR growth.

The Edge does not behave like a class because it imposes no future restrictions and does not determine VAM or Mastery choices.

### Level 3 — GREEN
21 BAR plus the first Ability step creates a clear intrinsic-growth moment. Characters begin carrying richer configurations without yet accessing the sixth-die Mastery layer.

### Level 4 — STRONG GREEN
24 BAR plus first Skill Mastery remains the campaign's largest mechanical watershed. A 7–9 BAR Mastery VAM is expensive enough to define a configuration but leaves approximately Level-1-scale capacity for other systems.

Peer enemies using Mastery feel qualitatively different without requiring inflated HP.

### Level 5 — GREEN
27 BAR, Elite authorization, and the second Ability step provide breadth and refinement after the dramatic L4 change. Elite VAMs need to remain focused on options/team interaction rather than simply larger bonus dice.

### Level 6 — GREEN
30 BAR plus Continuance authorization and a second Mastered Skill works as a capstone. The second Mastered Skill broadens the veteran; it does not add a second Mastery Die to one roll.

30 BAR still does not permit an optimized Vector to load every desirable veteran VAM. An 8-BAR Mastery system plus an 11–12 BAR Continuance system consumes 19–20 BAR, leaving 10–11 BAR for foundations and utility. Loadout identity therefore survives.

## Edge comparison

### Driven — reroll one die once per session
This is mechanically stronger on a decisive roll than simply adding a d4 because the player can target a poor result on a valuable die. That is acceptable for a once-per-session identity resource, but the reroll must keep the new result and cannot recursively reroll.

A representative five-die pool [d10,d8,d8,d6,d4] averaged approximately 15.9 under Protocol scoring in Monte Carlo testing. Adding a d4 increased the average to about 16.9; an optimally targeted one-die reroll increased the average to about 19.4. Against a sample threshold of 20, baseline success was about 30%, the added d4 about 36%, and the targeted reroll about 52%.

**Revision:** Driven should be declared **after the roll but before outcome narration**, and remains the strongest pure-roll Edge. This is acceptable only because it is once per session.

### Inspired — add d4 once per session
Smaller than Driven but uniquely useful when every die is already acceptable and the character simply needs more output. It also creates another blank-bearing die and interacts naturally with Matched Blanks.

**Revision:** Inspired's d4 occupies the ordinary bonus-die slot. If that slot is already occupied, Inspired may **replace** the existing ordinary bonus die with its d4 only if desired; it does not create a seventh die.

### Prepared — small plausible preparation once per session
Strong in investigative, infiltration, survival, and technical scenes. It is difficult to reduce to a single probability but repeatedly converts a hard problem into a better-shaped problem rather than automatic success.

**Guardrail:** cannot create a VAM, unique future technology, major weapon, rare credential, or fact that contradicts established fiction.

### Connected — plausible contact/access once per session
Strongest in social/investigative play but remains bounded because the contact supplies opportunity, information access, introduction, or communication—not automatic truth, loyalty, equipment, or success.

### Durable — +5 maximum HP permanently
Durable is powerful at Level 2 and steadily becomes a smaller percentage of total HP. It is especially attractive to low-STR specialists but does not erase the durability advantage of STR investment.

Low-STR d4 path: 18 → 25 at L2 with Durable → 27 → 29 → 31 → 33 at L6.
Typical unchanged d6 without Durable: 18 → 21 → 24 → 27 → 30 → 33.
Typical d6 with Durable: 18 → 26 → 29 → 32 → 35 → 38.
High unchanged d8 with Durable: 18 → 27 → 31 → 35 → 39 → 43.

**Finding:** +5 is substantial but not mandatory. The low-STR Durable character essentially purchases enough durability to finish near the ordinary d6 character, while the high-STR Durable build becomes deliberately tanky at the cost of having no renewable Edge trick.

## Assist / Feature slot

**GREEN.** Treating Assist, VAM Feature dice, ordinary gear bonus dice, and ordinary situational bonus dice as competing sources for one bonus-die slot is the cleanest anti-stacking rule.

This does not make Assist useless. Assistance can:
- supply the bonus die when the acting character has none;
- provide a better die than the acting character's available Feature;
- provide non-die positioning/preparation effects where a rule says so;
- help non-Mastery characters reach their normal enhanced pool.

Mastery remains its own source class and can coexist with the single ordinary bonus die.

## Practical dice ceiling

The integrated test supports:

**Core Pool + one ordinary Bonus Die + one Skill Mastery Die.**

This produces the intended common upper veteran pool without normalizing seven-plus dice. Any seventh die requires explicit exceptional text and should be rare enough to feel like a special override.

## HP / lethality

STR-based HP remains preferable to flat +3 because it preserves character texture and creates a meaningful long-term Strength choice. The pre-advancement sequencing rule prevents same-Level exploitation.

High-Level encounters should not simply scale weapon damage upward to chase HP. Pressure should increasingly come from peer competence, coordinated threats, denied options, mission clocks, civilians, Exposure/Carrier stakes, environmental hazards, and simultaneous objectives.

## Mastery opposition

Upper-Level peer enemies should use Mastery selectively. A peer specialist with six dice communicates danger immediately. Giving every enemy Mastery would erase the signal.

Recommended encounter language:
- ordinary historical threat: no Mastery;
- trained/elite historical threat: stronger Skills/Features but generally no Mastery;
- Opposition specialist / temporal peer: one Mastered Skill with access mechanism;
- major recurring peer/capstone enemy: one or two Mastered Skills, never two Mastery dice on one roll.

## Matched Blanks observation

The narrowed Matched Blanks concept did not destabilize advancement because it is resolved after the ordinary action result and is not used to inflate success totals.

Do not finalize its effect table as part of the advancement freeze. Continue separate probability/effect testing. It may replace part of the emotional role of secondary critical effects, but ordinary crits remain intact.

## Final Wave 5 verdict

**GREEN.**

The advancement architecture is strong enough to consolidate as the **v0.188 advancement candidate** with the following targeted wording revisions:

- Level 2 gains one Edge.
- Driven is once/session, reroll one die after seeing the roll; keep the new result.
- Inspired is once/session d4 and obeys/replaces the ordinary bonus-die slot rather than stacking beyond it.
- Durable is +5 maximum HP.
- Prepared and Connected remain bounded horizontal options.
- Assist/Feature/gear/situation dice share one ordinary bonus-die slot.
- Level 4 grants first Mastered Skill.
- Level 6 grants second Mastered Skill.
- only one Mastery Die can enter a roll.
- 16/18/21/24/27/30 BAR survives.
- 18 starting HP + half pre-advancement STR maximum per later Level survives.
- 3 AP / 1 Reaction survives.
- no flat numeric roll/damage/defense bonuses.
