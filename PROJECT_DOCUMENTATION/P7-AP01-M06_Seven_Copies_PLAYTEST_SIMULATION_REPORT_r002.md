# P7-AP01-M06 — Seven Copies
## Playtest Simulation Report r002

**Rules baseline:** Protocol 7 v0.187 PRE-BETA  
**Intended team:** Level 6 Vectors  
**Format:** two-session capstone  
**Result:** AMBER-GREEN first-pass; suitable for campaign audit, not release-ready.

## 1 — Investigative / experienced table
Players infer early that the seven objects are relational rather than redundant and aggressively question Aletheia.

**Break found:** the finale can lose momentum if the GM treats early inference as spoiling later scenes.

**Fix:** reward correct inference. The later revelation is not “objects differ” but what the seventh route means and who controls it.

## 2 — Combat-forward table
Players attempt to identify Venn as the boss and solve the finale by neutralizing him.

**Break found:** killing/capturing Venn could appear to erase the choice.

**Fix:** Venn’s severance case and method exist independently in evidence and allies. His HP cannot be the campaign’s philosophical lock.

## 3 — Cautious / low-initiative table
Players wait for Aletheia to assign exact tasks in the Sanctuary.

**Fix:** local simultaneous crises demand visible choices. Aru, Tamar, Nesra, Hadu, and Kesh each ask for concrete help without knowing the temporal problem.

## 4 — Accelerated / convention-equivalent table
Two sessions compress poorly if every local route is investigated separately.

**Fix:** group the six human routes into three playable clusters and permit prior strong Carrier outcomes to establish one cluster automatically. Preserve Venn, Aletheia disclosure, collapse, choice, and Verification at all costs.

## 5 — Lateral-thinking table
Players propose abandoning both Aletheia and Venn and building an independent temporal council.

**Result:** valid. This becomes the Distributed Authority route if they can define credible constraints and preserve the local crisis response.

## 6 — Bad-luck table
Repeated failures during fracture stabilization and rescue threaten to consume the finale.

**Fix:** failures cost time, equipment, Exposure, separation, or secondary objectives. They never remove the final governance decision.

## 7 — High-Exposure table
Players use future scanning, translation, medical and structural technology openly.

**Result:** powerful and viable. Local lives may be easier to save, but the Sanctuary accumulates anomalous traces and the seventh route becomes harder to govern cleanly. Covenant actors prioritize anomalous evidence.

## 8 — Covenant-sympathetic / anti-Aletheia table
Players accept Venn’s diagnosis immediately.

**Break found:** a simple “yes, sever it” could flatten Session Two.

**Fix:** severance still requires protecting the six human routes during collapse and accepting the loss of future temporal recoverability. The question becomes whether they still accept the cost when it is concrete.

## 9 — Prehistoric-setting-blind table
Players initially expect huts, primitive social organization, and helpless locals.

**Fix:** opening read-aloud immediately shows specialized labor, communal work, routes, food processing, stoneworking, argument, childcare, and expertise. At least one clean solution comes only from listening to Kesh.

## 10 — Full-path continuity: strong M01–M05 outcomes
Strong Carriers give the team conceptual shortcuts and stable temporal references. They recognize object-vs-Carrier, redundancy, deliberate incompleteness, observation-vs-interpretation, and living-network logic quickly.

**Balance finding:** do not turn these rewards into auto-success. They save time and reduce ambiguity, freeing veteran characters to tackle more simultaneous rescue/governance objectives.

## 11 — Full-path continuity: mixed/failed M01–M05 outcomes
Weak prior Carriers create unstable references, misleading concentrated records, missing comparison points, and harder route reconstruction.

**Result:** finale remains playable. Failure creates work rather than locking endings. High prior Exposure can paradoxically provide additional anomalous evidence, preserving the campaign rule that Exposure is not merely a punishment meter.

## 12 — Level 6 advancement / power-curve simulation
The Level 6 team is tested against the Level 1 M01 experience.

**Target:** veteran competence should appear as breadth, reliability, tactical flexibility, VAM/BAR reconfiguration choices, stronger Skills/Abilities/HP, and the ability to manage simultaneous objectives.

**Finding:** M06 should not raise target numbers merely to defeat advancement. Veteran power is allowed to solve individual problems quickly. Difficulty comes from deciding which of several solvable problems receives attention and what Exposure/authority cost accompanies the strongest tools.

## Ending legitimacy test

### Keep seventh route
Narratively legitimate. Best when players believe recoverability outweighs concentration-of-authority risk. Requires explicit trust/governance aftermath.

### Sever seventh route
Narratively legitimate. Best when players prioritize historical autonomy and accept loss of temporal safety net.

### Distribute authority
Narratively legitimate but not a secret golden ending. It creates slower, contested intervention and potential paralysis.

### Damaged/uncontrolled
Narratively legitimate failure/complication state and strong sequel hook, but should not be framed as equivalent to a deliberately governed choice.

## Historical-respect test
The fictional Sanctuary does not explain Göbekli Tepe and Vectors do not invent monumental building, agriculture, religion, social organization, stoneworking, or long-distance networks. Local competence is structurally necessary to success.

## GM coherence test
The GM can summarize the finale as:

> Seven apparently redundant prehistoric objects reveal six human ways continuity survives. Venn wants those six preserved and temporal intervention severed. Aletheia admits it used the campaign partly to investigate that seventh route. During a physical and temporal collapse, the Vectors save what they can and decide who, if anyone, should retain the power to repair the past.

If the GM cannot retain that paragraph, later revisions should simplify rather than add lore.

## Promotion recommendation

Do not label the Adventure Path release-ready yet. No frozen Protocol 7 v0.187 mechanic changed.
