# Decision: Historical Anchor

M02 will be anchored to **Alaska, January–February 1925, during the Nome diphtheria emergency and serum relay**, but the Vectors will operate adjacent to the famous relay rather than replacing its real participants.

This is a strong Protocol 7 insertion because the real event already embodies the campaign theme: survival depended on a chain of independent human beings, transportation links, telegraph/radio communication, medical judgment, territorial authority, and local improvisation. The Vectors do not secretly make the famous serum run happen. The historical serum relay remains the achievement of the people and dog teams who actually performed it.

The fictional continuity break concerns a communications practice that emerges during the emergency and is lost afterward.

## Historical guardrails for drafting

- Nome experienced a diphtheria outbreak in winter 1925.
- Nome was seasonally isolated by sea ice; rapid outside aid depended on overland routes and communications.
- Antitoxin was moved to Nenana and then carried toward Nome by a relay of dog teams.
- Contemporary reporting documents rapid messages among Nome, territorial officials, and other communities, and the Nome radio station was ordered to continue service day and night during the crisis.
- The Washington-Alaska communications system and related radio/telegraph infrastructure provide a historically plausible communications environment.
- Real mushers, physicians, officials, and communities may be mentioned as historical context, but the adventure must not appropriate their achievements by making fictional Vectors the hidden reason the serum reaches Nome.

# Run This Tonight — Story Spine

Nome is fighting a diphtheria outbreak. A life-saving antitoxin relay is already moving through Alaska. The Vectors arrive because Aletheia has detected a secondary discontinuity: during the communications strain surrounding the emergency, a fictional operator named **Evelyn “Evie” Mercer** develops a simple procedure for relaying incomplete or damaged emergency traffic across multiple stations without allowing one bad link to destroy the message.

At first the mission appears to be about a failing remote relay station and a critical message needed by the serum operation. The team can repair the station, deliver the message, and save the immediate situation.

That is the false finish line.

The true Carrier is Mercer's **relay discipline**: a repeat-back and fragment-confirmation procedure that lets several independent stations reconstruct urgent traffic even when static, weather, damaged lines, exhausted operators, or partial reception corrupt individual transmissions.

Baseline history uses the method once and forgets it. Mercer writes the procedure on loose operating sheets; the emergency ends; equipment and personnel change; the sheets disappear into ordinary station waste. Similar ideas emerge later, but the practical field procedure misses the moment when it could have spread through Alaska's communications culture.

The Vectors must make the method independent of Mercer without turning themselves into the inventors.

Meanwhile the Covenant offers a troubling alternative: ensure the serum-related traffic succeeds, save the people in danger now, and let the communications procedure vanish. They argue that every redundancy standard Aletheia asks the Vectors to preserve is another ancestor of the integrated emergency-command systems Aletheia eventually controls.

# Historical Fiction Boundary

Evie Mercer, the specific relay procedure, the local station crisis, and the Covenant/Vector events are fictional.

The Nome epidemic, real serum relay, regional communications infrastructure, geographic isolation, and broad communications conditions are historical.

The adventure must visibly respect that boundary in GM notes. Protocol 7 should inhabit the gaps around history, not steal credit from historical people.

# The True Carrier

**Carrier:** A field-tested emergency relay procedure based on four behaviors:

- break urgent traffic into numbered fragments;
- require receiving stations to repeat fragment identifiers rather than merely acknowledge receipt;
- allow a downstream station to request only missing fragments from any station that copied the traffic;
- keep an independent handwritten reconstruction log so no single operator or transmission contains the only complete message.

This is intentionally fictionalized procedure, not a claim about invention of a specific real-world communications protocol.

## Carrier states

**Fragile:** Mercer and/or one station possesses the procedure; the immediate emergency message may succeed but the practice remains personal and disposable.

**Established:** A second independent station copies and uses the procedure, or it enters an ordinary station operating notebook/circular in a way that can survive Mercer.

**Durable:** Multiple independent stations use it and at least one training/administrative route preserves it beyond the emergency.

# False Objective and Revelation

**Surface objective:** Restore the winter relay station and make sure a critical emergency message gets through.

Players should initially feel that getting the message through is obvious mission success.

Then Aletheia's verification remains incomplete.

Mercer, irritated and exhausted, can make the distinction human rather than abstract:

> “You fixed tonight. I am trying to fix the next night when nobody here is left to remember how.”

The adventure then pivots from rescue/repair to continuity.

# The Immediate Human Problem

The fictional station serves several scattered settlements and relay points. Severe cold and wind have damaged an aerial/line connection and made generator reliability uncertain. Operators are exhausted. Emergency traffic is arriving faster than it can be cleanly copied. A medical/logistical message relevant to the broader response arrives in fragments.

The players must choose between technically elegant solutions and historically plausible ones. Future equipment can solve almost everything quickly but risks Exposure.

The first hour should feel physical: cold, darkness, ice, machinery, dogs, wet wool, battery fumes, numb hands, and voices reduced to bursts of code through static.

# Primary Cast

## Evelyn “Evie” Mercer — relay operator

**First impression:** Sharp, sleep-deprived, territorial about her station, pencil behind one ear.  
**Wants now:** Get emergency traffic through without trusting any single failing link.  
**Deeper want:** Make other operators stop treating improvisation as something that lives only in an experienced person's head.  
**Trusts:** Competence, concise communication, anyone willing to copy exactly before offering improvements.  
**Resists:** Heroics that cannot be repeated by ordinary operators after the heroes leave.  
**Voice:** Short sentences. Repeats critical numbers. Never says “probably” about a copied message.

## Jonah Pike — line repairman / dog driver

**Role:** Physical route into the communications network.  
**Wants:** Keep the line/station alive and get home with all fingers attached.  
**Use:** Turns repair into movement and survival rather than a stationary technical check.  
**Secret pressure:** He knows a safer alternate route exists but using it will delay a separate local medical request.

## Lt. Thomas Vale — communications administrator

**Role:** Institutional route for making the Carrier Established or Durable.  
**Wants:** Restore normal operations and eliminate improvised practices after the emergency.  
**Obstacle:** He regards Mercer's procedure as useful emergency clutter unless shown it works at more than one station.  
**Use:** Gives players a non-villain reason why valuable practices disappear.

## Silas Venn or Covenant successor

Use Venn only if M01 continuity permits. Otherwise introduce **Dr. Ruth Calder**, a Covenant analyst operating under a plausible period cover.

**Offer:** Covenant will help guarantee the immediate medical traffic and even assist with the damaged station if the Vectors agree not to institutionalize Mercer's procedure.

**Argument:** “Nobody in Nome needs your future communications architecture. They need the message tonight.”

The argument must hurt because it is partly true.

# M01 Callback

M01 consequences appear without determining M02 success.

If Quill's Carrier was **Durable**, Pike has access to a recently circulated engineering note whose mundane bracing comparison gives the team a historically ordinary option for stabilizing a damaged mast/support. This saves time or Exposure.

If **Established**, the idea exists but must be found or recognized.

If **Fragile/failed**, no such convenience exists. The Vectors can still solve the problem using local improvisation, skill, gear, or future capability.

If M01 produced **high Exposure**, Covenant has a better description of the team's methods and anticipates one piece of future equipment or one habitual tactic.

# Seven-Stroke Mystery Advancement

M02 must establish that the seven-stroke phenomenon is **not bound to one artifact**.

After the Carrier becomes Established or Durable, two independently created copies of Mercer's relay procedure—physically separated and produced by different hands—develop the same seven-stroke annotation in corresponding but not identical positions.

The mark must not simply materialize in front of the players like magic. Verification after extraction reveals that archived copies now contain the annotations.

Aletheia can establish:

- the two documents had independent custody histories;
- no known person handled both after the point at which the Carrier split;
- the marks are materially consistent with the writing media of each document;
- this makes “one saboteur marking objects” a weaker theory, not an impossible one.

Do not explain more in M02.

# Revelation Ladder

1. The station is failing and a critical message is fragmented.
2. Mercer has a practical method for reconstructing incomplete traffic.
3. Getting this one message through solves the immediate crisis but does not close Aletheia's discontinuity.
4. Mercer's procedure—not the message—is the Carrier.
5. The procedure only becomes resilient when independent stations can use it without Mercer.
6. Covenant is willing to help save today's lives if the Vectors allow the method to disappear.
7. More transmission routes improve Carrier strength while increasing Exposure and historical footprint.
8. Verification finds the seven-stroke mark on two independent descendants of the procedure.

# Clue Architecture

## Conclusion: Mercer's method matters beyond one message

Door A: Mercer reconstructs two unrelated messages with the same procedure.  
Door B: Pike notes that operators normally improvise differently, making Mercer's repeatable structure unusual.  
Door C: A downstream station successfully requests only a missing numbered fragment.  
Fail-forward: Aletheia confirms that successful delivery of the immediate message did not close the discontinuity.

## Conclusion: independence is required

Door A: Mercer explicitly worries the method dies with her notebook.  
Door B: Vale orders temporary emergency notes discarded when normal service resumes.  
Door C: A second operator independently reconstructs traffic from Mercer's written instructions.  
Fail-forward: Covenant offers to preserve Mercer and one private copy while preventing distribution.

## Conclusion: Covenant's concern is systemic

Door A: Calder/Venn points to the difference between saving one medical message and standardizing a network.  
Door B: Recovered Covenant notes map prior Carrier repairs to later coordination infrastructure.  
Door C: Aletheia openly acknowledges that resilient communications are part of the future systems it eventually coordinates.

# Scene Architecture

## Scene 1 — Briefing: The Message Is Already Moving

Aletheia makes clear that the famous emergency is not theirs to create. Historical people are already responding. The discontinuity appears inside the communications traffic surrounding the response.

**Purpose:** Prevent “we secretly caused the serum run” framing and establish humility toward history.

## Scene 2 — Insertion: Cold Enough to Break Things

The team arrives into an immediate physical problem: damaged line/mast, stranded repair party, generator trouble, or a combination scaled to table time.

**Purpose:** Establish Alaska as an active antagonist and let non-social/non-investigative characters contribute immediately.

## Scene 3 — The Fragmented Message

At the station, traffic is chaotic. Mercer demonstrates her improvised fragment system while the Vectors help recover a critical message.

**Purpose:** Teach the Carrier through action before naming it.

## Scene 4 — Repair the Relay

The team must restore or bypass the damaged link. Offer local repair, dangerous physical route, social requisition, and future-tech solutions.

**Purpose:** Give a clear short-term objective and an apparent victory.

## Scene 5 — False Finish Line

The critical message gets through. The local crisis eases. Aletheia does not verify mission completion.

**Purpose:** Deliver the campaign's second Carrier lesson: saving the message is not saving the method.

## Scene 6 — Make It Reproducible

The Vectors identify ways to get Mercer's procedure into independent hands. They may demonstrate it across stations, persuade Vale, create an ordinary operating circular, or invent another historically plausible route.

**Purpose:** Strategic player agency.

## Scene 7 — Covenant's Offer

Opposition offers practical help with the emergency in exchange for containing the procedure.

**Purpose:** Force the players to separate humanitarian urgency from long-term continuity.

## Scene 8 — Whiteout Interference

Weather and Covenant action converge. A physical copy, courier, repair route, or station becomes endangered. Combat is possible but never mandatory.

**Purpose:** Make the team's chosen transmission route expensive.

## Scene 9 — Independent Copy

At least one other operator must be able to use the procedure without Mercer for Established status. Durable requires broader propagation.

**Purpose:** Pay off the mission concept at the table instead of only in debrief.

## Scene 10 — Extraction and Verification

The Vectors leave knowing the Carrier state and Exposure cost. Verification shows historical consequences and the paired seven-stroke marks.

**Purpose:** Advance the Adventure Path mystery.

# Eight Simulation Targets

Before release, run:

1. Investigative table: Does the false finish line still land if players identify the procedure early?
2. Combat-forward table: Can Covenant remain ideological opposition rather than target practice?
3. Cautious table: Are there obvious actionable choices at every stage?
4. Four-hour convention table: Can Scenes 2–4 and 8 be compressed without losing the Carrier revelation?
5. Lateral-thinking table: Can players establish the procedure through a route not listed here?
6. Bad-luck table: Can failed repair/communication rolls increase cold, time, equipment damage, or Exposure without blocking clues?
7. High-Exposure table: What happens if future communications gear trivializes the immediate message problem?
8. Covenant-sympathetic table: Does accepting the Covenant's humanitarian compromise create a playable outcome rather than a GM veto?

# Expected Simulation Stress Points

- Players may correctly identify “the protocol is the Carrier” before Scene 5. Do not suppress that insight. If they are right, reward it and move the conflict to propagation versus Exposure.
- Technical communications detail can become homework. The fictional procedure must be understandable in under one minute and playable without Morse/radio expertise.
- The 1925 serum run is famous enough that players may focus on Togo/Balto/Seppala. Keep those events in the historical background unless a researched cameo is genuinely useful; never make celebrity recognition necessary.
- Cold should create choices, not repetitive saving throws.
- Covenant's offer must actually help people now or it has no moral force.
- A Durable solution should require more historical footprint than an Established solution.

# GM Research Notes for Full Draft
