# P7-AP01-M01 — The Ash Ledger r002
## Playtest Simulation and Coherence Report

**Purpose:** Diagnose GM comprehension, narrative coherence, clue robustness, pacing, player agency, and failure handling without changing frozen Protocol 7 mechanics.

## Simulation profiles

### 1. Experienced investigative table
Players aggressively theorized about the Apocryphon, split leads efficiently, and identified Quill quickly.

**r001 failure:** Once Quill was found, the session briefly felt “solved.” The distinction between Quill and the Carrier existed in the text but was not staged as a dramatic revelation.

**r002 correction:** Quill is deliberately framed as a false finish line. Her own motivation, Venn’s compromise, and the carbon evidence all reinforce that reproducible knowledge—not personal survival—is the objective.

### 2. Combat-forward table
Players treated Covenant agents as obvious enemies and attempted to remove them early.

**r001 failure:** The Covenant could collapse into generic encounter opposition before its ideological function became clear.

**r002 correction:** Venn appears first with an actionable compromise rather than a threat. Covenant escalation now tracks Carrier strength, giving their behavior a visible strategic logic.

### 3. Cautious / low-initiative table
Players waited for the GM to tell them which lead mattered most.

**r001 failure:** The investigation had adequate clues but insufficient signposting about what each lead was for.

**r002 correction:** Each scene now states its narrative purpose, and the clue architecture distinguishes conclusions from optional corroboration.

### 4. Convention-speed table
The GM had approximately 10–15 minutes to prepare and needed to finish in four hours.

**r001 failure:** Useful facts were present but scattered. The GM had to synthesize the adventure before being able to explain it.

**r002 correction:** A Run This Tonight section explains the story, dramatic question, five beats, and central reversal in roughly two minutes of reading. A pacing table identifies safe compression points.

### 5. Clever lateral-thinking table
Players proposed creating an independent engineering record through a route not named in the adventure.

**r001 failure:** Named NPC routes could be interpreted as required solutions.

**r002 correction:** Player-created transmission routes are explicitly valid when historically plausible, independent, reproducible, and assigned an Exposure footprint.

### 6. Bad-luck table
Several investigation rolls failed.

**r001 failure:** The text said not to gate conclusions behind one roll, but did not consistently tell the GM what failure should change instead.

**r002 correction:** Failure costs time, access, safety, witness footprint, or Fire Clock pressure. Essential information remains multiply accessible.

## Main diagnosis

The core premise was sound. The largest problem was not plot contradiction; it was **presentation coherence**. The r001 manuscript often functioned as a reference manual for a GM who already understood the scenario. A hurried GM needed the manuscript itself to teach the adventure.

The revision therefore focuses on:

- a two-minute whole-story briefing;
- a clearer before/history/after causal chain;
- an explicit revelation ladder;
- stronger scene purposes and transitions;
- Quill as a false finish line;
- Covenant actions tied to Carrier state;
- multiple independent clue doors;
- explicit fail-forward techniques;
- support for player-created solutions;
- pacing guidance for four-hour tables;
- clearer Adventure Path thematic continuity.

## Frozen-mechanics audit

No frozen v0.187 core mechanic was intentionally changed. Carrier states and the four mission outcome categories remain intact. Observed mechanical concerns should continue to be logged separately rather than silently solved inside an Adventure revision.

## Recommended live-play questions

1. Can a GM explain the mission after reading only “Run This Tonight”?
2. Do players discover that Quill is not the Carrier through play rather than exposition?
3. Does Venn’s compromise tempt at least one player before the Covenant becomes physically hostile?
4. Can the group propose a valid transmission route not explicitly listed?
5. Does every failed investigation roll change circumstances rather than stop progress?
6. Does the table reach Verification with enough time to discuss what changed?
7. Does the seven-stroke mark feel like a campaign hook rather than random sequel bait?
