# Protocol 7 v0.188 — Encounter & Lethality Simulation Wave 3 r001

**Status:** SIMULATION REPORT

## Scope

Wave 3 tests the advancement package at encounter scale rather than as isolated character snapshots. Focus areas:
- Level 1 / 3 / 4 / 6 lethality;
- STR-based HP progression versus flat +3 HP per level;
- Mastery specialist versus broad veteran;
- ordinary enemies versus peer-grade Mastery elites;
- fixed 3 AP / 1 Reaction action economy;
- six-die Skill Mastery as the routine veteran ceiling;
- minimal blank-pattern incidence only (11/22/33 and 111/222/333), with no effects assigned yet.

## Blank-pattern observation

Representative five-die pool: d10,d8,d8,d6,d6.
Representative six-die Mastery pool: d10,d10,d8,d8,d6,d6.

Simulation results:
- five-die average Protocol score: about **17.39**;
- six-die average Protocol score: about **22.33**;
- DC 20 success: about **38.4%** at five dice versus **64.3%** at six dice;
- one specific blank pair (11 or 22 or 33) appears in about **14%** of five-die rolls and **17.6%** of six-die rolls;
- one specific blank triple (111/222/333) appears in about **2.0%** of five-die rolls and **3.2%** of six-die rolls.

This supports the narrowed rule. Individual pairs are noticeable but not constant; individual triples remain uncommon even with Mastery. Effects should therefore stay modest for pairs and more consequential for triples.

## HP models

### Model B — fixed 18 HP + half current STR maximum each new level
Typical unchanged STR progressions:
- d4: L1 18 → L3 22 → L4 24 → L6 28
- d6: 18 → 24 → 27 → 33
- d8: 18 → 26 → 30 → 38

Higher STR is visibly rewarded, but the gap remains understandable and gradual.

### Model C — fixed 18 HP +3 each level
All characters: L1 18 → L3 24 → L4 27 → L6 33.

### Encounter finding

Model C is cleaner, but Model B creates more character texture and a stronger reason for STR to matter outside direct physical rolls. The main exploit risk is advancing STR before an HP award; the previously proposed sequencing rule prevents same-level retroactive benefit:

1. gain HP using current STR;
2. gain Skill Ranks / BAR / authorization;
3. resolve Core Growth/Ability step.

**Wave 3 preference: retain Model B for one more test wave.** No promotion yet.

## Lethality by campaign tier

### Level 1
18 HP still produces a vulnerable starting Vector. Ordinary firearm or heavy melee threats remain meaningful. Survival depends more on cover, positioning, and avoiding concentrated fire than on raw HP.

**Finding:** good. Level 1 should feel dangerous.

### Level 3
HP in the low-to-mid 20s plus 21 BAR produces noticeable resilience and more tactical flexibility without trivializing ordinary threats.

**Finding:** good. This is the first clearly veteran-feeling midpoint without changing action economy.

### Level 4
24 BAR plus first Skill Mastery creates the largest qualitative jump. A Mastery specialist can routinely dominate their chosen problem when the Mastery condition is satisfied, but the 7–9 BAR commitment prevents them from simultaneously loading every broad utility option.

**Finding:** this is the intended watershed and should remain.

### Level 6
30 BAR allows sophisticated configurations but does not remove opportunity cost once Elite/Continuance VAMs cost 8–12 BAR. One capstone plus one Mastery system can consume roughly two-thirds of capacity, leaving meaningful decisions for the remainder.

**Finding:** 30 BAR is not automatically too high. The high-end catalog cost structure is doing the balancing work.

## Specialist versus broad veteran

### Mastery specialist
- much stronger at one prepared/conditional domain;
- routinely accesses six dice;
- spends roughly one-third of L4 BAR on Mastery;
- more likely to keep cheap L1 utility VAMs around the expensive core system.

### Broad veteran
- remains at ordinary pool ceiling more often;
- can load several 3–6 BAR systems instead of one 8–9 BAR Mastery system;
- performs better across simultaneous mixed problems;
- swaps less often because more solutions are already active.

**Finding:** both builds remain viable. Mastery is not mandatory in mixed-scene play.

## Peer-grade opposition

An elite enemy with one Mastered Skill and an appropriate access mechanism feels meaningfully different from ordinary opposition without requiring inflated HP.

Recommended enemy grammar for later NPC design:
- ordinary historical threat: no Mastery;
- trained/elite historical threat: strong Skills / equipment / tactics, usually no Mastery;
- Covenant/peer-grade enhanced operative: one Mastered Skill plus bounded access;
- exceptional recurring antagonist: possibly two Mastered Skills, but should not routinely activate both on the same roll.

Six dice should tell the table, mechanically, 'this opponent operates at Vector-grade capability.'

## Action economy

3 AP and 1 Reaction continue to hold. The advancement package gains power through better options, BAR, Skill growth, and Mastery rather than extra turns.

**Do not increase baseline AP or Reaction count for v0.188.**

## Feature-die stacking guardrail

Wave 2's concern remains. For the next rules draft, test the following wording:

> A roll may normally include only one bonus die granted by a Feature/VAM. The Skill Mastery Die is not a Feature bonus die and may be added separately when its access condition is met. Gear, Advantage/Disadvantage, and other explicitly distinct sources follow their own rules, but no effect may create routine seven-die escalation unless it expressly overrides this limit.

This needs a final source taxonomy before canonization, but the principle is strongly supported.

## Blank-pattern effects remain unresolved

No effects are assigned in this wave. Only frequency and table visibility were tested.

Observed design target:
- pairs: small, optional rider;
- triples: notable event;
- if irrelevant, ignore;
- no searching beyond obvious 1/2/3 matches.

## Wave 3 verdict

**GREEN-AMBER.**

The core advancement structure is now strong enough to continue toward a candidate rules package:
- BAR 16/18/21/24/27/30 survives encounter testing;
- Level 4 Mastery works as the veteran threshold;
- six dice work as the practical routine ceiling;
- broad and Mastery builds both remain viable;
- fixed action economy remains healthy;
- STR-based HP is still viable but needs one more optimization/lethality comparison before promotion;
- Feature-die stacking requires explicit guardrail language;
- blank-pattern effects/name remain intentionally unresolved.
