# P7-AP01 — Campaign Advancement and Leveling Plan r001

**Status:** Adventure Path balance authority for first-pass campaign progression
**Core baseline:** Protocol 7 v0.187
**Change policy:** This document does not alter frozen advancement mechanics. It defines campaign pacing targets and audit questions.

## Design intent

The Continuance Files should take a starting Protocol 7 Vector from approximately **Level 1 through Level 6** across the six-mission campaign. That overall growth span should feel roughly analogous to a typical d20 campaign moving from about **Level 1 through Level 12**: characters begin capable but vulnerable, acquire clearly noticeable new options and resilience, and finish as highly competent specialists with broad campaign history behind them.

This is a pacing analogy, not a rules conversion. Protocol 7 should not imitate d20 class features or numerical scaling directly.

## Provisional campaign level schedule

- **M01 — The Ash Ledger:** begin Level 1; award advancement sufficient to reach Level 2 by the post-mission/debrief window.
- **M02 — The Winter Relay:** Level 2 play; advance to Level 3 after completion.
- **M03 — The Paper Harbor:** Level 3 play; advance to Level 4 after completion.
- **M04 — The Quiet Machine:** Level 4 play; advance to Level 5 after completion.
- **M05 — The Last Water Map:** Level 5 play; advance to Level 6 after completion.
- **M06 — Seven Copies:** Level 6 finale. Do not require a Level 7 destination for this Adventure Path unless the core campaign framework later explicitly supports one.

This creates five meaningful advancement moments across six missions.

## What advancement must feel like

Each post-mission advancement should produce at least one change a player can notice immediately at the next table session. Across the campaign, advancement should increase several dimensions rather than only raw numbers:

- reliability in signature Skills;
- survivability where the frozen HP/Ability rules permit it;
- access to stronger or more flexible VAM/loadout choices;
- tactical breadth within the existing 3-AP economy;
- useful gear and mission resources;
- broader competence outside the character's initial specialty;
- increased ability to manage Exposure and complex multi-objective missions through player choices rather than automatic bonuses.

## d20 pacing analogy

The desired emotional progression is approximately:

- **Protocol 7 Level 1 ≈ early d20 tier (levels 1–2):** competent but outcomes can turn quickly; limited specialization depth.
- **Protocol 7 Level 2 ≈ d20 levels 3–4:** core identity is clear; first strong advancement payoff.
- **Protocol 7 Level 3 ≈ d20 levels 5–6:** characters feel established; multiple approaches become practical.
- **Protocol 7 Level 4 ≈ d20 levels 7–8:** strong specialists with enough breadth to carry difficult scenes.
- **Protocol 7 Level 5 ≈ d20 levels 9–10:** veteran operatives; dangerous environments and multi-part objectives should challenge decisions more than basic competence.
- **Protocol 7 Level 6 ≈ d20 levels 11–12:** campaign-capstone competence; the finale should test synthesis, tradeoffs, and consequences rather than merely increasing target numbers.

The equivalence is deliberately approximate. The audit should judge *felt capability and encounter complexity*, not attempt mathematical CR conversion.

## Balance rules for adventure design

1. Do not scale every check upward merely because the party leveled.
2. Later missions should become harder primarily through simultaneous objectives, clocks, environmental pressure, conflicting responsibilities, opposition competence, and consequences.
3. Advancement should allow earlier obstacles to feel easier when revisited or echoed. That contrast is desirable evidence of growth.
4. No mission should require one specific advancement choice.
5. A generalist and a specialist should both remain useful.
6. Advancement should not erase Exposure as a meaningful concern.
7. Do not use higher level to justify excessive combat frequency. The post-first-pass Style & Balance Audit evaluates action/investigation/exploration balance separately.

## Adventure Path advancement awards

For the first complete pass, use **milestone-level pacing at mission boundaries** for campaign testing even if the core advancement system ultimately expresses the award as Skill Ranks, Progress Ranks, Ability advancement, VAM access, HP change, or another canonical combination.

The purpose of the first-pass campaign test is to ensure M02 is tested by Level 2 characters, M03 by Level 3 characters, and so forth. The exact canonical award package at each boundary must be derived from the v0.187 advancement rules and audited before final release.

Do not invent a new advancement currency inside the Adventure Path.

## Required post-first-pass advancement audit

After M06 first-pass completion, the Style & Balance Audit must include an **Advancement & Power-Curve Audit** covering:

- whether each level-up is noticeable and satisfying;
- whether any level produces a disproportionate power spike or dead level;
- Skill-die progression and Progress Rank pacing;
- Ability-die advancement frequency and impact;
- HP growth and survivability across Levels 1–6;
- VAM/BAR loadout breadth and whether higher levels create meaningful new decisions;
- attack/defense reliability and combat duration at each level;
- whether environmental hazards remain meaningful without becoming arbitrary damage taxes;
- whether later investigative and social challenges reward growth without simply inflating difficulties;
- whether generalist versus specialist characters remain viable;
- whether advancement choices become obvious/mandatory;
- whether M06 feels like a Level 6 capstone rather than a Level 1 scenario with larger numbers;
- whether the Level 1→6 experience broadly delivers the desired d20 Level 1→12 sense of campaign growth.

## Simulation requirement

The completed Adventure Path should be replayed in simulation using a persistent representative party whose sheets are advanced after every mission according to the current canonical advancement rules. At minimum compare:

- a balanced four-Vector party;
- a heavily combat-specialized party;
- an investigation/social-heavy party;
- an intentionally broad/generalist party.

Track success rates, damage/HP pressure, AP use, VAM reconfiguration, Skill contribution, Exposure, spotlight distribution, and how often advancement materially changes available decisions.

## Current implementation note

Until the advancement audit is complete, M05 should be designed assuming a **Level 5 party**, and M06 assuming **Level 6**. Do not alter frozen v0.187 advancement mechanics merely to fit these missions. If simulations show a mismatch, record it as a balance finding for explicit core review.
