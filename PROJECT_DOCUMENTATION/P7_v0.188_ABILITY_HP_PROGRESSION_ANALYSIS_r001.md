# Protocol 7 v0.188 — Ability & HP Progression Analysis r001

**Status:** REVISION ANALYSIS — recommendation for simulation
**Baseline authority:** Protocol_7_v0.187_CANONICAL_RULES_SOURCE.json

## Reconciled rules history

The current v0.187 canonical source explicitly defines starting HP as **CON die maximum × 3**: d4 12, d6 18, d8 24, d10 30, d12 36. It does not define level-up HP or Ability advancement.

Earlier Protocol 7 design work also established a different remembered model: **all starting characters begin at 18 HP**, then gain HP at advancement equal to half the maximum value of the Strength die (d4 +2, d6 +3, d8 +4, d10 +5, d12 +6).

These models conflict. They must not be combined accidentally.

## Design test

### Model A — CON-derived HP, no automatic HP gain
Keep v0.187 HP exactly as written. Ability increases may indirectly increase HP if CON advances.

**Strengths**
- Minimal rule change.
- Keeps HP tied to one Ability.

**Problems**
- CON becomes disproportionately valuable if raising it grants +6 HP per die step.
- A character who advances other Abilities may gain no durability over a six-level campaign.
- Level 1 characters range from 12 to 24 HP under the starting array, which conflicts with the earlier fixed-18 design intent.
- Core Growth becomes harder to balance because CON is both a broad Ability die and a large HP increase.

### Model B — fixed 18 starting HP + Strength-based incremental HP
Restore the earlier Protocol 7 model: everyone begins with 18 HP. At each level after 1, gain HP equal to half the maximum of the current Strength die.

If Strength never changes, total Level 6 HP would be:
- STR d4: 28 HP
- STR d6: 33 HP
- STR d8: 38 HP
- STR d10: 43 HP
- STR d12: 48 HP

**Strengths**
- Every level visibly increases durability.
- Starting characters have equal baseline survivability.
- Strength has a persistent advancement role without making it the sole determinant of starting HP.
- Easy to explain and track.

**Problems**
- This explicitly reopens and replaces the v0.187 HP formula.
- Strength becomes a long-term HP optimization choice.
- Five automatic HP increases may be too steep unless damage/encounter pressure scales through scope rather than raw damage.

### Model C — fixed 18 starting HP + fixed HP per Protocol 7 level
Example test: +3 HP per level after 1, producing 33 HP at Level 6 for everyone before other effects.

**Strengths**
- Cleanest balance curve.
- Does not privilege CON or STR.
- Very easy for homebrew translation.

**Problems**
- Least connected to Ability dice.
- Less character differentiation.
- Moves away from previously discussed Protocol 7 advancement identity.

## Recommendation for simulation

**Test Model B first:** fixed 18 starting HP and half-Strength-die HP at each Protocol 7 level after 1.

Reason: it is previously established Protocol 7 design material, creates visible advancement every level, and avoids the v0.187 problem where a CON Ability step simultaneously improves many pools and grants +6 HP.

Do not promote it without persistent combat simulations.

## Ability/Core Growth recommendation

Do **not** grant an automatic Ability die increase at Levels 3 and 5.

Instead test **two Core Growth choices across the campaign**, at Levels 3 and 5, where the player may step one Ability die up by one die size subject to a d12 ceiling and any final array/cap rule established by the Engine.

This is a choice, not a mandatory predetermined Ability. It allows specialization or shoring up a weakness.

### Why only two
Ability dice participate in many Skill pools. A single Ability step can improve numerous actions simultaneously. Two steps over a six-level Protocol 7 campaign should therefore be treated as major growth events.

### HP separation
Under the recommended Model B test, increasing CON does **not** recalculate maximum HP. HP progression is separate. Increasing STR can affect future level-up HP gains because the current Strength die is consulted when the next level is gained; it does not retroactively recalculate prior gains.

This separation prevents one CON step from producing an immediate +6 HP spike.

## Working Level package for simulation

- **Level 1:** starting Ability array d8,d8,d6,d6,d4,d4; 20 Skill Ranks; 18 HP; 16 BAR.
- **Level 2:** +4 Skill Ranks; HP gain = half current STR maximum; Advanced I VAM authorization.
- **Level 3:** +4 Skill Ranks; HP gain; +2 BAR; one Ability die may step up; High-Integration I authorization.
- **Level 4:** +4 Skill Ranks; HP gain; Advanced II authorization.
- **Level 5:** +4 Skill Ranks; HP gain; +2 BAR; one Ability die may step up; Elite authorization.
- **Level 6:** +4 Skill Ranks; HP gain; +2 BAR; Continuance/Capstone authorization.

AP remains 3. Normal Reactions remain 1.

## Required balance questions

1. Does STR become a mandatory optimization because it controls recurring HP gain?
2. Does fixed 18 HP make low-CON characters too durable or high-CON characters insufficiently distinct?
3. Are two Ability steps enough to feel like major milestones?
4. Does stepping STR before later levels create an interesting choice or an obvious exploit?
5. Is Level 6 HP in the 28–48 theoretical range too wide?
6. Does the campaign's damage model still threaten Level 5–6 characters without damage inflation?
7. Would a fixed +3 HP per level outperform the Strength-based model in actual play?
