# Protocol 7 v0.188 — HP Philosophy & Vitality Ritual Rulebook Brief r001

**Status:** RULEBOOK DESIGN AUTHORITY — HP candidate requires final lethality resimulation

## Purpose

The rulebook must not present Hit Points as an unexplained number. It should teach what HP represents, why the starting ritual uses all six Ability dice, how the mathematics protect character viability, and how physical dice communicate fictional capability at the table.

The explanation should be thoughtful and transparent without becoming a probability lecture. A player should finish the section understanding both the rule and the design logic behind it.

## What Hit Points represent

Hit Points are not a literal measurement of blood, flesh, Constitution, or the number of wounds a person can physically absorb.

HP represents the entire reserve that keeps a character functioning when events turn dangerous: physical toughness, conditioning, reflexes, awareness, judgment, experience, preparation, confidence, pain tolerance, luck, timing, social instinct, the ability to recognize danger before it fully develops, and other difficult-to-isolate qualities.

That is why Protocol 7 should not derive HP from Constitution alone. Constitution matters to survival, but it does not explain survival by itself. Strength may matter. Dexterity may matter. Intelligence may matter because a person anticipates danger. Wisdom may matter because a person notices it. Charisma may matter because people hesitate, listen, cooperate, or react differently to that person. The exact mixture is intentionally not predetermined.

The starting HP ritual lets the Ability dice help reveal which part of this particular character's total capability happens to dominate that intangible survival reserve.

## The Vitality Ritual — candidate rule

After assigning the starting Ability array **d8, d8, d6, d6, d4, d4**, roll each Ability die once, separately, in order:

1. STR
2. DEX
3. CON
4. INT
5. WIS
6. CHA

Write each face beside its Ability. Do **not** score these as a normal Protocol Dice pool. For this ritual, read the face showing on each individual die.

Circle the highest face rolled.

**Starting HP = 18 + the highest face.**

Because a normal result requires at least one Ability die to show 4 or higher, ordinary starting HP ranges from **22 to 26** under the current starting array.

Record which Ability produced the highest result. This may be called the character's **Vitality Origin** during development. It is primarily interpretive: it invites the player to consider why that quality helps this Vector survive, but does not force a personality, class, or mechanical path.

If multiple Abilities tie for the highest face, the player may choose which tied Ability to record as the Vitality Origin.

## All-Blanks exception — development rule

If **all six Ability dice show 1, 2, or 3**, the ordinary calculation does not apply.

This is the development result informally nicknamed **Mr. Irrelevant**: the apparently worst possible Vitality ritual becomes a rare exceptional character-creation result rather than creating the weakest character at the table.

**Candidate reward: Starting HP = 28.**

This preserves the ritual's drama and ensures a run of terrible-looking dice never produces a crippled long-term character.

The development nickname is not automatically player-facing terminology. Final setting-neutral language should be chosen during rulebook editing.

## Why the math works

The current starting Ability array contains two d8s, two d6s, and two d4s. The player therefore gets six independent chances to establish the Vitality result.

The ritual does not average the Abilities and does not designate one Ability as the mandatory HP Ability. Instead, it asks a simpler question: **what is the strongest survival signal the dice reveal about this character today?**

If the roll is not the special all-blanks result, at least one die must show 4 or better. With the base 18, that makes **22 HP the absolute ordinary minimum**. The d8 starting ceiling makes **26 HP the ordinary maximum**. The controlled 22–26 band preserves meaningful randomness without allowing one unlucky roll to leave a character permanently far behind the rest of the party.

The all-blanks result is rare enough to feel special but common enough that groups may eventually see it. It deliberately rewards the extreme low-tail event rather than punishing it.

## The dice as physical information

The rulebook should explain a broader Protocol Dice principle near this section and reinforce it throughout play examples.

An Ability die is not merely a modifier translated into another format. It is a physical representation of one-sixth of the character's core capability. When several dice are assembled into a pool, the table can literally see the ingredients of the attempted action.

A GM building an opponent's pool is therefore communicating information before the dice are rolled. Players can see the size and number of dice. In the fiction, their characters are seeing the opponent move, hearing the confidence in a voice, noticing posture, speed, strength, awareness, attractiveness/presence, intelligence, hesitation, training, equipment, and other observable signals.

The dice become a tabletop vocabulary for those observations.

A handful of four, five, or six dice should become increasingly readable with experience. The table learns that the pool itself tells a story: what capabilities are contributing, how broad the action is, whether a Feature is helping, and eventually whether Skill Mastery is present.

This is a major reason Protocol Dice uses Ability dice rather than reducing every interaction to one highly variable linear die plus a collection of arithmetic modifiers. The pool distributes uncertainty across several visible components. Stronger characters improve by changing the composition and quality of the dice rather than merely adding larger static numbers to the same random 1–20 swing.

The rulebook may explain this comparison conceptually but should avoid attacking or belittling d20 games. The purpose is to help the reader understand Protocol Dice on its own terms.

## Cognitive teaching goal

Before asking a player to roll, the rules should help answer:

- What am I trying to accomplish?
- Which Skill represents my learned competence?
- Which Ability dice describe the parts of me that matter to this attempt?
- Is a Feature, Assist, gear, or circumstance contributing the ordinary bonus die?
- If I am a veteran, does Skill Mastery apply?
- Looking at the pool in my hand, what does this tell me about the action before I roll it?

The GM should be taught the same process for NPCs. Building the pool is part of describing the situation, not merely an invisible calculation behind the screen.

## Rulebook presentation requirements

The final rulebook treatment should include:

- a concise definition of HP;
- a short philosophical explanation of why HP is not Constitution;
- the six-roll Vitality ritual in an easy visual sequence;
- one complete worked example showing all six Ability dice and the resulting HP;
- a small transparent math callout explaining the 22–26 ordinary range;
- a clearly separated all-blanks exception;
- a short example of interpreting a surprising Vitality Origin, such as INT or CHA;
- a nearby explanation of dice pools as visible fictional information;
- GM guidance explaining how opponent dice communicate capability;
- no excessive probability tables in the player-facing text.

## Worked example seed

Example starting array:
- STR d6 → rolls 4
- DEX d8 → rolls 3
- CON d6 → rolls 5
- INT d8 → rolls 7
- WIS d4 → rolls 2
- CHA d4 → rolls 1

Highest face: **7 from INT**.

Starting HP: **18 + 7 = 25 HP**.

Interpretation: the game is not claiming intelligence literally creates bodily durability. The result suggests that this Vector's survival reserve may be especially connected to anticipation, planning, rapid understanding, recognizing patterns, or simply being difficult to catch unprepared. The player may embrace that interpretation, reinterpret it, or leave it unexplained.

## Advancement consequence

The previous v0.188 candidate of fixed 18 starting HP plus STR-derived HP gains is reopened. Once HP is explicitly defined as an amalgam of the whole character, making all later HP growth depend only on STR risks contradicting the new philosophy.

Before promotion, simulate predictable level-up HP growth independent of a single Ability against the new 22–28 starting range. Candidate fixed growth should be compared with any dice-based advancement ritual that cannot create harmful party variance.

Do not synchronize public v0.188 HP rules until this final advancement-HP test is complete.
