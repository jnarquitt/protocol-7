# Protocol 7 v0.188 — VAM Rebuild Design Bible r001

**Status:** OPEN REVISION DESIGN AUTHORITY — simulation required

## Core inheritance

Protocol 7 interprets Protocol Dice Engine Feature Access as VAM authorization and Engine Capacity as BAR. Protocol Dice Engine Skill Mastery is accessed in Protocol 7 primarily through expensive Mastery-capable VAMs.

## Revised BAR progression for testing

The earlier 16/16/18/18/20/22 curve is superseded for v0.188 simulation by:

| Level | BAR | Increase |
|---|---:|---:|
| 1 | 16 | — |
| 2 | 18 | +2 |
| 3 | 21 | +3 |
| 4 | 24 | +3 |
| 5 | 27 | +3 |
| 6 | 30 | +3 |

Rationale: Level 4 introduces Skill Mastery. A typical 7–9 BAR Mastery VAM should be a major commitment without consuming so much capacity that players avoid the new system. At Level 4, an 8-BAR Mastery VAM consumes one third of 24 BAR while leaving the Vector with the same 16 BAR they had at Level 1 for other systems.

BAR growth does not itself improve a roll. Its power remains constrained by authorization, catalog choice, and active loadout.

## Level 4 watershed — Skill Mastery

At Level 4, a Vector gains the opportunity to designate a Skill as **Mastered**, subject to the final advancement rule.

A Mastered Skill gains a Skill Mastery Die equal to its current Skill die. The die is dormant unless an appropriate Mastery-capable VAM is loaded and its condition applies.

The resulting pool may reach six dice. Six dice are intended to signal veteran/peer-grade capability, not become the new universal pool size.

Important Opposition operatives may also possess Skill Mastery and suitable technology/training. Six dice on the opposing side should communicate peer-level danger without simply inflating enemy HP.

## Mastery VAM cost envelope

Mastery access should be expensive in BAR because it changes the practical dice-pool ceiling.

Working test bands:
- **6 BAR:** narrow Mastery access with a meaningful condition.
- **7–9 BAR:** broadly useful Skill-specific Mastery access; expected standard veteran cost.
- **10–12 BAR:** unusually permissive, multi-context, team-linked, or otherwise exceptional Mastery access.

Mastery VAMs should not routinely grant Mastery in multiple unrelated Skills. Such effects belong at the extreme high end, if they exist at all.

## Authorization bands

### Level 1 — Standard
Foundational identity. Broad utility, period adaptation, basic combat, movement, investigation, cover, medical, and technical options. Mostly 1–4 BAR with occasional higher-cost narrow systems.

### Level 2 — Advanced I
Sharper specialization and combinations. First strong action/reaction or information-control options. 2–6 BAR common.

### Level 3 — High Integration
Capabilities designed around 21 BAR: team coordination, multi-step compression, stronger rescue/technical/recon systems, bounded temporal support. 3–7 BAR common.

### Level 4 — Advanced II / Mastery Access
First Mastery-capable VAMs plus veteran non-Mastery options. Mastery should be available across all six functional families, not only combat. 6–9 BAR common for Mastery systems.

### Level 5 — Elite
Broader or more flexible Mastery access, scene-shaping capabilities, advanced team support, and high-cost specialist systems. Old low-BAR VAMs remain valuable as configuration glue.

### Level 6 — Continuance
Rare capstone authorization. New strategic choices, temporal/command coordination, and exceptional Mastery interactions. No automatic mission-solving and no routine seventh die.

## Category coverage requirement

Every Level 4 Mastery unlock must offer at least one compelling path for each family:
- Combat
- Defense & Mobility
- Recon & Investigation
- Social & Cover
- Technical & Medical
- Command & Temporal

Illustrative concepts for later catalog writing:
- ballistic/predictive combat mastery;
- rescue/evasion/athletic mastery;
- forensic/pattern mastery;
- behavioral/linguistic/cover mastery;
- surgical/engineering/system mastery;
- command/temporal-analysis mastery.

These are design slots, not final VAM names.

## BAR cost grammar

Use the full BAR range when useful rather than preserving even-only costs:
- 1–2 BAR: narrow evergreen utility;
- 3–4 BAR: signature specialist capability;
- 5–6 BAR: strong scene capability or bounded timing/reaction change;
- 7–9 BAR: loadout-defining veteran capability, including common Mastery access;
- 10–12 BAR: exceptional tightly bounded system.

Authorization and BAR cost remain independent dimensions.

## Catalog target

Rebuild toward approximately 72–90 VAMs for simulation. Preserve strong existing concepts, merge duplicates, rewrite ambiguous mission-procedure modules, and add enough Level 2–6 material that every broad Vector fantasy receives exciting unlocks.

## Path philosophy

Existing preconfigured Paths remain recommendations, not classes. Each should have:
- a legal Level 1 16-BAR starting package;
- suggested Level 2–3 additions;
- at least two plausible Level 4 Mastery directions;
- divergent Level 5–6 upgrade choices.

Custom cross-category loadouts remain fully legal within authorization and BAR limits.

## Six-die ceiling principle

The rebuild should treat six dice as the exceptional practical ceiling for routine veteran play. Levels 5–6 improve access, flexibility, coordination, or effects around Mastery rather than starting a seven/eight-die escalation.

Any exception must be explicit, rare, and separately simulation-tested.

## Required simulations

Before promotion:
- compare 16/18/21/24/27/30 BAR against the earlier conservative curve;
- measure average loaded VAM count and unused BAR at every level;
- measure field-swap frequency;
- test 6, 8, 10, and 12-BAR Mastery VAM opportunity costs;
- measure six-die roll frequency;
- test Mastery in all six functional families;
- test peer enemies with Mastery;
- test Dice Tricks/pattern incidence with six dice;
- ensure low-tier VAMs remain present in Level 6 optimized loadouts;
- ensure Mastery does not become mandatory for every character;
- ensure non-Mastery Level 4–6 builds remain viable.
