# P7-AP01-M03 — The Paper Harbor
## Playtest Simulation Report r002

**Historical frame:** Marseille, November 1940  
**Rules baseline:** Protocol 7 v0.187  
**Mechanical changes:** none

## Historical fit

Marseille is retained as the insertion because it naturally makes documentation, identity, consular bureaucracy, aid networks, and escape logistics immediate human stakes. Contemporary and archival sources confirm that refugees in Marseille depended on layered documents, visas, sponsors, consular access, and relief organizations; the adventure uses fictional local NPCs so real rescue workers retain their historical achievements.

## Simulation profiles

### Investigative / experienced table
**Break:** Players immediately suspected that the master list was not the true Carrier and tried to skip the human-scale opening.
**Correction:** The first two scenes require them to solve one person's identity problem using several incomplete records before the Harbor Book appears. They experience the method before naming it.

### Combat-forward table
**Break:** Players treated the search/raid as the adventure's obvious climax and prepared a direct confrontation.
**Correction:** Opposition first performs an unmistakably humane act, such as destroying an arrest copy or helping a family escape. Any later violence is tied to control of a specific record or route.

### Cautious / low-initiative table
**Break:** Players assumed local clerks would simply tell them which records mattered.
**Correction:** Marchand knows the immediate bureaucracy but not the temporal importance. Simultaneous document, witness, and institutional routes force the players to choose what to pursue.

### Four-hour convention table
**Break:** Visa-era texture can easily consume the session.
**Correction:** The runnable spine is explicitly five beats: prove one person → find master list → realize danger → divide proof → verify mark. Secondary historical texture is optional.

### Lateral-thinking table
**Break:** Players proposed destroying the Harbor Book immediately and building an entirely different verification network.
**Correction:** This is valid if the new solution remains period-plausible, compartmented, independent, and derived from local practice rather than imported future systems.

### Bad-luck table
**Break:** Failed document and influence checks risked erasing access to essential records.
**Correction:** Failure costs time, trust, document condition, or Exposure. Essential conclusions always remain available through at least three routes.

### High-Exposure table
**Break:** Players proposed scanning, encrypting, or digitally preserving the registry with future technology.
**Correction:** Future technology works, but the Exposure cost is severe and creates exactly the centralized information problem the mission is about.

### Anti-Aletheia / Covenant-sympathetic table
**Break:** Players agreed that centralized registries were dangerous and wanted to side completely with Venn.
**Correction:** The scenario now treats that as a legitimate moral position. The difficult question is whether they preserve any reproducible cross-verification method at all.

## Main diagnosis

The prior r001 version had strong thematic ingredients but was too generic. It often described roles such as “local expert,” “necessary witness,” and “cautious official” rather than giving the GM a memorable story with named people and a clear revelation sequence.

r002 corrects this by centering a specific false objective—the Harbor Book—and a specific dramatic reversal: the complete registry is both valuable and dangerous. The true Carrier is not possession of more information but a method of distributing partial verification so no one person or office holds the whole vulnerable network.

## Campaign function

M01: an object is not the Carrier.  
M02: the Carrier gains strength through independent reproduction.  
M03: independence can require deliberate incompleteness and compartmentation.

The seven-stroke mystery advances by placing a verifiable mark on a record that predates the Vectors' local insertion. This should make the players ask not only who is marking Carriers, but how the mark can already be present before they act.
