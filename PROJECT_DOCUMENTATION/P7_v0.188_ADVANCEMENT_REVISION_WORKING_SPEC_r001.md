# Protocol 7 v0.188 — Advancement Revision Working Specification r001

**Status:** OPEN CORE REVISION — simulation required before promotion
**Previous frozen baseline:** v0.187
**Reason for revision:** The Continuance Files Level 1–6 power-curve audit found that v0.187 does not define a complete authoritative level-award package. Adventure Path second-pass mechanical balancing is paused until advancement is explicit.

## Design inheritance

Protocol 7 is an implementation of the Protocol Dice Engine. It should interpret the Engine's generic advancement axes rather than invent an unrelated progression subsystem.

Protocol Dice Engine progression axis → Protocol 7 interpretation:

- Skill Growth → Skill Ranks / Skill dice
- Core Growth → Ability die advancement and resulting derived-stat changes
- Capacity Growth → BAR capacity
- Feature Access → VAM authorization/catalog tiers
- Horizontal Advancement → contacts, permissions, period resources, institutional authority, safehouses, favors, relationships, mission-specific access

## Campaign compression target

The Continuance Files uses six Protocol 7 Levels as a compressed campaign-growth span intended to feel roughly like levels 1–12 in a traditional d20 campaign.

**Working model:** one Protocol 7 Level represents approximately **two underlying Protocol Dice Engine Advancement Awards**.

This means every mission-boundary level should feel substantial without requiring inflated target numbers or additional AP.

## Working Level 1–6 progression

This table is a simulation target, not yet promoted canon.

| Level | Skill growth at boundary | BAR | VAM authorization | Major growth |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Starting 20 Skill Ranks | 16 | Standard catalog | Starting Ability array |
| 2 | +4 Skill Ranks | 16 | Advanced catalog I | Horizontal reward / mission access |
| 3 | +4 Skill Ranks | 18 | High-integration catalog I | Core Growth opportunity |
| 4 | +4 Skill Ranks | 18 | Advanced catalog II | Horizontal reward / mission access |
| 5 | +4 Skill Ranks | 20 | Elite catalog | Core Growth opportunity |
| 6 | +4 Skill Ranks | 22 | Continuance-class/capstone catalog | Capstone authorization |

Across Levels 1→6 this produces **+20 Skill Ranks** after character creation and BAR growth from **16→22**.

## Skill Growth

Each Protocol 7 level boundary currently represents two Engine Advancement Awards and therefore grants **+4 Skill Ranks** under the inherited +2 ranks per Award model.

Simulation must specifically test Skill breakpoint behavior. A level-up must not feel dead merely because allocated ranks do not immediately cross a die breakpoint.

Possible mitigation should be tested before changing the award amount: allow players to split ranks, pair Skill growth with a VAM authorization/horizontal reward, and ensure every level contains at least one immediately visible advancement axis.

## BAR Capacity

BAR is Protocol 7's implementation of Engine Capacity.

**Working progression:**

- Level 1: 16 BAR
- Level 2: 16 BAR
- Level 3: 18 BAR
- Level 4: 18 BAR
- Level 5: 20 BAR
- Level 6: 22 BAR

BAR growth is deliberately milestone-based rather than automatic every level. The goal is to let veteran Vectors sustain richer VAM combinations while preserving loadout pressure and the importance of inexpensive VAMs.

The existing rule that loading into unused BAR is free and swapping/replacing already-loaded VAMs costs 1 AP remains unchanged during this test.

## VAM Authorization Tiers

VAM progression is Protocol 7's implementation of Engine Feature Access.

The catalog should be reorganized for testing into authorization bands. Exact VAM assignments and names require a separate catalog audit.

### Standard — Level 1
Broad, useful low-to-moderate BAR VAMs. These establish character identity and must remain useful throughout the campaign.

### Advanced I — Level 2
More specialized capabilities and stronger combinations. New options should be exciting without simply invalidating Standard VAMs.

### High-Integration I — Level 3
First VAMs designed around the increased 18-BAR capacity. May include capabilities that substantially change approach to a scene.

### Advanced II — Level 4
Veteran breadth, team coordination, unusual technical/sensory/social/tactical options.

### Elite — Level 5
Powerful high-cost modules and combinations suitable for veteran Vectors. Some may cost roughly 5–7 BAR so choosing one materially shapes the loadout.

### Continuance / Capstone — Level 6
Rare high-authorization capabilities appropriate to a campaign finale. These should create new decisions, not automatic solutions.

### Tier design law
A higher-tier VAM is not automatically a numerically superior replacement for a lower-tier VAM. Cheap Standard modules should remain attractive at Level 6 because BAR remains finite.

## Core Growth / Ability Dice

Ability advancement remains the least-settled part of this revision.

The working test places **Core Growth opportunities at Levels 3 and 5**, but the exact rule is NOT YET CANONICAL. Candidate implementations must be simulated against:

- Ability-array ceilings;
- HP impact;
- attack/defense reliability;
- specialist/generalist balance;
- whether CON becomes mandatory because of HP;
- whether two Ability steps across six levels create too much or too little intrinsic growth.

Do not update player-facing products with an Ability advancement rule until this is resolved.

## HP

Starting HP remains the current Protocol 7 rule during revision testing. Any HP change caused by Ability advancement must follow the canonical derived-stat rule unless that rule itself is explicitly reopened.

The earlier remembered advancement concept of gaining HP based on half the Strength die must be reconciled against the current v0.187 canonical source before promotion; it must not be silently mixed with the current derived-stat model.

## Fixed values

During this revision, the following remain fixed unless simulation demonstrates a specific failure:

- 3 AP per turn;
- 1 Reaction per round;
- core Protocol Dice scoring;
- free loading into unused BAR;
- 1 AP for a field swap of already-loaded VAMs.

The intent is for veteran Vectors to get more value from the same action economy, not simply take more turns.

## Horizontal rewards

Each mission may also grant setting-facing advancement such as contacts, permissions, safehouses, institutional authority, favors, relationships, era-specific resources, or improved access to Aletheia support.

These rewards should be recorded because they are part of campaign power even when they do not change dice.

## Required simulations before v0.188 promotion

Run persistent Level 1→6 advancement simulations for at least:

- balanced four-Vector party;
- combat-heavy specialists;
- investigation/social specialists;
- broad generalists;
- VAM-heavy optimization party;
- deliberately inefficient advancement choices.

Track at every level:

- Skill dice and breakpoint crossings;
- average/check success profile;
- attack/defense reliability;
- HP and damage pressure;
- BAR utilization and frequency of swaps;
- number and tier of VAMs actually loaded;
- whether old VAMs remain useful;
- AP and Reaction use;
- Exposure consequences;
- spotlight distribution;
- whether each level-up contains an immediately noticeable benefit;
- whether Level 6 feels roughly like the intended campaign-growth endpoint.

## Promotion gate

Do not resume the Adventure Path mechanical second pass until:

1. Ability/Core Growth is resolved;
2. VAM catalog has been assigned provisional authorization tiers;
3. Level 1→6 persistent simulations pass;
4. Protocol Dice Engine framework and Protocol 7 implementation remain cleanly separated;
5. rulebook, interactive guide, app, canonical source, and project locks have a synchronized v0.188 change plan.

Until then, v0.187 remains the public playtest baseline and this document remains a working revision specification.
