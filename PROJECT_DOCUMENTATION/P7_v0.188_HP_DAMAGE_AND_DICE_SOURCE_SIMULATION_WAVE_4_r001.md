# Protocol 7 v0.188 — HP, Damage & Dice-Source Simulation Wave 4 r001

**Status:** SIMULATION REPORT — candidate decisions

## Scope

Wave 4 stress-tests the remaining advancement pressure points before freezing a provisional Level 1–6 sequence:

- fixed 18 starting HP + half current STR maximum at advancement;
- flat +3 HP alternative;
- STR optimization and advancement sequencing;
- Level 1/3/4/6 damage pressure;
- Skill Mastery combined with other bonus-die sources;
- a formal dice-source taxonomy;
- the Protocol Dice principle that bonuses are expressed as dice or bounded mechanical effects, never flat numeric modifiers;
- Matched Blanks treated as a secondary effect layer, with no flat numeric bonuses.

## HP candidate decision

### Candidate rule

**All Level 1 Vectors begin with 18 HP.**

Whenever a Vector gains a new Protocol 7 Level, increase maximum HP by half the maximum value of the Vector's current STR die:

- STR d4 → +2 HP
- STR d6 → +3 HP
- STR d8 → +4 HP
- STR d10 → +5 HP
- STR d12 → +6 HP

### Advancement order

At a level boundary:

1. increase HP using the STR die the character had before advancement;
2. allocate Skill Rank growth;
3. increase BAR / authorization as specified by the new Level;
4. resolve Core Growth / Ability step if that Level grants one;
5. choose newly available VAMs/loadout changes.

An STR increase therefore affects **future** HP awards, never the HP award from the same Level and never retroactively recalculates earlier HP.

## HP stress cases

### Low-STR specialist
A Vector who leaves STR at d4 throughout the campaign reaches:
18 → 20 → 22 → 24 → 26 → 28 HP.

This character remains meaningfully fragile at high Level, but advancement still improves durability every time.

### Typical STR
A d6 STR Vector reaches:
18 → 21 → 24 → 27 → 30 → 33 HP.

This closely matches the flat +3 model while preserving Ability identity.

### High STR
An unchanged d8 STR Vector reaches:
18 → 22 → 26 → 30 → 34 → 38 HP.

This is materially tougher without doubling the low-STR character's HP.

### STR optimization
A character who spends a Level 3 Core Growth step increasing STR gains no same-level HP. The larger HP increment begins at Level 4. A second STR step at Level 5 likewise affects only the Level 6 award.

This makes STR investment valuable but delayed. It competes with improving an Ability that may influence a larger number of the character's core Skills.

**Finding:** the sequencing rule removes the strongest obvious exploit. STR-based HP is preferred over flat +3 for the v0.188 candidate package.

## Damage pressure

The advancement model should not require raw enemy damage inflation simply because HP rises.

High-level danger should increasingly come from:
- peer-grade six-die attacks;
- better enemy positioning and coordinated fire;
- conditions and denied options;
- mission clocks and simultaneous objectives;
- Exposure/Carrier pressure;
- environmental consequences;
- enemies with Mastery access;
- threats that force Vectors to protect other people rather than merely preserve their own HP.

Ordinary historical weapons remain dangerous, especially under concentrated fire. Elite enemies become more dangerous through competence and options, not giant damage modifiers.

## Protocol Dice bonus principle

**No flat numeric bonuses.**

A mechanical bonus in Protocol Dice should normally be expressed as:
- an added die;
- an upgraded/replaced die when a rule explicitly permits it;
- Advantage/Disadvantage;
- an AP/timing effect;
- a Reaction effect;
- movement/positioning;
- a condition;
- permission to attempt or accomplish a bounded thing;
- an additional damage/effect die;
- another explicitly defined dice-facing mechanic.

Avoid +1, +2, +3, etc. as bonuses to rolls, damage, defenses, or derived outcomes.

## Dice-source taxonomy

To prevent veteran pools from expanding uncontrollably, every die entering a roll should have a source class.

### Core Pool
The ordinary Skill die plus the Ability dice called for by the action. This is the character's underlying roll.

### Feature Die
A bonus die granted by a Talent, VAM, power, technique, or analogous Engine Feature.

**Candidate stacking rule:** only **one Feature Die** may be added to a roll unless a rule explicitly states otherwise.

If multiple loaded VAMs could grant a Feature Die, choose one. Their non-die effects may still apply if otherwise legal.

### Mastery Die
The Skill Mastery Die is its own source class. It may be added in addition to one Feature Die when an appropriate Mastery access mechanism permits it.

This is the intentional path to a routine veteran six-die pool.

### Advantage / Disadvantage
Advantage/Disadvantage remains its own resolution layer and is not automatically a Feature Die. Its exact interaction with pool size must remain consistent with the canonical Engine rule.

### Gear / Situation
Equipment or circumstances may grant dice only where the relevant rules explicitly do so. A gear die must not become a loophole for routine seven-plus-die pools. If a gear benefit duplicates a Feature Die, use the stronger/most appropriate single source rather than stacking by default.

### Assist / Team Support
Team support may grant a die where a rule allows it, but the final v0.188 wording should classify whether that die occupies the Feature slot or a separate bounded Assist slot. Until that is resolved, **do not allow Assist + Feature + Mastery to create routine seven-die pools.**

## Practical ceiling

The intended ordinary progression remains:

- normal capable roll: core pool;
- enhanced roll: core + one Feature Die;
- veteran Mastery roll: core + one Feature Die + Mastery Die, producing the intended six-die practical ceiling in common optimized cases.

Seven or more dice require an explicit exceptional rule and should be rare enough to be recognized as an override, not normal optimization.

## Matched Blanks interaction

Matched Blanks remain secondary to success resolution and ordinary criticals.

Wave 4 adopts the following design restriction:

**Matched Blank effects may not use flat numeric bonuses.**

Impact effects should add or manipulate damage/effect dice. Tempo should manipulate AP, timing, movement, or Reactions. Control should manipulate positioning, conditions, information, precision, or bounded consequences.

The frequency ladder—not arbitrary numeric bonuses—determines effect strength:
- pairs: frequent, small effect;
- triples: uncommon, strong action effect;
- four matching blanks: rare, major effect;
- five: extraordinary;
- six: legendary Mastery-only possibility.

## Wave 4 verdict

**GREEN-AMBER.**

Candidate decisions now strong enough to carry forward:
- restore fixed 18 starting HP for the revision candidate;
- use half current STR maximum for each later Level's HP award;
- calculate HP before same-level Ability advancement;
- retain 3 AP / 1 Reaction;
- retain BAR 16/18/21/24/27/30;
- Level 4 introduces Skill Mastery;
- no flat numeric bonuses;
- classify bonus dice by source;
- one Feature Die per roll by default;
- Mastery Die is the explicit additional veteran source;
- routine seven-plus-die stacking remains prohibited unless explicitly overridden.

Still unresolved before freeze:
- exact Assist classification;
- whether Level 6 grants a second Mastered Skill automatically, optionally, or through another advancement choice;
- final Matched Blanks name/effect table;
- exact Ability ceiling/cap wording inherited from the Engine;
- final individual VAM rule text and loadout simulation.
