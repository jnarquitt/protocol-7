## Protocol Dice
Faces 1–3 are blanks. Faces 4+ contribute face value. A normal Skill pool is the Skill die plus the Skill's three listed Ability dice. Repeated Ability references are intentional. Additional dice require named sources: one primary relevant Gear die, an explicit VAM die, legal Mastery access, or Advantage. There is no generic bonus-die slot.

Difficulties: Easy under pressure 8; Moderate 12; Hard 16; Very Hard 20; Extreme 24. Winning margins: 1–3 Narrow; 4–7 Solid; 8–11 Powerful; 12+ Overwhelming.

## Abilities and Skills
Level 1 Ability array: d8, d8, d6, d6, d4, d4. Ability progression: d4 → d6 → d8 → d10 → d12. Levels 3 and 5 increase one Ability one step. Starting characters receive 20 Skill Ranks; each later Level grants 4. Untrained d4; ranks 1–2 d6; 3–5 d8; 6–9 d10; 10+ d12.

**Archery — DEX / DEX / WIS.** Bows and crossbows across historical eras. Precision Weapons remains for specialized precision ranged systems such as sniper/marksman weapons.

**Riding — DEX / WIS / CON.** Riding, controlling and maneuvering ordinary trained mounts under pressure. Routine travel does not require a roll. Mounted attacks still use the weapon Skill; Riding resolves control/positioning.

Skill Mastery: choose one Mastered Skill at Level 4 and a second at Level 6. Mastery does not automatically add a die. A Mastery die requires the Skill to be Mastered and explicit Mastery access, normally through an appropriate VAM. When legal, it equals the Skill die. Only one Mastery die applies.

## Common action adjudication
- **Intimidate:** normally Influence. Deception may apply when a convincing lie is primary.
- **Read motives / sense intent:** normally Perception; Investigation when interpreting accumulated evidence.
- **Tail / shadow:** Stealth for remaining unseen; Tradecraft for surveillance patterns, cover identity, handoffs or countersurveillance.
- **Assist:** plausible material help gives the acting character Advantage. One effective Assist is the ordinary limit. Risky help may require a helper check first.
- **Cover:** cover that can physically interfere with an attack normally gives Advantage to the appropriate active defense. Armor remains Gear. Complete obstruction can make an attack impossible.
- **Ready / reload:** meaningful drawing, stowing or reloading under action pressure normally costs 1 AP unless Gear/VAM says otherwise.
- **Generic resistance:** Fortitude for poison/disease/exhaustion/bodily endurance; Will for coercive psychological pressure; Reflex for sudden bodily hazards; Survival when sustained environmental preparation/fieldcraft is central.

## Chase procedure — CURRENT PLAYTEST
Use a chase only when relative position matters and ordinary movement alone would not resolve the scene. The GM establishes a simple **Lead** from -3 to +3 from the pursuer's perspective: 0 = close/contested; positive = pursuer closing; negative = quarry opening distance. Each chase exchange, both sides describe their approach and roll the most appropriate Skill: Athletics for foot pursuit, Driving for vehicles, Riding for mounts, or another Skill when the fiction clearly changes the contest. Compare totals. The winner shifts Lead one step in their favor; a Powerful or Overwhelming winning margin shifts it two steps. At +3 the pursuer catches/intercepts the quarry; at -3 the quarry escapes or creates enough separation to end the chase. Hazards, clever routes, superior vehicles/mounts, VAMs or Gear may provide Advantage/Disadvantage or change which Skill applies. Do not roll merely for covering ordinary distance.

This procedure uses existing opposed-roll math. It adds scene structure rather than a new die subsystem.

## Defending another Vector / interception
A Vector who can plausibly intervene may use their Reaction to become the defender against an incoming attack aimed at an adjacent/reachable ally. Resolve the attack against the intervening Vector's appropriate active defense instead. Repositioning required to intercept must be fictionally possible; the Reaction does not grant unlimited movement. A Vector cannot intercept if they have no Reaction available. This is protection, not a free counterattack.

## Surprise and ambush
Surprise is handled through fictional position and Advantage/Disadvantage rather than a separate surprise round. If attackers achieve an ambush, the GM may grant Advantage to the opening relevant action and/or impose Disadvantage on a defender whose ability to respond is genuinely compromised. A completely unaware target may be unable to make an active defense when the fiction provides no plausible response. Once the ambush is revealed, normal action economy begins. Do not grant recurring bonuses merely because one side started hidden.

## Multiple attackers
Each attacker resolves their own action normally. A defender does not suffer an automatic stacking penalty simply because several enemies exist. The tactical cost emerges through positioning, cover, conditions, spent Reaction, and the fiction. If coordinated attackers materially create a superior position, use the ordinary Advantage/Disadvantage rules rather than adding unnamed dice or arithmetic modifiers.

## Environmental hazards and falling
Use the existing Difficulty ladder when a hazard calls for a check. Reflex handles sudden avoidable hazards; Fortitude handles bodily endurance; Survival handles sustained exposure/fieldcraft. Failure consequences should follow the fiction and severity rather than a universal damage table when no attack is involved.

**Falls:** if a fall is meaningfully dangerous, Reflex or Acrobatics may reduce/avoid the consequence when a plausible response exists. The GM sets severity from distance/surface. Minor falls may cause a condition or small fixed harm; major falls may cause substantial HP loss, conditions, incapacitation or death when fiction makes survival implausible. This remains intentionally judgment-based during v0.188 playtest rather than introducing a distance-to-damage formula before table evidence exists.

**Drowning / suffocation:** ordinary brief breath-holding requires no roll. Under meaningful pressure, Fortitude handles continued bodily endurance; Athletics may govern active swimming/escape. Repeated failure escalates from Disoriented/exhausted fiction to HP harm and eventual incapacitation. Exact round-count timing remains a playtest question.

**Fire, electricity, explosions and severe exposure:** use Reflex to avoid immediate danger where possible, Fortitude to endure unavoidable bodily stress, and appropriate Gear/cover when physically relevant. The GM assigns harm based on threat severity and winning/failing margin when an opposed or difficulty roll is used. No hidden universal elemental-resistance subsystem exists.

## Advantage / Disadvantage
Advantage adds one d6 Advantage die. Disadvantage removes the smallest Ability die in the pool; if tied, player chooses. Each caps at one effective instance and opposing reasons cancel one-for-one. Gear is not Advantage.

## Action Economy and Reaction
A Vector normally begins a turn with 3 AP. A normal Attack costs 1 AP and may be repeated. Movement is 30 feet per AP: 1 AP = 30 ft, 2 AP = 60 ft, 3 AP = 90 ft. There is no universal Run action or ×2 Run multiplier.

A Vector normally has one Reaction between turns. Reaction is not a fourth free AP. Unspent AP remains banked for Reaction until the next turn. Spend banked AP first; if none remains, at most 1 AP may be borrowed from the next turn. Borrowed AP reduces the next turn. Unspent banked AP expires when the next turn begins.

## Vitality and Hit Points — CURRENT EXPERIMENT
After assigning the six starting Ability dice, roll STR, DEX, CON, INT, WIS and CHA once each. The app may roll all six simultaneously; this is identical to rolling six physical dice together. Faces 1–3 are blanks. If at least one Ability shows 4+, starting HP = 18 + highest face. If several Abilities tie for highest qualifying face, choose one tied Ability as Vitality Origin. If all six dice are blank, starting HP = 28 and the Vector has Perfect Equilibrium.

**Vitality Spark — once/session:** after a Skill roll whose three listed Ability dice include the Vitality Origin Ability, if the pool contains a natural 1, reroll one die showing a natural 1 and keep the new result.

**Perfect Equilibrium — once/session:** after any Skill roll containing a natural 1, reroll one die showing a natural 1 and keep the new result.

All-six-blanks on the Level-1 array occurs 81/4096 ≈ 1.98%. A natural 1 contributes zero; expected improvement from rerolling it is d4 1.00, d6 2.50, d8 3.75, d10 4.90, d12 5.83. Each Level after 1 adds 4 maximum HP.

## Level Progression — CURRENT EXPERIMENT
Level 1: BAR 16; 20 Skill Ranks; Vitality Ritual. Level 2: BAR 18; +4 HP; +4 Skill Ranks; choose one Edge. Level 3: BAR 21; +4 HP; +4 Skill Ranks; increase one Ability one step. Level 4: BAR 24; +4 HP; +4 Skill Ranks; Master one Skill. Level 5: BAR 27; +4 HP; +4 Skill Ranks; increase one Ability one step. Level 6: BAR 30; +4 HP; +4 Skill Ranks; Master a second Skill.

Level 2 Edges: Driven — once/session reroll one die after rolling and keep new result. Inspired — once/session gain Advantage before a roll. Prepared — once/session declare reasonable ordinary preparation. Connected — once/session establish a plausible useful contact/introduction/social access. Durable — persistent +5 maximum HP.

## BAR and VAMs
BAR is loaded capacity, not fuel. Loading into unused BAR costs 0 AP. During active play, replacing/unloading already-loaded VAMs as a field reconfiguration costs 1 AP for the whole swap. A VAM cannot exceed BAR or authorization. VAM dice remain named VAM dice; VAMs that negate Disadvantage remove a named reason rather than granting Advantage; Mastery VAMs provide access rather than mastering a Skill.

## Gear, Armor, Combat and Damage
One primary relevant Gear die is the ordinary limit unless a rule permits another source. Armor adds its listed Gear die to an appropriate active defense pool when it can physically protect against the threat; it normally does not then subtract damage again. Combat uses opposed attack and active-defense pools. If attacker total does not exceed defense, no normal damage. If attacker wins, normal damage equals winning margin. v0.188 currently has no universal critical subsystem; Matched Blanks remain disabled.

## Conditions
Current registry: Prone, Grappled, Suppressed, Stunned, Disoriented, Restrained. Conditions use named rules language rather than hidden penalty dice. Stunned removes Reaction and increases AP cost of the first action next turn by 1.

## Character Creation
Choose Custom or preconfigured Vector; enter name; assign d8/d8/d6/d6/d4/d4; perform Vitality Ritual; spend 20 Skill Ranks; load legal starting VAMs up to 16 BAR; select Gear; review pools and derived values. Preconfigured Vectors are shortcuts, not exceptions.

## Historical Operations
Carrier states: Fragile → Established → Durable. Carrier strength and Exposure are competing objectives. Outcomes remain Clean Success, Exposed Success, Contained Failure, Exposed Failure. Mission procedure: Briefing → Insertion → Establish Cover → Investigate → Identify Leverage → Intervene → Establish Carrier → Contain Exposure → Extract → Historical Verification.

## Testing and app requirements
Simulation passes use named personas and the persistent common/uncommon/rare action checklist. They record action attempted, rules mapping, PLAY support, missing Skill/Gear/VAM/procedure/UI, contradiction and proposed fix. New actions found during actual play are appended rather than forgotten.

**Build the Vector elsewhere. Play the Vector on PLAY.** PLAY is the sole normal action-resolution and rolling surface. The app must support equivalent physical- and digital-dice paths from the same visible assembled pool. It should use progressive disclosure, recognition over recall, minimal typing, professional visual hierarchy and contextual teaching. A new player should be able to play before mastering the rules and learn the rules through play.
