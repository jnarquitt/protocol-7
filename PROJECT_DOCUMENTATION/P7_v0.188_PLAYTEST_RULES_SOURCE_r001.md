# Protocol 7 v0.188 — Playtest Rules Source r002

**Status:** ACTIVE PLAYTEST RULES DRAFT — publication source for v0.188 reconciliation
**Purpose:** Consolidate the playable mechanical baseline before regenerating the rulebook, guide, and app.

This is a playtest authority. Rules may change after reading and table testing. Older mechanics do not return merely because they exist in a previous product.

# 1. Protocol Dice

Protocol 7 uses ordinary polyhedral dice as Protocol Dice. On a Protocol Die, faces 1–3 are blanks. A face of 4 or higher contributes its visible face value to the roll total. Roll the complete pool together and total the contributing faces.

The physical pool is information. A Skill die, three Ability dice, Gear die, VAM die, Advantage die, and Mastery die are not interchangeable anonymous bonuses. Each die represents a reason the character is capable of the action. The table should be able to identify why every die is present.

Matched Blanks and the broader Dice Tricks experiment are disabled for the v0.188 core playtest. They are not substituted for ordinary resolution or critical-style outcomes.

## Difficulties and margin

When a roll is made against a fixed difficulty rather than an opposing pool, use the established scale:

| Difficulty | Target |
|---|---:|
| Easy under pressure | 8 |
| Moderate | 12 |
| Hard | 16 |
| Very Hard | 20 |
| Extreme | 24 |

When the amount by which a roll succeeds matters, use the winning margin:

| Winning Margin | Result |
|---|---|
| 1–3 | Narrow |
| 4–7 | Solid |
| 8–11 | Powerful |
| 12+ | Overwhelming |

The margin is not an extra die or modifier. It is the difference between the successful total and the opposing total or target.

# 2. Abilities

The six Abilities are Strength (STR), Dexterity (DEX), Constitution (CON), Intelligence (INT), Wisdom (WIS), and Charisma (CHA).

At Level 1 assign exactly: **d8, d8, d6, d6, d4, d4** among the six Abilities.

Ability progression uses d4 → d6 → d8 → d10 → d12. No Ability exceeds d12. At Levels 3 and 5 increase one Ability by one step.

# 3. Skills

A normal Skill roll uses the Skill die plus the three Ability dice assigned to that Skill. The reconciled 40-Skill registry is the structured Skill authority. Repeated Ability references are intentional: if a Skill lists DEX, DEX, WIS, the Vector rolls the current DEX die twice and the current WIS die once.

Untrained Skills use d4. Starting characters receive 20 Skill Ranks. Each Level after 1 grants 4 additional Skill Ranks.

| Ranks | Skill Die |
|---:|:---:|
| 0 | d4 |
| 1–2 | d6 |
| 3–5 | d8 |
| 6–9 | d10 |
| 10+ | d12 |

**d12 is the ordinary Skill ceiling.** Additional ranks may still be invested if another rule cares about rank count, but they do not create a die larger than d12.

# 4. Skill Mastery

Skill Mastery replaces progression beyond the ordinary d12 Skill die.

At Level 4, choose one Mastered Skill. At Level 6, choose a second.

Mastering a Skill does not automatically add a die to every roll. A Mastery die requires both the Skill being rolled to be Mastered and legal explicit Mastery access, normally through a high-investment VAM or another rule that specifically provides Mastery access.

When active, the Mastery die equals the current die of the Skill being rolled. Only one Mastery die can apply to a single roll. Mastery is its own named dice source and is never Advantage, Gear, or a generic bonus.

# 5. Building a Roll

The ordinary core pool is one Skill die plus that Skill's three Ability dice. Build the complete pool before rolling.

Additional pool changes require a named legal source.

**Gear:** One primary relevant piece of Gear may add its listed die when its rules apply. Gear is not Advantage. Compatible Gear does not stack unless a specific rule permits it.

**VAM:** A VAM adds a die only when its own rule explicitly grants one. A VAM die remains a VAM die.

**Mastery:** Adds the Skill-matching Mastery die only when Mastery requirements are satisfied.

**Advantage / Disadvantage:** Situational pool adjustment described below.

There is no generic bonus-die slot and no routine flat +1/+2 modifier system.

The normal construction procedure is:
1. Define what the action is trying to accomplish.
2. Choose the Skill.
3. Add the Skill's three associated Ability dice.
4. Add the Skill die.
5. Add one primary relevant Gear die if applicable.
6. Apply explicit VAM effects and other named rule sources.
7. Resolve Advantage and Disadvantage.
8. Add a legal Mastery die if the Skill is Mastered and access is active.
9. Roll the complete pool once; 1–3 are blank and every 4+ scores at face value.

# 6. Advantage and Disadvantage — v0.188 Playtest Rule

Advantage and Disadvantage represent circumstances, not equipment statistics. The GM may award them because of positioning, preparation, teamwork, surprise, research, environmental leverage, exploitation of a known weakness, or another concrete fictional reason. A specific rule may also trigger them automatically. Players are strongly encouraged to create Advantage through smart play.

For this playtest:
- **Advantage:** add one d6 Advantage die to the pool.
- **Disadvantage:** remove the smallest Ability die in the pool for that roll. If more than one Ability die is tied for smallest, the player chooses which tied die is removed.
- Advantage and Disadvantage each cap at one effective instance on a roll. Multiple reasons do not normally stack additional A/D effects.
- If both apply, reasons cancel one-for-one. If reasons remain on only one side, apply that side once.

A d6 Advantage die has an expected Protocol contribution of 2.5 points. Removing a d6 has the same expected magnitude. Removing a d4 is milder at 1 point expected; removing a d8 is stronger at 3.75. This asymmetry is intentional for the v0.188 playtest and is documented in the math-validation record.

# 7. Action Economy

A Vector normally begins a turn with **3 Action Points (AP)**.

A normal Attack costs **1 AP** and may be repeated by paying 1 AP for each attack unless a specific rule says otherwise.

**Movement:** Spend 1 AP to move up to 30 feet. Movement is repeatable: 1 AP = 30 feet, 2 AP = 60 feet, 3 AP = 90 feet. There is no universal Run action and no ×2 Run multiplier.

Other actions state their AP costs. A VAM or specific rule can override an action cost only when it explicitly says so.

# 8. Reaction

A Vector normally has **one Reaction between turns**. A Reaction is not a fourth free AP; it uses the AP economy.

When a Vector ends a turn with unspent AP, that AP remains banked and available to pay for the Vector's Reaction until the beginning of the next turn.

When a Reaction is used:
- spend banked AP from the turn that just ended first;
- only if no banked AP remains may the Vector borrow **at most 1 AP** from the next turn;
- borrowed AP reduces the AP available when the next turn begins;
- any banked AP not spent on a Reaction expires when the next turn begins.

A VAM that activates with a Reaction consumes this normal Reaction unless it explicitly creates an exception. It does not silently grant an additional Reaction.

# 9. Vitality and Hit Points

Hit Points are not meat points and are not Constitution multiplied by a constant. They represent the accumulated ability to remain an effective participant in danger: physical toughness, reflex, judgment, experience, luck, awareness, confidence, positioning, and the countless small choices that turn a potentially decisive event into something survivable.

Protocol 7 deliberately lets all six Abilities speak during character creation rather than declaring one Ability to be the sole owner of survival.

## The Vitality Ritual

After assigning the six starting Ability dice, roll each Ability die once: STR, DEX, CON, INT, WIS, CHA. Record the face associated with each Ability.

Faces 1–3 are blanks for the ritual just as they are for Protocol Dice.

If at least one Ability shows 4 or higher, starting HP equals **18 + the highest face rolled**. With the Level 1 starting array, ordinary starting HP is therefore 22–26.

If all six Ability dice show blanks, the extraordinary result is rewarded rather than punished: starting HP is **28**.

If multiple Abilities tie for the highest qualifying result, choose one of those tied Abilities as the Vector's **Vitality Origin**. This is an identity marker and has no additional numerical modifier unless a later rule explicitly refers to it.

Each Level after 1 adds 4 maximum HP.

# 10. Level Progression — v0.188 Playtest

| Level | BAR | Advancement |
|---:|---:|---|
| 1 | 16 | Starting character; 20 Skill Ranks; Vitality Ritual |
| 2 | 18 | +4 HP, +4 Skill Ranks, choose one Edge |
| 3 | 21 | +4 HP, +4 Skill Ranks, increase one Ability one step |
| 4 | 24 | +4 HP, +4 Skill Ranks, Master one Skill |
| 5 | 27 | +4 HP, +4 Skill Ranks, increase one Ability one step |
| 6 | 30 | +4 HP, +4 Skill Ranks, Master a second Skill |

The BAR progression is a v0.188 playtest progression designed to make expensive late-game VAM/Mastery access possible without making early capacity trivial.

# 11. Level 2 Edges — v0.188 Playtest

At Level 2 choose one Edge. An Edge is a small identity marker, not a class or archetype.

**Driven — once per session.** After rolling, reroll one die and keep the new result.

**Inspired — once per session.** Before a roll, gain Advantage for that roll.

**Prepared — once per session.** Declare a reasonable piece of ordinary preparation that the Vector could plausibly have arranged before the current situation. The GM may narrow an implausibly broad declaration; Prepared cannot create unique mission-critical technology, rewrite established events, or duplicate a VAM.

**Connected — once per session.** Establish a plausible useful contact, introduction, or ordinary piece of social access appropriate to the Vector and setting. It creates an opportunity, not automatic obedience or mission resolution.

**Durable — persistent.** Increase maximum HP by 5.

# 12. BAR and VAMs

BAR (Biocognitive Access Reserve) is capacity used to keep VAMs loaded; it is not energy spent when a VAM operates.

Loading a VAM into unused BAR capacity costs 0 AP. During active play, replacing or unloading already-loaded VAMs as a field reconfiguration costs **1 AP for the whole swap**, not 1 AP per VAM. Outside active play, ordinary reconfiguration does not consume encounter AP.

A VAM cannot be loaded if its BAR cost would exceed current capacity or the Vector lacks its authorization.

The reconciled 84-VAM playtest catalog uses six families: Combat, Defense & Mobility, Recon & Investigation, Social & Cover, Technical & Medical, and Command & Temporal. VAM operations follow the v0.188 reconciliation authority. In particular, VAM dice remain VAM dice; VAMs that negate Disadvantage remove a named reason rather than granting Advantage; Mastery VAMs provide access rather than mastering a Skill; and a VAM reference to a short move means up to 30 feet unless it explicitly states otherwise.

# 13. Gear and Armor

Gear is concrete equipment and matters when it is actually relevant to the action. One primary relevant Gear die is the ordinary limit unless a specific rule explicitly permits another Gear source.

**Armor:** When armor can physically protect the Vector from the incoming threat, add the armor's listed Gear die to the appropriate active defense pool. Armor normally does **not** also subtract damage after the defense roll unless the item or another explicit rule specifically says it does.

# 14. Combat Resolution and Damage

Protocol 7 uses active defense rather than a universal passive Armor Class.

An attack uses the appropriate Combat Skill and its three Ability dice, plus legal Gear/VAM/A/D/Mastery sources. The defender uses the appropriate Defense Skill and its three Ability dice, plus legal armor/Gear/VAM/A/D/Mastery sources. Roll each complete pool and compare totals.

If the attacker does not exceed the defense, the attack does not inflict normal damage. If the attacker wins, **normal damage equals the winning margin**: attacker total minus defense total.

Because armor is normally already represented in the defense pool, it does not then subtract from that damage a second time.

The same margin bands used for other resolution remain useful descriptions of attack quality: 1–3 Narrow, 4–7 Solid, 8–11 Powerful, 12+ Overwhelming. A weapon, VAM, condition, Adventure rule, or other explicit effect may care about one of those bands.

**Critical-style effects:** v0.188 does not invent a new universal critical subsystem merely to fill a publication gap. Explicit weapon/VAM/special-action effects triggered by margin or another stated condition operate as written. Matched Blanks remain disabled. If playtesting shows the game needs a universal critical rule beyond winning-margin damage, that is a later explicit design decision and requires probability testing before promotion.

# 15. Conditions

The reconciled six-condition registry is the structured authority: Prone, Grappled, Suppressed, Stunned, Disoriented, and Restrained.

Conditions use named rules language. Suppressed and Disoriented can create named Disadvantage reasons; they do not carry private d4/d6 penalty dice. Prone and Restrained impose situational Disadvantage only when the condition materially hinders the particular action or defense. Stunned removes Reaction and increases the AP cost of the first action on the next turn by 1. Grappled controls movement according to its source and escape procedure.

# 16. Character Creation Sequence

1. Choose Custom Vector or a preconfigured Vector.
2. Enter the Vector's name.
3. Assign d8, d8, d6, d6, d4, d4 to the six Abilities.
4. Perform the Vitality Ritual and determine starting HP/Vitality Origin.
5. Spend 20 Skill Ranks.
6. Load legal starting VAMs up to 16 BAR; unused BAR is allowed.
7. Select starting Gear under the current Gear rules.
8. Review the resulting pools and derived combat values.

Preconfigured Vectors follow the same rules; they are shortcuts, not exceptions.

# 17. Historical Operations

Protocol 7 missions may use Historical Carrier and Exposure as competing objectives. The established Carrier states are **Fragile → Established → Durable**. Stronger Carrier and lower Exposure are intentionally competing goals rather than one combined score.

Operations identify the minimum Carrier strength required to survive Historical Verification. Mission outcomes remain:
- **Clean Success:** Carrier requirement met; Exposure within target.
- **Exposed Success:** Carrier requirement met; Exposure exceeds target.
- **Contained Failure:** Carrier requirement not met; Exposure within target.
- **Exposed Failure:** Carrier requirement not met; Exposure exceeds target.

The established mission procedure is Briefing → Insertion → Establish Cover → Investigate → Identify Leverage → Intervene → Establish Carrier → Contain Exposure → Extract → Historical Verification.

# 18. Playtest Change Discipline

When a rule feels wrong at the table, record the observation. v0.188 is expected to change. The important distinction is between a deliberate playtest change and an accidental resurrection of an obsolete rule.

Probability-sensitive changes require math/simulation before promotion. Non-probability changes require deliberate consistency review. The rulebook and synchronized canonical rules source define play; app code and databases implement them rather than independently inventing mechanics.
