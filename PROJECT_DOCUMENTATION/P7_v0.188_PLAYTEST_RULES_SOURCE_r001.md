# Protocol 7 v0.188 — Playtest Rules Source r001

**Status:** ACTIVE PLAYTEST RULES DRAFT — publication source for v0.188 reconciliation
**Purpose:** Consolidate the playable mechanical baseline before regenerating the rulebook, guide, and app.

This is a playtest authority. Rules may change after reading and table testing. Older mechanics do not return merely because they exist in a previous product.

# 1. Protocol Dice

Protocol 7 uses ordinary polyhedral dice as Protocol Dice. On a Protocol Die, faces 1–3 are blanks. A face of 4 or higher contributes its visible face value to the roll total. Roll the complete pool together and total the contributing faces.

The physical pool is information. A Skill die, three Ability dice, Gear die, VAM die, Advantage die, and Mastery die are not interchangeable anonymous bonuses. Each die represents a reason the character is capable of the action. The table should be able to identify why every die is present.

Matched Blanks and the broader Dice Tricks experiment are disabled for the v0.188 core playtest. Ordinary critical rules remain whatever the current combat procedure specifies; unfinished Matched Blank effects are not substituted for them.

# 2. Abilities

The six Abilities are Strength (STR), Dexterity (DEX), Constitution (CON), Intelligence (INT), Wisdom (WIS), and Charisma (CHA).

At Level 1 assign exactly: **d8, d8, d6, d6, d4, d4** among the six Abilities.

Ability progression uses d4 → d6 → d8 → d10 → d12. No Ability exceeds d12. At Levels 3 and 5 increase one Ability by one step.

# 3. Skills

A normal Skill roll uses the Skill die plus the three Ability dice assigned to that Skill. The current 40-Skill registry is the structured Skill authority.

Untrained Skills use d4. Starting characters receive 20 Skill Ranks. Each Level after 1 grants 4 additional Skill Ranks.

Skill die by ranks:

| Ranks | Skill Die |
|---:|:---:|
| 0 | d4 |
| 1–2 | d6 |
| 3–5 | d8 |
| 6–9 | d10 |
| 10+ | d12 |

**d12 is the ordinary Skill ceiling.** Additional ranks may still be invested if another rule cares about rank count, but they do not create a die larger than d12.

# 4. Skill Mastery

Skill Mastery replaces progression beyond the ordinary d12 Skill die.

At Level 4, choose one Mastered Skill. At Level 6, choose a second.

Mastering a Skill does not automatically add a die to every roll. A Mastery die requires both:
- the Skill being rolled is one of the Vector's Mastered Skills; and
- the Vector has legal explicit Mastery access, normally through a high-investment VAM or another rule that specifically says it provides Mastery access.

When active, the Mastery die equals the current die of the Skill being rolled. Only one Mastery die can apply to a single roll. Mastery is its own named dice source and is never Advantage, Gear, or a generic bonus.

This intentionally makes six-die-capable pools an investment rather than an automatic consequence of leveling.

# 5. Named Dice Sources

The ordinary core pool is:
- one Skill die;
- the Skill's three Ability dice.

Additional pool changes require a named legal source.

**Gear:** One primary relevant piece of Gear may add its listed die when its rules apply. Gear is not Advantage.

**VAM:** A VAM adds a die only when its own rule explicitly grants one. A VAM die remains a VAM die.

**Mastery:** Adds the Skill-matching Mastery die only when Mastery requirements are satisfied.

**Advantage / Disadvantage:** Situational pool adjustment described below.

There is no generic bonus-die slot and no routine flat +1/+2 modifier system. When a design needs a mechanical benefit, use the established dice-facing language or another explicit rule.

# 6. Advantage and Disadvantage — v0.188 Playtest Rule

Advantage and Disadvantage represent circumstances, not equipment statistics. The GM may award them because of positioning, preparation, teamwork, surprise, research, environmental leverage, exploitation of a known weakness, or another concrete fictional reason. A specific rule may also trigger them automatically. Players are strongly encouraged to create Advantage through smart play.

For this playtest:
- **Advantage:** add one d6 Advantage die to the pool.
- **Disadvantage:** remove the smallest Ability die in the pool for that roll. If more than one Ability die is tied for smallest, the player chooses which tied die is removed; this normally matters only for source display, because equal dice are mathematically identical.
- Advantage and Disadvantage each cap at one effective instance on a roll. Multiple reasons strengthen the fictional justification but do not stack additional A/D dice/removals unless an explicit rule says otherwise.
- If both apply, one Advantage and one Disadvantage cancel. If reasons remain on only one side after cancellation, apply that side once.

Why this playtest rule: a d6 Advantage die has an expected Protocol contribution of 2.5 points. Removing a d6 has the same expected magnitude. Removing a d4 is milder (1 point expected), while removing a d8 is stronger (3.75 expected); therefore Disadvantage naturally hurts highly capable pools somewhat more when their lowest relevant Ability is larger. This is intentional for testing and should be reviewed after playtest data.

A/D never changes the identity of Gear, VAM, or Mastery dice.

# 7. Action Economy

A Vector normally begins a turn with 3 Action Points (AP).

Normal attacks cost 1 AP unless a specific action says otherwise.

**Movement:** Spend 1 AP to move up to 30 feet. Movement is repeatable. A Vector may therefore spend 1 AP for 30 feet, 2 AP for 60 feet, or all 3 AP for 90 feet. There is no universal Run action and no ×2 Run multiplier.

Reaction remains a separate reactive resource under the current combat procedure; do not convert movement into a special Run action or consume Reaction merely to move.

# 8. Vitality and Hit Points

Hit Points are not meat points and are not Constitution multiplied by a constant. They represent the accumulated ability to remain an effective participant in danger: physical toughness, reflex, judgment, experience, luck, awareness, confidence, positioning, and the countless small choices that turn a potentially decisive event into something survivable.

Protocol 7 deliberately lets all six Abilities speak during character creation rather than declaring one Ability to be the sole owner of survival.

## The Vitality Ritual

After assigning the six starting Ability dice, roll each Ability die once: STR, DEX, CON, INT, WIS, CHA. Record the face associated with each Ability.

Faces 1–3 are blanks for the ritual just as they are for Protocol Dice.

If at least one Ability shows 4 or higher, starting HP equals **18 + the highest face rolled**. With the Level 1 starting array, ordinary starting HP is therefore 22–26.

If all six Ability dice show blanks, the extraordinary result is rewarded rather than punished: starting HP is **28**.

If multiple Abilities tie for the highest qualifying result, choose one of those tied Abilities as the Vector's **Vitality Origin**. This is an identity marker explaining which part of the character seemed to carry them through the ritual; it does not create an additional numerical modifier unless a later rule explicitly refers to it.

Each Level after 1 adds 4 maximum HP.

# 9. Level Progression — v0.188 Playtest

| Level | BAR | Advancement |
|---:|---:|---|
| 1 | 16 | Starting character; 20 Skill Ranks; Vitality Ritual |
| 2 | 18 | +4 HP, +4 Skill Ranks, choose one Edge |
| 3 | 21 | +4 HP, +4 Skill Ranks, increase one Ability one step |
| 4 | 24 | +4 HP, +4 Skill Ranks, Master one Skill |
| 5 | 27 | +4 HP, +4 Skill Ranks, increase one Ability one step |
| 6 | 30 | +4 HP, +4 Skill Ranks, Master a second Skill |

The BAR progression is a v0.188 playtest progression designed to make expensive late-game VAM/Mastery access possible without making early capacity trivial. It is explicitly subject to playtest revision.

# 10. Level 2 Edges — v0.188 Playtest

At Level 2 choose one Edge. An Edge is a small identity marker, not a class or archetype.

**Driven — once per session.** After rolling, reroll one die and keep the new result.

**Inspired — once per session.** Before a roll, gain Advantage for that roll. This is Advantage, not an anonymous bonus die.

**Prepared — once per session.** Declare a reasonable piece of ordinary preparation that the Vector could plausibly have arranged before the current situation. The GM may narrow an implausibly broad declaration; Prepared cannot create unique mission-critical technology, rewrite established events, or duplicate a VAM.

**Connected — once per session.** Establish a plausible useful contact, introduction, or ordinary piece of social access appropriate to the Vector and setting. It creates an opportunity, not automatic obedience or mission resolution.

**Durable — persistent.** Increase maximum HP by 5.

# 11. BAR and VAMs

BAR (Biocognitive Access Reserve) is the capacity used to keep VAMs loaded.

Loading a VAM into unused BAR capacity costs 0 AP. During active play, replacing already-loaded VAMs as a field swap costs 1 AP for the whole swap, not 1 AP per VAM. Outside active play, ordinary reconfiguration does not consume encounter AP.

A VAM cannot be loaded if its BAR cost would exceed current capacity or if the Vector lacks its required authorization/access.

The v0.188 VAM database is a playtest catalog and must be reconciled against this rules source before publication. Any VAM that uses generic bonus terminology, obsolete Skill progression, or an incompatible A/D rule must be revised rather than allowed to override this document.

# 12. Gear

Gear is concrete equipment and should matter when it is actually relevant to the action. One primary relevant Gear die is the ordinary limit unless a specific rule explicitly permits another Gear source.

Armor belongs to defense resolution rather than becoming passive arithmetic. Do not reintroduce flat Armor Class-style modifiers into the system.

# 13. Combat Baseline

v0.188 retains the established Protocol 7 combat baseline except where this source explicitly supersedes it:
- 3 AP per normal turn;
- ordinary attacks generally cost 1 AP;
- movement is 30 feet per AP and repeatable;
- defenses are rolled pools rather than a passive universal target number;
- armor participates as a defense-pool Gear die where relevant;
- Reaction remains part of the active defense economy;
- damage, weapon profiles, attack Skill mappings, Reaction banking/borrowing details, and condition effects must be carried forward from the last promoted combat rules unless a v0.188 playtest rule explicitly replaces them.

This section is intentionally conservative: v0.188's major changes are character progression, Vitality, Mastery, dice-source discipline, and app usability. It is not an invitation to redesign unrelated combat mechanics during publication.

# 14. Conditions

Use the current structured condition registry after reconciliation. Conditions must state exactly what they do using named rules language. A condition may impose Disadvantage only if it explicitly says so. No condition may manufacture an unexplained flat modifier or anonymous bonus/penalty die.

# 15. Character Creation Sequence

1. Choose Custom Vector or a preconfigured Vector.
2. Enter the Vector's name.
3. Assign d8, d8, d6, d6, d4, d4 to the six Abilities.
4. Perform the Vitality Ritual and determine starting HP/Vitality Origin.
5. Spend 20 Skill Ranks.
6. Load legal starting VAMs up to 16 BAR; unused BAR is allowed.
7. Select starting Gear under the current Gear rules.
8. Review the resulting pools and derived combat values.

Preconfigured Vectors must follow the same rules; they are shortcuts, not exceptions.

# 16. Playtest Change Discipline

When a rule feels wrong at the table, record the observation. v0.188 is expected to change. The important distinction is between a deliberate playtest change and an accidental resurrection of an obsolete rule.

Probability-sensitive changes require math/simulation before promotion. Non-probability changes require deliberate consistency review. The rulebook and synchronized canonical rules source define play; app code and databases implement them rather than independently inventing mechanics.
