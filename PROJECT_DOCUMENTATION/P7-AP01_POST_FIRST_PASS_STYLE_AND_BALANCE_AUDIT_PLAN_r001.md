# P7-AP01 — Post-First-Pass Style and Balance Audit Plan r001

**Trigger:** Immediately after M06 completes its first full playable/simulated pass and before final Adventure Path release polish.

## Purpose

The first pass establishes that all six missions work individually and that the Continuance Files story resolves coherently. The second pass must evaluate the Adventure Path as one complete play experience rather than six manuscripts.

## Audit dimensions

### Adventure-mode balance
For each mission and across the full path, estimate actual table time devoted to:
- action/combat;
- investigation/mystery;
- exploration/environmental interaction;
- social interaction/negotiation;
- survival/disaster pressure;
- technical/problem-solving play;
- moral/strategic choice;
- debrief/continuity revelation.

Do not impose an arbitrary equal split. The goal is deliberate variety and pacing. Flag missions that feel mechanically or dramatically too similar to their neighbors, long stretches with no physical jeopardy, and repeated climaxes that resolve through the same mode of play.

### Action audit
Check whether each mission contains meaningful opportunities for physical action even when combat is not mandatory. Action may include rescue, pursuit, hazardous traversal, disaster response, sabotage, escape, protection, or combat. Verify that combat-forward characters have useful contributions without requiring every mission to become a firefight.

### Investigation audit
Check clue density, revelation pacing, redundancy, false-finish-line clarity, and fail-forward behavior. Ensure later missions do not simply repeat the same 'discover the object is not the Carrier' structure with different props.

### Exploration audit
Evaluate whether each historical location produces playable geography rather than background wallpaper. Check maps/routes, environmental choices, sensory orientation, movement through unfamiliar spaces, and whether location-specific features matter to decisions.

### Social-play audit
Check recurring Covenant arguments, historical NPC agency, negotiation opportunities, cover identities, and whether NPCs have conflicting motives rather than existing as clue dispensers.

### Historical-era differentiation
Review the path as a sequence of places and eras. Each mission should have a distinct sensory and social identity. Check clothing, technology, transportation, communication, architecture, language assumptions, authority structures, ordinary daily life, and what characters can plausibly know.

### Read-aloud and GM orientation
Especially for unfamiliar eras, verify that the GM receives enough cinematic read-aloud material to establish where the characters are, what is happening around them, what is culturally/technologically unfamiliar, and what environmental facts matter to play. Read-aloud text should teach through sensory detail rather than become an encyclopedia lecture.

### Mechanical spotlight balance
Track which Skills, Ability combinations, VAM categories, gear, AP decisions, defenses, combat options, and Exposure choices receive meaningful use across the path. Identify character concepts that repeatedly lack useful spotlight opportunities.

### Carrier/Exposure balance
Check whether Carrier and Exposure choices remain meaningfully different from mission to mission. Exposure must not become a simple punishment meter, and Durable must not always be the obviously correct answer.

### Difficulty and resource pressure
Compare expected roll difficulty, action pressure, time clocks, resource consumption, VAM reconfiguration opportunities, and consequences of failed rolls. Look for difficulty spikes and missions that accidentally become trivial for one common character build.

### Campaign rhythm
Audit opening intensity, mid-mission pivots, false victories, climaxes, debriefs, callbacks, and seven-stroke revelations. The six missions should rise and fall rather than feel like six copies of the same dramatic curve.

### Repetition audit
Explicitly search for repeated structures, phrases, NPC roles, clue delivery methods, Covenant tactics, disaster beats, and moral dilemmas. Repetition is acceptable when it creates thematic callback; otherwise revise it.

### Player-agency audit
For every mission, identify at least three materially different successful approaches and meaningful partial/failure states. Verify that clever player-created routes are supported and that the campaign does not secretly require one canonical solution.

### GM workload audit
Run a quick-review test on every guide. A GM with 10–15 minutes should understand the story, Carrier, opposition, major NPCs, scene flow, historical setting, and likely failure points. Check information placement, cross-references, mobile readability, and table usability.

### Full-path simulation
After individual mission simulations, simulate the complete six-mission path with several campaign personas, including at minimum:
- investigation-heavy group;
- action/combat-forward group;
- high-Exposure interventionists;
- low-Exposure historical minimalists;
- Covenant-sympathetic/anti-Aletheia group;
- mixed-experience convention-style group.

Track accumulated consequences and whether callbacks actually matter.

## Deliverables

The post-first-pass audit should produce:
- one mission-by-mission style/balance matrix;
- one full-path pacing analysis;
- one mechanical spotlight matrix;
- one repetition report;
- one historical/read-aloud coverage report;
- prioritized revision list: critical / important / polish;
- revised campaign architecture if first-pass discoveries require it;
- synchronized landing pages and current-state documentation after promoted revisions.

## Release rule

Do not call the Adventure Path release-ready merely because M01–M06 are individually playable. The path must pass this whole-campaign style and balance audit first.
