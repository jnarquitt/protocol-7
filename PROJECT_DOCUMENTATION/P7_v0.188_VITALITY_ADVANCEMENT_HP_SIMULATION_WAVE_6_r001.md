# Protocol 7 v0.188 — Vitality & Advancement HP Simulation Wave 6 r001

**Status:** SIMULATION REPORT — candidate recommendation

## Question

The new Level 1 Vitality ritual defines HP as an amalgam of the entire character rather than a derivative of Constitution or Strength. Wave 6 asks how HP should grow after Level 1 without contradicting that philosophy or allowing long-term random HP inequality.

## Starting Vitality candidate

Roll STR, DEX, CON, INT, WIS, and CHA separately after assigning the standard d8,d8,d6,d6,d4,d4 array. Record each face. The highest face is the Vitality result.

**Starting HP = 18 + highest face.**

Ordinary range: **22–26 HP**.

If all six dice show blanks (1–3), use the exceptional all-blanks result: **28 HP**.

This means no normally generated Vector begins below 22 HP.

## Starting distribution observations

Because two d8s are present, 7 and 8 are common enough that the upper end of the 22–26 band is not exotic. The ritual creates variation, but the entire ordinary spread is only four HP from minimum to maximum.

The all-blanks result is roughly a 2% event under the current starting array. Its 28 HP reward is therefore a memorable exception rather than a routine optimization path.

No player can intentionally choose which Ability produces the highest face. Ability assignment changes the narrative identity of the winning die but, because the starting die multiset is fixed, does not change the overall HP probability distribution. This is a useful anti-optimization property.

## Advancement models tested conceptually

### A. STR-derived growth
Half current STR maximum per Level (+2 through +6).

**Rejected for current candidate.** It makes later HP increasingly become a Strength subsystem after the Level 1 rules explicitly teach that HP is not owned by one Ability. It also rewards Ability optimization twice: once through STR-related actions and again through durability.

### B. CON-derived growth
Fixed relationship to Constitution.

**Rejected.** It recreates the original problem in a different form and makes CON a mandatory durability tax.

### C. Roll for HP every Level
Roll one or more dice and add the result.

**Rejected for normal advancement.** Repeated random growth compounds variance across a six-Level campaign and can leave equally experienced characters permanently separated because of several unlucky advancement rolls. The Level 1 ritual already supplies the desired moment of uncertainty and character discovery.

### D. Fixed +3 HP per Level
Simple and predictable.

At Level 6, ordinary starting characters become **37–41 HP**; the all-blanks survivor becomes **43 HP**.

This is stable but slightly compresses the feeling of upper-tier durability relative to the previous d6/d8 STR growth expectations.

### E. Fixed +4 HP per Level
Simple and predictable.

At Level 6, ordinary starting characters become **42–46 HP**; the all-blanks survivor becomes **48 HP**.

This preserves the initial 4-HP ordinary spread exactly across the campaign. No character falls farther behind another merely because of advancement randomness. The growth is substantial enough to support the intended Level 1→6 / d20 1→12 campaign analogy without requiring inflated starting HP.

### F. Escalating fixed growth (+3/+3/+4/+4/+5)
Produces 41–45 ordinary Level 6 HP and 47 for the exceptional survivor.

This creates more bookkeeping and an advancement table for very little additional design value. Rejected unless later lethality testing shows a specific need for a curve.

## Recommendation

**Test fixed +4 maximum HP at every advancement from Level 2 through Level 6.**

The complete HP progression then depends only on the Level 1 Vitality result:

| Starting HP | L2 | L3 | L4 | L5 | L6 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 22 | 26 | 30 | 34 | 38 | 42 |
| 23 | 27 | 31 | 35 | 39 | 43 |
| 24 | 28 | 32 | 36 | 40 | 44 |
| 25 | 29 | 33 | 37 | 41 | 45 |
| 26 | 30 | 34 | 38 | 42 | 46 |
| 28 exceptional | 32 | 36 | 40 | 44 | 48 |

The ritual creates identity and modest starting variation. Advancement then represents accumulated experience, luck, conditioning, judgment, confidence, resources, and survival knowledge as a universal benefit of becoming a more experienced Vector.

## Durable Edge interaction

The Level 2 **Durable +5 maximum HP** candidate remains mathematically meaningful under fixed +4 advancement:

- ordinary non-Durable L6: 42–46 HP;
- ordinary Durable L6: 47–51 HP;
- exceptional all-blanks + Durable: 53 HP.

This is a large but bounded durability specialization. Durable never improves attacks, defenses, Skills, BAR, Mastery, AP, or Reactions. It is therefore a genuine identity choice rather than a universal optimization requirement.

**Flag:** final combat lethality should specifically test a 53-HP L6 Durable exceptional survivor, because that is the foreseeable top end under current character creation without additional HP-granting Features.

## Why fixed advancement supports the fiction

Starting HP asks the dice to reveal something intangible about the newly created character. Later HP growth is different: the character has now survived missions. Their reserve grows because they have experience.

The game therefore uses randomness where randomness adds discovery, then predictability where randomness would create long-term unfairness.

This distinction should be explained briefly in the rulebook.

## Ability-dice design observation

The Vitality ritual also demonstrates a useful property of the standard Ability array. Because every starting character receives the same multiset of Ability dice, choosing where to place the dice changes **who the character is** without secretly changing the mathematical distribution of the Vitality ritual.

A player who places a d8 in INT is not buying more HP than a player who places that d8 in STR. Both have the same two d8/two d6/two d4 Vitality distribution. What changes is the probability that a particular Ability becomes the recorded Vitality Origin.

This cleanly separates **mechanical fairness** from **character interpretation**.

## Wave 6 verdict

**GREEN for further integration testing.**

Recommended HP candidate:
- Level 1: Vitality ritual; 18 + highest individual Ability face;
- ordinary Level 1 range 22–26;
- all six blanks: 28 HP exceptional result;
- Level 2–6: +4 maximum HP each Level;
- Durable Edge: +5 maximum HP at Level 2 instead of a once-per-session Edge resource.

Before public promotion, rerun encounter lethality using the new HP bands, especially:
- L1 22 HP minimum;
- L1 26 HP ordinary maximum;
- L1 28 HP exceptional;
- L6 42 HP minimum;
- L6 46 HP ordinary maximum;
- L6 48 HP exceptional;
- L6 51 HP ordinary Durable maximum;
- L6 53 HP exceptional + Durable maximum.

If these survive, replace the previous STR-derived HP language in the consolidated v0.188 advancement candidate.
