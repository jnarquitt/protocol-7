# Protocol 7 v0.188 — VAM Catalog Audit & Rebuild Scope r001

**Status:** REVISION ANALYSIS — REBUILD RECOMMENDED
**Source audited:** Protocol_7_v0.187_Interactive_Mobile_Character_Sheet.html
**Purpose:** Determine whether the existing VAM catalog can support Level 1–6 authorization progression or requires a systematic rebuild.

## Executive finding

**RED for simple tiering; GREEN for rebuild.**

The current catalog contains **48 VAMs** and a useful foundation of concepts, but it was designed primarily around BAR cost bands and starting loadouts, not around six levels of advancement. Simply assigning the existing entries to Level 1–6 authorization tiers would create severe category gaps, uneven reward cadence, and several cases where BAR cost is doing the work that authorization tier should do.

The correct v0.188 approach is a **catalog rebuild that preserves/revises good existing concepts**, not a patch adding one or two high-level VAMs.

## Current catalog inventory

### By BAR cost
- **2 BAR:** 15 VAMs
- **4 BAR:** 19 VAMs
- **6 BAR:** 9 VAMs
- **8 BAR:** 4 VAMs
- **12 BAR:** 1 VAM
- **Total:** 48 VAMs

This distribution is strongly weighted toward inexpensive starting-grade options: 34 of 48 entries (71%) cost 2 or 4 BAR.

### By functional category
The live app currently groups VAMs into:
- Combat
- Defense & Mobility
- Recon & Investigation
- Social & Cover
- Technical & Medical
- Command & Temporal

These categories are good and should be retained as the initial taxonomy unless rebuild testing shows a reason to change them.

## What the current catalog does well

### Low-BAR utility is healthy
The 2-BAR layer contains many modules that remain useful even for veteran Vectors: Period Native, Fine Motor Stabilizer, Balance Assist, Grip Assist, Breath Control, Stress Governor, Sensory Filter, Vocal Control, Threat Focus, Motion Economy, Fast Draw, Snap Shot, Cover Protocol, Jury-Rigger, and Steady Hands.

This supports the v0.188 design law that low-tier VAMs must not become obsolete.

### Several 4–6 BAR concepts are excellent advancement seeds
Examples include Counterintelligence, Deep Cover, Pattern Index, Route Modeling, Rapid Triage, Protective Intercept, Command Link, Breach Window, Counter-Ambush, Prepared Contingency, Field Analysis, Field Fabrication, and Multi-Track Attention.

These are better than simple numerical bonuses because they change permissions, information, action economy, reactions, or decision space.

### Existing categories cover the major Vector fantasies
Combat, protection/mobility, investigation, social infiltration, technical/medical, and command/temporal functions provide a viable chassis for six-level progression.

## Why simple tiering fails

### 1. BAR cost and advancement tier are currently conflated
The existing UI presents VAMs by BAR tier: 2, 4, 6, 8, and 12. That encourages an implicit assumption that a more expensive VAM is automatically a higher-level VAM.

For v0.188, these must be independent dimensions:
- **Authorization Tier** answers: when may a Vector learn/use this capability?
- **BAR Cost** answers: how much active capacity does it occupy?

A Level 6 authorization may still unlock a 2-BAR specialist VAM. A Level 1 catalog may contain a costly but narrow module if balance supports it.

### 2. The high end is far too thin
Only five current VAMs cost 8+ BAR: Temporal Intuition, Tactical Reposition, Zero-Window Entry, Emergency Protocols, and Aletheian Intuition.

That is not enough breadth for Level 5 Elite and Level 6 Continuance authorization across six functional categories.

### 3. Advancement rewards would become role-dependent
A temporal or combat-oriented Vector has more obvious late-catalog material than a diplomat, investigator, engineer, medic, or social specialist. Level advancement must offer exciting choices to every major character fantasy.

### 4. Some existing VAMs are mission procedures disguised as general character options
Examples requiring review include Field Analysis's direct Carrier improvement, Thanatic Anchor's highly specific terminal-host insertion procedure, Temporal Intuition's fulcrum question, and Aletheian Intuition's encoded insight.

These may remain VAMs, but their scope must be tested against the generic VAM design grammar and Adventure Path procedures.

### 5. Several concepts overlap
Fine Motor Stabilizer and Steady Hands substantially overlap. Some combat/mobility modules create adjacent or partially redundant action-economy effects. A rebuild should merge, sharpen, or differentiate these rather than preserve duplication for catalog size.

### 6. Starting loadouts already consume much of the best identity space
The current app provides 14 preconfigured VAM paths plus Flexible/Blank. Many of their signature concepts are available immediately. Without new advancement design, later levels risk feeling like 'more options from the same shelf' rather than genuine authorization growth.

## Rebuild design target

### Catalog size
Target approximately **72–90 VAMs** for v0.188 testing, subject to simulation rather than quota.

Why: six functional categories × six levels does not require one new VAM per category per level, but every advancement boundary should present multiple attractive choices to every broad play style. A catalog in this range allows approximately 12–15 meaningful concepts per category while retaining cheap evergreen modules.

### Authorization bands
Use six explicit authorization bands tied to Protocol 7 Levels:

- **L1 Standard** — foundational identity and broadly useful modules.
- **L2 Advanced I** — sharper specialization, first meaningful combinations.
- **L3 High-Integration** — first capabilities designed around 18 BAR and Core Growth.
- **L4 Advanced II** — veteran breadth, coordination, multi-problem handling.
- **L5 Elite** — strong scene-shaping capabilities and expensive commitments.
- **L6 Continuance** — rare capstone options that create new strategic choices without solving missions automatically.

### Every authorization band must serve every play style
At each Level 2–6 unlock, the catalog should contain meaningful new options in at least:
- combat/control;
- defense/mobility/rescue;
- recon/investigation;
- social/cover;
- technical/medical;
- command/temporal.

Not every band needs equal counts, but no major archetype should go multiple levels without a compelling unlock.

## VAM design grammar for rebuild

Prefer VAMs that do one or more of the following:
- grant permission to attempt something otherwise unavailable;
- remove a narrow procedural obstacle;
- alter AP timing without increasing base AP;
- create or modify a Reaction;
- provide truthful but bounded information;
- allow team coordination;
- compress time or steps for a specialized task;
- let a Vector manage multiple simultaneous problems;
- transform a success into a new tactical/mission choice;
- interact with Exposure, Carrier, insertion, extraction, or historical constraints in a bounded way.

Use routine bonus dice sparingly. Avoid '+bigger die because higher level' as the primary advancement language.

## BAR-cost philosophy

The rebuilt catalog should deliberately mix costs inside authorization bands.

Suggested cost grammar for testing:
- **1–2 BAR:** narrow evergreen utility, sensory/motor filtering, small permissions.
- **3–4 BAR:** signature specialist capability or frequent tactical option.
- **5–6 BAR:** strong scene capability, coordination, or action-economy change.
- **7–8 BAR:** major loadout-defining capability.
- **9–12 BAR:** exceptional mission-shaping/capstone module; rare and tightly bounded.

The current even-only costs are not a required Engine rule. Odd BAR values should be considered if they improve loadout texture.

## Preserve / revise / reconsider examples

### Preserve concept, rewrite as needed
Period Native; Fast Draw; Cover Protocol; Counterintelligence; Deep Cover; Overwatch; Tactical Advance; Combat Rescue; Field Fabrication; Command Link; Prepared Contingency; Pattern Index; Memory Cueing; Route Modeling; Rapid Triage; Protective Intercept; Multi-Track Attention.

### Merge or differentiate
Fine Motor Stabilizer / Steady Hands.

### Reconsider scope or placement
Field Analysis; Temporal Intuition; Aletheian Intuition; Thanatic Anchor; Zero-Window Entry; Emergency Protocols.

'Reconsider' does not mean delete. These are distinctive Protocol 7 ideas but require clearer authorization, cost, and mission-procedure boundaries.

## Advancement experience target

A player reaching a new Level should experience three different forms of anticipation:

1. **I am better at my established job** — Skill/Ability progression.
2. **I can sustain a richer configuration** — BAR milestones.
3. **Aletheia has authorized things I could not do before** — new VAM band.

The third item is why catalog rebuild is necessary. VAM authorization should function as Protocol 7's setting-specific expression of the Protocol Dice Engine Feature Access axis.

## Starting character implications

The rebuild should not force all current starting VAM loadouts to disappear. Instead:
- audit each preconfigured path;
- rebuild each as a legal L1 Standard package at 16 BAR or less;
- preserve recognizable identity;
- ensure no starting package accidentally contains a later-tier capability;
- create suggested upgrade paths without turning Paths into classes.

Players remain free to build cross-category/custom VAM configurations within authorization and BAR rules.

## Homebrew boundary

Authorization Tier is a Protocol 7 interpretation of Protocol Dice Engine Feature Access. BAR is a Protocol 7 interpretation of Engine Capacity.

The generic Engine must not require VAMs, Aletheia authorization, temporal technology, or the specific six Protocol 7 categories. This rebuild must remain setting-layer content.

Only then rebuild the actual catalog and run Level 1→6 persistent-character simulations.

## Audit verdict

The user's design instinct is supported by the existing catalog analysis: **v0.188 needs a systematic VAM rebuild, not a handful of additions.**

The current 48-entry catalog is valuable source material and should be mined aggressively, but it is not structured to carry six levels of Feature Access progression by itself.
