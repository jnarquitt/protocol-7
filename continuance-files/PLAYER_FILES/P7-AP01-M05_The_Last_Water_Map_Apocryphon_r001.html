The Red City Thirsts: A Travelers’ Game of Wells, Gates, and Seven Households

Complete period artifact for The Last Water Map

Box Lid

This nineteenth-century parlor game claims to reproduce an “ancient desert diversion.” No maker is listed. The board depicts a rose-red city, six household wells, and one well outside the board.

Rules for Respectable Travelers

Players do not own water. Each controls a responsibility: Catch, Slow, Settle, Store, Share, or Repair. Water stones enter unpredictably. Nobody scores for filling a private cistern while another district is dry. The city survives only when no player commands the whole system.

The seventh role, Remember, is absent. Its track is rubbed white. Notes in three inks contradict: Remember moves water backward; Remember plays after the board is destroyed; Remember is not a player but the next table.

The Last Water Map

After an earthquake cracks a dam and blocks a channel, players choose between repairing the public diversion, opening a sealed refuge, or preserving the map from an imperial surveyor. All are possible, never on the same turn.

A reservoir is not a hole full of water. It is an agreement between rain, stone, labor, and strangers not yet born.

Travelers’ Notes

Sidebars repeat claims about buried cities, antediluvian engineers, star maps, giants, and a globe-spanning mother culture. Each receives a dry correction: “Or perhaps people living with floods became excellent at remembering floods.” The corrections’ initials spell NOT FIRST. NOT LAST. CONNECTED.

The Broken Board

Folded along earthquake lines, six channels align into the Sanctuary utility diagram first seen in M01. A seventh runs from the completed diagram backward to the game’s printing date.
