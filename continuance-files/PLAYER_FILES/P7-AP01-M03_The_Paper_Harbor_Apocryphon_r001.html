The Port Without Ships: A Bureaucratic Farce in Seven Carbon Copies

Complete period artifact for The Paper Harbor

Playbill

This cheap 1939 playbill advertises a comedy never staged, at a Marseille theater already closed. The cast includes a Clerk, Refugee, Inspector, Forger, Harbor Master, Woman Without a Country, and The Audience, appearing as itself.

Act I: Window Number Six

In the Republic of Paper, no ship may sail until its passenger proves where they came from, where they are going, why they left, why they stayed, and that every office agrees. The Refugee possesses six documents. Each proves another invalid.

The Clerk discovers the Inspector does not read names; he reads arrangements. A blue card behind a white card means review. A clipped corner means return tomorrow. A purple stamp means the person has become the problem. The Clerk changes arrangements while preserving every word.

Act II: The Harbor in a Drawer

The Forger proposes one perfect list of everyone deserving passage. The Woman Without a Country laughs: “A perfect list is a harbor one policeman can capture.” She tears it into seven categories useless alone: food needs, safe beds, document skills, family contacts, travel routes, danger signs, and a blank card held by no office.

The Inspector raids the theater. He finds hundreds of accurate cards and cannot reconstruct one person. Each refugee carries one fact about somebody else. The network remembers what the archive refuses to know.

Mercy needs records. Tyranny also needs records. The difference is whether one hand can close around all of them.

Audience Ballot

Readers choose what the Clerk keeps: names, routes, skills, debts, family ties, false identities, or nothing. Tiny print says: “Your selection will be entered into the next performance.”

Missing Biography and Corrections

The biography for The Audience is cut out. Held to light, fibers show seven strokes and YOU WERE THE NIGHT CLERKS.

The Refugee is corrected to plural. The Harbor Master is corrected to no one. The final curtain is corrected to a border crossing. A notice warns that a man called Fry will arrive after the play closes and must receive an office that looks less important than it is.
