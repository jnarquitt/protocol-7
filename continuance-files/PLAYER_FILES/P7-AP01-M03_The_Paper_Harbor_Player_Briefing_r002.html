Protocol 7 · P7-AP01-M03

The Paper Harbor

Marseille, France · November 1940 · Player Briefing r002

Mission Context

Marseille is crowded with people trying to leave Europe through a maze of consulates, aid offices, sponsors, travel papers, exit permissions, transit visas, and unofficial routes. Aletheia has identified a historical discontinuity connected to records that should help people remain identifiable when primary papers are lost or destroyed.

Real historical refugees and rescue workers retain their own achievements. Your mission concerns a fictional continuity node operating alongside those events.

Apocryphon

The harbor has no ships. Seven desks open onto it. A name written once is lost. A name written everywhere is found by the wrong man. Tear the page until no hand can hold the whole person, then ask three strangers whether she is still the same woman.

Known Constraints

  • A complete record may be more dangerous than an incomplete one.
  • Future technology can solve document problems quickly but creates serious historical evidence.
  • The mission may require preserving relationships between records rather than one object.
  • People whose identities appear in records are not merely clues; their consent and safety matter.

Vector Questions

  • When is being remembered by an institution protection?
  • When is it danger?
  • If no single record contains the whole truth, how much corroboration is enough?
  • Would your Vector destroy a truthful document to protect the people named in it?
