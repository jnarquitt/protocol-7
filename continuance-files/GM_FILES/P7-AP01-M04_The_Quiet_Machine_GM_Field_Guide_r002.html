The Quiet Machine

Antikythera & Athens · 1900–1902 · GM Field Guide · Protocol 7 v0.187

Run This Tonight — Two-Minute Brief

The story: In 1900–1901 Symian sponge divers and the Greek state recover an extraordinary ancient wreck off Antikythera. Among the corroded bronze is something nobody yet understands. The Vectors initially believe they must protect a unique machine from being lost, miscatalogued, or stripped apart. They are wrong.

The false objective: Save the Antikythera mechanism.

The true Carrier: A reproducible habit of technical inquiry: record relationships before interpretation, preserve fragments and provenance, make independent descriptions, and leave enough evidence that later investigators can challenge earlier conclusions. The machine is precious. The process by which strangers can rediscover what it does is the Carrier.

The dramatic question: Does civilization survive because somebody saves a masterpiece—or because ordinary people leave enough disciplined evidence for someone else to understand it later?

Seven beats: dangerous recovery → apparently worthless bronze → race to preserve the object → conflicting interpretations → Covenant offer to save the artifact but suppress reproducible documentation → realization that the documentation chain is the mission → verification reveals a seven-stroke relationship recorded before Aletheia's known temporal program.

Historical Background That Matters at the Table

The wreck was found by sponge divers from Symi around 1900. Recovery in 1900–1901 involved the Greek Archaeological Service and Royal Navy and became a foundational episode in underwater archaeology. The cargo included sculpture, luxury objects, and corroded bronze fragments. In 1902 investigators began recognizing that one bronze mass contained gears; early scholars disagreed about what the mechanism was. That disagreement is useful: the mission does not require the Vectors to hand history the modern answer.

GM boundary: Do not have the Vectors invent, decode, or receive credit for the mechanism. Historical people retain discovery and scholarship. The Vectors' invisible contribution is making sure evidence remains interpretable enough for those people—and later generations—to do their own work.

Table texture: wet ropes, heavy diving dress, decompression danger not yet fully understood, crates of spectacular sculpture competing for attention, salt and corrosion, museum rooms full of objects awaiting description, handwritten inventories, sketches, rubbings, arguments over whether an ugly bronze lump deserves scarce time.

GM Truth — What Is Actually Happening?

Before intervention: the fragments survive, but a small chain of handling and documentation errors makes later reconstruction substantially less reliable. One relationship between fragments is lost when cleaning, storage, and cataloguing separate evidence that only makes sense together.

Without the Vectors: the mechanism still becomes famous. This is not a mission to create its historical importance. What becomes Fragile is the transferable method demonstrated by its investigation: preserve context, measurements, competing hypotheses, and independent copies so future techniques can interrogate old evidence.

With intervention: several ordinary people independently record complementary facts. No one has the answer. Together their notes let later investigators reconstruct what none of them understood alone.

The Covenant is partly right: standardized technical description and interoperability eventually support enormous systems of coordination. Dr. Iona Morrow argues that Aletheia is not preserving curiosity; it is selecting ancestors for a world in which every system can be made legible to every other system.

Fifteen-Minute GM Preparation

  • Remember one sentence: save the evidence chain, not just the machine.
  • Draw four boxes for Recovery Pressure and four for Covenant Pressure.
  • Write three evidence routes on a card: sketch / measurements / provenance.
  • Give each important NPC one understandable priority unrelated to Protocol 7.
  • Decide which earlier mission consequence appears: M03 credentials can grant clean museum access; a weak M03 Carrier means the team's papers are inconsistent and require a human sponsor.
  • Never hide an essential clue behind one roll. Failure changes cost, time, trust, or Exposure.

Cast You Can Play Immediately

Eleni Vardas — junior cataloguer (fictional)

Wants: every recovered object logged before prestige items consume all attention. Fears: being dismissed as clerical labor. Voice: precise, impatient with grand theories. Changes her mind when: someone treats a mundane measurement as seriously as a beautiful statue. She is the human heart of the Carrier.

Nikos Daskalakis — Symian diver (fictional composite)

Wants: his crew's knowledge of where objects lay to be believed. Fears: another diver dying for an object officials value more than the men retrieving it. Leverage: he remembers which fragments were recovered together. Changes his mind when: the Vectors choose a person over an artifact under pressure.

Professor Petros Anagnostou — museum scholar (fictional)

Wants: defensible classification, not sensational claims. Fear: professional humiliation. Voice: “Show me what you know, then show me how you know it.” He can be wrong without being foolish.

Dr. Iona Morrow — Covenant scholar

Wants: the fragments physically safe but the anomalous relationship suppressed. Argument: “A machine can be forgotten. A standard teaches institutions how to understand one another.” Compassion: she will risk her operation to save a diver or junior worker. Line she will not cross: destroying the historical artifact merely to deny Aletheia an advantage.

Revelation Ladder

  • 1. The corroded bronze is more mechanically structured than its appearance suggests.
  • 2. Saving every fragment in one room is not enough; handling has already separated context from object.
  • 3. Three independent records disagree in interpretation but overlap in observable relationships.
  • 4. The overlap—not the correct theory—is the Carrier.
  • 5. Morrow knows this and wants one relationship omitted while preserving everything else.
  • 6. A seven-stroke relationship appears in a provenance sketch whose paper, ink, and custody predate the earliest known Aletheia-authorized insertion.

Clue Architecture — Three Doors Per Conclusion

The object is not the Carrier

  • Physical: two fragments fit a measurement pattern even when stored apart.
  • Human: Nikos remembers them coming from the same small recovery area.
  • Documentary: Eleni's first inventory number sequence shows they arrived together before later sorting.

Independent description is the Carrier

  • Sketch: one worker draws tooth spacing without theorizing about function.
  • Measurement: another records dimensions and orientation.
  • Provenance: a third records where and with what the fragment was found. Any two establish a usable route; all three make it Durable.

The mark predates known Vector operations

  • Custody: the marked sketch was sealed in an ordinary packet before the team's insertion point.
  • Material: ink and paper are mundane and period-correct; no future material is present.
  • Cross-check: Aletheia's insertion ledger and Covenant records independently agree neither side had an operative present when it was made.

Mission Walkthrough

Scene 1 — The Sea Gives Up Bronze

Open on recovery, not briefing. A line snaps or a diver surfaces in distress while a crate shifts. Give the Vectors a choice between protecting people, protecting recovered material, and remaining inconspicuous. Their choice establishes trust and Exposure. The ugly bronze fragments should initially look like background beside spectacular sculpture.

GM cue: Make the players care about the people who recovered the evidence before asking them to care about the evidence.

Scene 2 — The Unimportant Crate

At the sorting point, a crate of bronze fragments is at risk of casual cleaning, separation, or low-priority storage. The Vectors can intervene bureaucratically, socially, physically, or technologically. Future scanning gives immediate certainty but +Exposure; persuading Eleni to document first is slower but clean.

Scene 3 — Athens: Everyone Has a Different Machine

Weeks or months later, evidence is being interpreted. Present three plausible theories. Do not ask players to identify the modern truth. Reward them for separating observation from interpretation. This is where the mission becomes coherent: history does not need the Vectors' answer. It needs records that survive wrong answers.

Scene 4 — Morrow's Offer

Morrow prevents a damaging cleaning attempt or protects Eleni from blame, then approaches the team. She proposes preserving all physical fragments while deleting one cross-reference that makes independent reconstruction easier. Her proposal leaves the famous artifact intact. It weakens only the Carrier. If the players agree with her diagnosis, let them negotiate a narrower system rather than forcing combat.

Scene 5 — The Quiet Machine

The final pressure is administrative, not a supervillain switch: crates must be moved, notes recopied, a worker reassigned, an official wants one authoritative catalogue. The Vectors must distribute the process into at least two independent routes. For Durable, establish all three: sketch, measurements, provenance. The “quiet machine” is revealed to be the network of ordinary records that keeps working when every interpreter is wrong.

Scene 6 — Verification

Show recognizable history: the mechanism survives, scholars continue debating it, later techniques reveal more. Then show the invisible contribution: later researchers can revisit old observations because enough independent evidence endured. Finally reveal the seven-stroke diagram in a packet authenticated as earlier than Aletheia's known temporal program.

Pressure Clocks

Recovery Pressure

1: fragments separated. 2: a witness or provenance detail is about to leave. 3: preservation and immediate human safety conflict. 4: one evidence route is lost; mission remains winnable through the others.

Covenant Pressure

1: Morrow identifies the same relationship. 2: she recruits a sympathetic local. 3: one cross-reference disappears. 4: she forces a public choice where conspicuous intervention risks Exposure.

Simulation Pass — Eight Table Profiles

  • Investigative: risk of solving the mechanism instead of the mission. Fix: repeatedly distinguish observation from interpretation.
  • Combat-forward: risk of inventing a fight. Fix: physical danger comes from recovery and handling; Covenant violence requires player escalation.
  • Cautious: risk of waiting for certainty. Fix: clocks remove evidence routes but never the only clue.
  • Convention-speed: run Scenes 1, 3, 4, 5, 6; combine Scene 2 into Scene 3.
  • Lateral-thinking: if players distribute rubbings, photographs, training, or other historically plausible records, count them by function rather than demanding scripted artifacts.
  • Bad-luck: failed rolls create delay, damaged copies, skeptical officials, or Exposure; essential conclusions remain available through alternate doors.
  • High-Exposure: future scanning can make the technical problem trivial, but creates an anomaly trail M05/M06 can exploit.
  • Sympathetic-to-Covenant: players may suppress one route and still achieve Established through two others. Treat this as a meaningful campaign choice, not failure.

Simulation verdict: r001's Antikythera frame was evocative but contradicted the campaign architecture's intended mid-century standards/process premise and risked making the ancient mechanism itself the point. r002 deliberately keeps Antikythera but redefines the Carrier as reproducible technical documentation. This preserves the title and historical drama while restoring the campaign lesson.

Troubleshooting

“We just steal the mechanism.”

Let them. Verification then shows they preserved an object but destroyed its ordinary historical custody; Carrier remains Fragile until they create independent evidence routes.

“We tell the scholars exactly what it is.”

They can try, but that is high Exposure and undermines historical agency. More importantly, a correct answer transmitted once is still fragile. Ask: who can verify it after you leave?

“Morrow is right.”

Good. Make them choose which interoperability they are willing to preserve. The campaign works better if Covenant arguments sometimes change Vector behavior.

“We missed the mark.”

The mark is campaign evidence, not a perception tax. Put it in the verification packet automatically; rolls can determine extra context.

Outcomes & Continuity

Clean Success: at least two independent evidence routes survive, Exposure 0–2. Exposed Success: Carrier survives but future tech, witnesses, or anomaly records persist. Contained Failure: the artifact survives but reproducible context remains Fragile; M05 loses a clean technical cross-check. Exposed Failure: context is badly damaged and anomalous evidence survives; M06 gains a distorted reconstruction problem.

M03 callback: Durable/Established identity records provide plausible credentials or introductions in Athens. A weak M03 outcome makes institutional access less reliable but never blocks the mission.

M05 seed: the lesson moves from technical standards to civic systems: a reliable process becomes more powerful—and morally harder—when an entire city depends on it.

Debrief — Do Not Cut This

  • What mattered more in the end: the machine or the records around it?
  • Which interpretation did you allow history to get wrong?
  • Was Morrow's fear of interoperability reasonable?
  • Who did you trust with a piece of the Carrier?
  • How can the seven-stroke evidence exist before Aletheia says temporal operations began?
