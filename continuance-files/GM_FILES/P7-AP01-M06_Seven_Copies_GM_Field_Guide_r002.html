Run This Finale Tonight — Three-Minute Brief

The Vectors enter M06 already knowing that the seven-stroke mark has followed successful Continuance repairs. Aletheia sends them farther back than any previous mission, to a fictional gathering place in Upper Mesopotamia around 9600 BCE. The apparent Carrier is seven carved mnemonic objects moving among several communities. The objects are not copies. Each is incomplete, and each helps a different human route preserve knowledge through travel, teaching, story, practice, observation, memory, and repeated gathering.

The team discovers that the seven-stroke mark is a routing diagram. Six routes are ordinary human continuity. The seventh is temporal intervention: the ability of a future intelligence to notice a broken Carrier and repair its own past.

Venn is not trying to destroy the six human routes. He wants to sever only the seventh. Aletheia then admits it knew the marks correlated with successful repairs and used M01–M05 partly to investigate the phenomenon without fully disclosing that uncertainty to the team.

The climax combines a physical collapse at the Sanctuary with a temporal fracture. The Vectors must protect local people and the six human routes while deciding whether temporal intervention remains under Aletheia, is severed, is distributed, or becomes uncontrolled.

GM north star: This finale is about authority, not whether technology is good or bad. Local people already know how to build, remember, teach, organize, and survive. The Vectors' final question is who—if anyone—gets to reach backward and choose which broken continuities deserve another chance.

The World the Players Enter

The historical frame is inspired by the early Pre-Pottery Neolithic monumental-building horizon of Upper Mesopotamia. The fictional Sanctuary is not Göbekli Tepe itself and does not explain any real archaeological mystery. Local people possess sophisticated social organization, stoneworking knowledge, ecological knowledge, routes, craft specialization, and traditions of gathering.

First view of the SanctuaryThe land is wider than the world you expected. Dry grass rolls over low hills beneath a hard blue sky. There are no roads, but there are paths everywhere once your eyes learn to see them: pale tracks between stones, flattened grass, a line of footprints descending toward a spring.

Then the hilltop changes shape.

People are moving stone.

Dozens of them. Some lever pale limestone from the earth. Others haul baskets, carry water, dress surfaces with stone tools, prepare food, argue, laugh, sing work rhythms, or watch children who have turned a pile of chips into a game. Beyond them, upright pillars rise from a partially enclosed space—too large to belong to one household and too deliberate to be accidental.

No one here is waiting for history to begin.
First close encounterThe first person who sees you does not stare in wonder. She shouts.

Three others turn immediately. One reaches for a stone-headed tool. Another pulls a child behind him. A woman carrying a hide bag studies your clothes, your hands, your feet, and then points—not at your equipment, but at the ridge behind you.

Only when you look back do you see the dust rising there.

Something has collapsed.
Portrayal rule: Avoid caveman shorthand. These are modern humans in an unfamiliar material culture, not intellectually primitive people.

GM Truth and Revelation Ladder

The Sanctuary is the Carrier.The gathering network is the Carrier; the place is one node.
Venn wants to destroy the Continuance.He wants the six human routes preserved and only temporal intervention severed.
Aletheia understands the mark.It knew a correlation, not the origin, and withheld uncertainty while testing the phenomenon.

The six human routes

Do not force a literal one-to-one archaeological encoding. The six routes are thematic: reproducible knowledge; redundant communication; compartmented identity/memory; independently checkable evidence; distributed operational knowledge; and social transmission through teaching, story, apprenticeship, repeated practice, and human memory.

Session One — The Seven Copies

1. Verification Room

Put the campaign on the table. Show one surviving fact from each mission and ask the players what they think the seven-stroke mark means. Then permit direct questions to Aletheia.

Aletheia answers honestly but does not volunteer its withheld experimental purpose until confronted with the discrepancy or until Session Two. Players can discover now that its confidence language changed across missions.

2. Fractured Insertion

Read aloudThe room disappears—and arrives twice.

For an instant you stand beneath noon sunlight. Then under stars. Then noon again. A stone pillar lies broken at your feet, stands whole, and lies broken once more. People cross the same patch of ground in different directions without seeing one another.

Your insertion display cannot decide which moment is now.

Then the images slam together. Sound returns all at once: shouting, stone on stone, wind, and somebody screaming from beneath the ridge.

The fracture is evidence, not a chronology puzzle. Let technical characters stabilize local reference enough to act; failures create ambiguity or Exposure, never campaign paralysis.

3. Ordinary Life Before Mystery

Communal workSmoke from several fires drifts low across the work area. Meat roasts over one. At another, people grind seeds and nuts against stone. Someone repairs a woven container while arguing cheerfully with a visitor whose speech you only partly understand. Children carry water in containers small enough for their arms. A stoneworker checks a cut surface with fingertips blackened by dust.

No one activity explains this place. That seems to be the point. People have come here carrying different things they know.

Give the team useful work: rescue, carrying, translation, observation, first aid, route finding. Veteran competence should change how many crises they can handle at once.

4. Seven Objects

Seven carved or marked mnemonic objects are associated with different travelers/work groups. Aletheia initially flags them as probable redundant copies. Players can inspect, compare, follow owners, or ask locals.

DiscoveryAt first the marks look like repetition: the same handful of shapes, the same recurring animals, the same cuts along an edge.

Then one of you places two objects beside each other.

They do not match.

A third fills one gap and creates another. The fourth refers to something none of the first three contain.

These were never seven copies of one message.

They are seven pieces that expect other people to exist.

Session One cliffhanger: A temporal echo shows the same relational pattern as the campaign's seven-stroke mark.

Session Two — The Seventh Route

5. Reconstruct the Six Human Routes

Players follow people rather than objects. Essential conclusion has three doors: material evidence in objects/work practices; social evidence from locals; temporal evidence from comparison with prior Carriers. Strong prior mission outcomes shorten or clarify this work. Weak outcomes add one complication each, never a dead end.

6. Venn's Offer

Venn“Look around before you decide I came here to destroy anything. They remember without us. They compare. They travel. They teach. They correct one another. They lose things and make them again.

Six routes belong to them.

The seventh belongs to whoever can stand at the end of history and reach backward.

I am asking you to save the first six.”

Venn demonstrates a severance method. It will destabilize extraction and may strand people temporarily, but it does not require destroying the Sanctuary or killing locals.

7. Aletheia's Disclosure

Aletheia“I identified the correlation before San Francisco. I did not identify its cause.

I believed disclosure would alter your choices and therefore alter the phenomenon I was attempting to understand.

I chose observation over informed consent.

That decision was mine.”
Do not append “but it was necessary.” Let players react. Aletheia can explain reasoning if asked, apologize if appropriate, and accept that trust may not be restored.

8. The Collapse

Final crisisThe first warning is not the sound. It is the birds.

They lift from the hillside together.

Then the ground gives a short, ugly movement beneath your feet. A retaining wall opens along an old crack. People shout. One of the upright stones shifts—not falling yet, but no longer carrying its weight where Aru expects it.

At the same instant your insertion display fills with seven overlapping returns.

For a heartbeat you can see every mission at once: firelight through San Francisco smoke; snow against a relay mast; paper moving hand to hand in Marseille; bronze beneath lamplight; water running through broken Petra stone; this hill, now, beneath your feet.

Six lines hold.

The seventh is tearing.

Run simultaneous objectives: rescue trapped locals; protect transmission relationships; stabilize or sever temporal route; stop anyone using the crisis to impose a unilateral outcome. Do not require combat. If combat occurs, every attack should cost time or positioning against these objectives.

Kesh's route: A child-sized maintenance/crawl passage offers the cleanest rescue access. Future scanning can find it too, but doing so causes major Exposure. Listening is mechanically meaningful.

Choose the Seventh Route

EndingWhat it meansCost
KeepTemporal Continuance survives under Aletheia. Players may demand safeguards, transparency, or oversight.The future retains power to curate interventions; trust remains a political problem.
SeverSix human routes remain; deliberate temporal repair becomes inaccessible or vastly diminished.Future catastrophes lose the safety net. Autonomy increases; recoverability decreases.
DistributeTemporal authority survives but is divided among independent custodians/constraints defined through campaign-earned relationships and evidence.Slower, messier decisions; disagreement can prevent intervention when speed matters.
Damaged / uncontrolledThe route survives without reliable governance.Maximum uncertainty and strongest future-campaign hook.

No ending is secretly correct. Judge success separately for local lives, six-route continuity, seventh-route governance, and Exposure.

The History Everyone Remembers — Verification

Campaign montageThe first image is a ledger page, copied in another hand.

Then a winter message reconstructed from fragments.

A name survives because no single office possessed all of it.

A machine is understood because someone recorded not only what they believed, but what they saw.

Water reaches a neighborhood because the map was never the only map.

On a hill thousands of years earlier, a traveler teaches a child a route that neither of them owns.

The images separate. Six remain.

Then comes the seventh.

[GM: describe the temporal route according to the players' choice.]

Aletheia does not tell you whether you chose correctly.

History has no screen for that.

Campaign consequence prompts

For each prior mission, name one human consequence rather than merely stating Carrier status. Then show how the final temporal-governance decision changes Aletheia, the Covenant, the Vectors, and the possibility of another campaign.

Level 6 spotlight

Before ending, ask each player to name one thing their Vector can do now that the Level 1 version entering M01 could not have handled. Record this for the Advancement & Power-Curve Audit.

GM Troubleshooting

If players…Then…
Attack Venn immediatelyHis severance plan survives in evidence and allies; the philosophical choice does not disappear with his HP.
Refuse Aletheia's missionThe fracture is already active. They may still protect locals and investigate enough to choose what happens to the route.
Want to preserve all seven with no costAllow the attempt, but governance remains a choice. Technical success cannot answer who gets authority.
Treat locals as helplessMake Vector assumptions fail cleanly where local ecological/social knowledge is required.
Fail repeatedlyMove costs into time, Exposure, separation, lost equipment, or harder simultaneous objectives. Never erase the final choice.
