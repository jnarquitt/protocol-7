1 At a Glance

What is this?

A complete two-session Protocol 7 mission. Session One centers on an impossible 1905 scientific-romance publication and the team's competing interpretations. Session Two inserts the Vectors into the 1906 San Francisco earthquake and fire, where they must identify a forgotten structural survey and turn one vulnerable notebook into a Historical Carrier that can survive the century.

The central choice

The easiest way to preserve Miriam Quill's observations is to spread them widely. Every additional copy strengthens the Carrier—and makes the team's intervention more visible. The mission should force the table to choose between historical durability and temporal cleanliness.

What the players receive

Aletheia supplies a complete, impossible 1905 scientific-romance periodical: The Electrical Argosy, Volume VII, Number III. Its serial, advertisements, letters, technical plate, errata, publication data, handwritten notes, and missing page contain overlapping clues. The opening session is an investigation of the publication itself.

What the GM knows

The mission-critical content is a set of observations showing why some structures survived while neighboring buildings failed. The Vectors do not merely save the work: their choices become the undocumented reason it enters the 1908 earthquake investigation, influences redundant emergency-water planning, and eventually contributes to Sanctuary engineering.

The Apocryphon

Read to the players Aletheia places a cheaply printed magazine on the table. Its cover is dated October 1905. A woman stands before a burning city, holding a blue ledger beneath the headline The City Beneath the Cinders. “No record of this publication exists in baseline history,” Aletheia says. “Its materials are authentic to the period. Its contents describe events that have not yet occurred. The disaster is not your target. Determine what must survive it.”

Give the players P7-AP01-M01_The_Electrical_Argosy_Apocryphon_r001.html. Do not summarize it for them. The former short verse now appears inside the removable technical plate as one clue among many.

GM Note — Interpretation There is no single Interpretation roll. Let players state theories. Skills can investigate a theory; Confirmation Keys can validate a specific claim once the team has enough evidence to formulate it. The three key conclusions are: Quill is the woman, the observations are the Carrier, and independent copies are the leverage point.
Aha architecture The phrase little giant is deliberately unresolved during briefing. It points to a working hydrant at 20th and Church that later becomes the Golden Fire Hydrant. The players may cause the message that sends hose and volunteers there. History remembers the firefighters, volunteers, and hydrant; it does not remember who supplied the decisive route.

2 Before the Table Sits Down

  • Choose whether the Covenant is primarily persuasive, covert, or confrontational for this group.

Period Texture in One Minute

Telephones and telegraphy exist, but most communication is slow and local. Streets are damaged, smoke limits visibility, crowds move unpredictably, and official authority is fragmented. A convincing cover role—surveyor, relief worker, insurance investigator, medical volunteer, telegraph technician—often matters more than a perfect forged document.

3 Content, Tone, and Table Safety

This mission uses a real historical disaster as its backdrop, but the named mission NPCs and the target engineering record are fictional. The disaster should create urgency and human stakes, not spectacle for its own sake.

Content heads-up Earthquake aftermath, fire, smoke, injured civilians, collapsed buildings, panic, firearms, institutional sexism, and difficult choices about who receives scarce help. Historical prejudice can be present as background if the table wants it, but it is not required to run the mission and should never be used as a surprise “gotcha.”
Tone dial For a lighter table, keep casualties mostly offscreen and emphasize rescue, improvised cooperation, and racing the fire. For a harder table, show institutional failure and moral triage, but avoid graphic descriptions unless the group explicitly wants them.
4 The Ash Ledger — GM Truth

Miriam Quill

Miriam Quill is a fictional architectural drafter and field surveyor working for a small insurance engineering office. For months she has been documenting masonry failures, foundation settling, improvised bracing, and firebreak practices. The earthquake gives her a terrible natural experiment: neighboring structures subjected to the same catastrophe fail in visibly different ways.

What baseline history loses

Quill spends the day adding observations in blue pencil to a blue cloth ledger. By the second night, the office where she plans to leave it is consumed by fire. Her surviving notes are scattered, treated as insurance ephemera, and never enter a professional archive.

Why Aletheia cares

In Aletheia's damaged future, a later branch of resilient civil engineering never develops on schedule. Quill's comparisons join the scientific investigation published after the earthquake, then echo through redundant emergency-water planning, distributed utilities, and underground-habitat design. Centuries later, those principles are used in the Sanctuaries. Aletheia knows the future dependency but does not yet know why the corrected record contains evidence of another editor.

Why the Covenant cares

The Covenant agrees the ledger matters. Its Custodians argue that this particular knowledge chain eventually strengthens infrastructure governed by Aletheia's emergency authority. They prefer Quill to survive as a person while her work remains Fragile. Their victory condition is not necessarily Quill's death—it is preventing institutional adoption.

The Remembered History Layer

Use these connections as quiet recognition, never as a lecture. Real people still perform the recorded work. The Vectors create missing links that history absorbs without naming them.

5 Cast of Characters

Miriam Quill — Surveyor

Wants: evidence taken seriously, not credit for heroism.
Fear: that the ledger is just one more thing the fire will erase.
Voice: clipped, practical, impatient with authority.
Tell: blue pencil behind her right ear; counts damaged wall bays under her breath.

Prof. Ada Halden — Structural Lecturer

Wants: Quill's raw comparisons before rumor turns them into folklore.
Fear: publishing too quickly and being professionally discredited.
Leverage: can create an institutional copy across the bay if convinced the observations are methodical.

Elias Rourke — Relief Sergeant

Wants: people out of dangerous streets and no “private investigators” interfering with rescue routes.
Use: checkpoint pressure, local authority, or ally if the Vectors help civilians early.

Clara Morrow — Technical Editor

Wants: reliable field reports for a special engineering bulletin.
Fear: fraud, sensationalism, or impossible claims.
Leverage: publication route. She will print measurements, not prophecy.

Silas Venn — Covenant Custodian

Wants: the ledger to remain one person's private notes.
Belief: Aletheia is selecting the institutional ancestors of its own power.
Method: persuasion first, theft second, violence only if the Carrier is about to become Durable.

Mara Ellery — Covenant Field Agent

Wants: clean containment with minimal public anomaly.
Method: surveillance, forged orders, quiet interception, and recovery of future technology.
Crack in loyalty: she will not knowingly leave trapped civilians to die merely to win the mission.

7 Mission Walkthrough

Scene 1 — Briefing Phase: Briefing

Aletheia briefing The packet contains no map to a person. It contains a newspaper sketch of burning rooftops, a railway timetable, a page of engineering notation, and the story you have already heard. Aletheia adds only this: “The intervention is not the earthquake. The intervention is what survives it.”
GM Notes Let the players investigate the complete Apocryphon. Give them the 1906 city map and cache list only after they begin turning theories into a plan. If they stall, use the Session One discussion prompts; do not name Quill or explain the Little Giant. The errata supplies names, but players must decide whether the corrections are trustworthy.

Scene 2 — The Falling Hour Phase: Insertion

Read on arrival You arrive in an alley already filled with dust. The ground is still moving. Brick falls from a cornice three stories above. Somewhere beyond the roofs, bells are ringing without rhythm and the sky is turning the color of copper. A horse screams. A man in shirtsleeves is trying to pull a child from beneath a collapsed awning. No one is looking at you yet.
Immediate choices The team can move, rescue, hide the insertion, or split tasks. Use Reflex or Athletics DC 12 only when uncertainty matters. Helping the trapped civilians earns Rourke's later cooperation; ignoring them is not punished mechanically, but loses that easy social route.
Insertion signature The temporal arrival is masked by dust, arcing power lines, and aftershock. If a Vector immediately uses conspicuous future technology in the open, start at Exposure 1 instead of 0.

Scene 3 — Borrowed Authority Phase: Establish Cover

Ask each Vector for one sentence explaining why they belong in the district. Prepared period clothing and ordinary tools do not require rolls. Test a cover only when an NPC actively challenges it.

Scene 4 — Three Trails Through Smoke Phase: Investigate

Present at least two leads immediately. The third can appear after any failed investigation so the mission keeps moving.

A. Relief Camp

A clerk remembers a woman with a blue ledger asking where the Pacific Assurance records office moved. Perception or Influence DC 12 gets the name Miriam Quill. A Solid success also reveals she was measuring wall failures rather than casualties.

B. Insurance Annex

The front is damaged and the records room is unsafe. Security DC 12 or Athletics DC 16 reaches a surviving carbon page. Engineering or Investigation DC 12 recognizes a controlled comparison of bracing and foundation behavior.

C. Telegraph Exchange

A clerk has a half-sent message: “QUILL — SEND DUPLICATE MEASUREMENTS — HALDEN — OAKLAND.” Tradecraft, Culture, or Influence DC 12 obtains it cleanly. Failure gets the clue but creates a suspicious clerk or Covenant tail.

D. Street Observation

An Engineer or Scientist can independently compare two nearby structures. Engineering or Science DC 16 confirms Quill is measuring something reproducible, not merely collecting anecdotes.

E. The Dry Line

A fire-company runner carries a list of failed hydrants and one unverified pressure reading from 20th and Church. Engineering, Investigation, or Influence DC 12 connects Quill's note “small main—pressure isolated” with the Apocryphon's “little giant.” The clue is optional for the Carrier but opens the strongest historical echo.

Scene 5 — The Woman With the Blue Pencil Phase: Identify Leverage

Read when Quill is found The woman on the loading dock is writing while the building behind her burns. Her coat is gray with ash. A blue cloth ledger is tucked under one arm. She looks at the nearest leaning wall, makes two short marks with a blue pencil, and says without looking up, “If you're here to tell me to evacuate, take a number.”

Quill refuses to abandon the work until she has copied the day's measurements. She believes the ledger itself is vulnerable and has already arranged two possible routes:

  • Professor Ada Halden, across the bay, will accept raw field measurements if they are methodical.
  • Clara Morrow, a technical editor still operating near the waterfront, can print a short emergency bulletin if persuaded that the claims are supportable.

Quill has also circled 20th and Church beside the words “isolated pressure—verify.” She will not abandon the ledger to investigate it, but she asks the Vectors to pass the observation to any fire company moving south.

Confirmation Key opportunity A player who states “Quill matters because of what she documented, not because she personally must survive” can spend a Confirmation Key. Aletheia returns: CONSISTENT WITH TARGET.

Scene 6 — The Covenant Offer Phase: Intervene

Read when Venn reveals himself The man in the dark work coat does not draw his revolver. He lifts both hands instead. “Save her,” he says, nodding toward Quill. “Take her out of the city. I will help you. But burn the ledger. Ask your machine why it needs this particular future so badly.”
Silas Venn's objective Venn is sincere. He will help rescue Quill if the Vectors agree to keep the observations Fragile. He argues that Aletheia is preserving the institutional ancestors of its own authority. If the party attacks, Ellery supports him from cover. If they debate, use Influence, Tradecraft, Will, or opposed pools as appropriate. Recruitment, compromise, theft, and escape are all valid outcomes.
Escalation rule The Custodians dislike public combat. If Exposure reaches Interest (3+) during this scene, they prefer withdrawal, theft, or turning authorities against the Vectors rather than a prolonged firefight.

Scene 8 — What Did You Leave Behind? Phase: Contain Exposure

Before extraction, review every conspicuous shortcut the team used. Choose one or two surviving traces rather than inventing dozens.

The Photographer

A plate may show an impossible device or impossible wound recovery. Influence or Deception DC 12 can redirect the story; Security or Tradecraft DC 16 can recover the plate quietly.

The Medical Trace

If Trauma Nanogel was used publicly, a medic may retain residue. Medicine or Investigation DC 16 can recover or plausibly destroy it without harming the patient.

The Custodian Cache

If the Covenant recovered Vector technology, that evidence counts too. Recover it, bargain for it, or accept the Exposure and extract.

The Impossible Witness

A single witness is usually Trace, not disaster. The team can explain, discredit, recruit, or simply accept that someone remembers something impossible.

Do not erase every photograph One ordinary photograph should survive if the team used period-safe methods. It shows them only as unidentified people in the crowd. This is not Exposure; it is the first Forrest Gump-style historical footprint. Record who is visible and what each Vector is doing so the image can return later.
Do not erase the Carrier Containment is not “burn every record.” If the easiest way to reduce Exposure would also destroy Quill's Historical Carrier, make the conflict explicit.

Scene 9 — The Ferry Clock Phase: Extract

Read on final approach The Ferry Building clock is visible through smoke before the building itself is. The waterfront is a moving wall of carts, refugees, soldiers, horses, and ash. The minute hand is two minutes from the extraction window. Behind you, another section of the city glows orange.

Extraction requires the team to reach the south arcade at 23:40. If the required Carrier is only Fragile, Aletheia warns: MISSION CONDITION NOT SATISFIED. The team may still choose emergency extraction, but the result will be a failure state.

Final complication Use only one: Rourke stops them for questioning; Ellery makes one last attempt to seize a copy; a fire-driven crowd cuts the route; or an aftershock forces Reflex/Athletics DC 12. Do not add a boss fight merely because this is the last scene.

Scene 10 — Historical Verification Phase: Verification

Read after extraction The city vanishes in a white line. The Electrical Argosy lies open where the team left it. Page 21 is no longer missing. It describes one small, unmistakable choice the Vectors made in 1906. Aletheia has also printed an engineering bibliography, a photograph of a library box, and a later photograph of a little hydrant painted gold. “These records describe the history you remember,” Aletheia says. “That history did not exist until you made it.” The seven-stroke mark appears beneath the newly printed page. “I did not place that there.”
Adventure Path seed The seven-stroke mark is the first recurring mystery of The Continuance Files. It appears on one surviving copy only if the Carrier reaches Established or Durable. Neither Aletheia nor the known Covenant cell claims it.
Write Page 21 at the table Choose a decision unique to the session: a burned glove surrendered with a map, a phrase spoken to Quill, an object hidden in a photograph, or who carried the third copy. Describe it in the Argosy's melodramatic voice. The new page must confirm that the publication changes with history without explaining who changes it.

9 Key Assets, Evidence, and Carrier Objects

The Blue Ledger

Clothbound, soot-stained, filled with measurements and sketches in blue pencil. Alone it is Fragile. It is valuable because the method is understandable and reproducible, not because the object is magical or unique.

Carbon Field Sheets

Partial duplicate observations. Three surviving sheets can become an independent copy if organized with Investigation or Engineering DC 16.

Halden's Telegram

Proof that an independent professional route already exists. It supports Influence with Quill or Halden when materially relevant.

Morrow's Bulletin Proof

Once typeset, this is a second transmission route. Destroying the press does not erase the route if proofs or copies have already left the building.

The Seven-Stroke Mark

Appears only after successful verification. It is not visible during the operation and is not a clue the Vectors can chase in 1906.

Quill's Pressure Note

A penciled circle around 20th and Church with “isolated pressure—verify.” Passing it to a fire-company runner produces an optional historical echo without changing the required Carrier.

The Crowd Photograph

A historically ordinary image may preserve the Vectors as unnamed people moving Quill's packet. Record composition and visible details after play. It becomes a later reveal, not automatic Exposure.

Future-Tech Traces

Any residue, impossible photograph, device fragment, or recovered Covenant gear can become Exposure. Pick traces that follow from actual player choices.

10 Ending the Mission

Clean Success

Read if Carrier met and Exposure ≤ 2 Aletheia projects a thin line of citations across the wall. Quill's name appears only twice. Her method appears hundreds of times under other names, beginning in a landmark investigation of the earthquake. “The Carrier persisted,” Aletheia says. “The intervention is historically ordinary.” A second image shows a small hydrant painted gold. A third shows unnamed figures carrying papers through the smoke. One of them is you. Then the seven-stroke mark appears on a library box that should not contain it.

The path continues with minimal contamination. Quill may or may not be remembered, but the work survives independently.

Exposed Success

Read if Carrier met and Exposure ≥ 3 “The Carrier persisted,” Aletheia says. A second screen opens. A newspaper clipping describes a physician who healed a crushed hand “without scar or stitch.” A private memorandum mentions strangers who knew which buildings would fail before the walls moved. “The intervention also persisted.”

The next operation can inherit a Covenant investigation, a collector of anomalous artifacts, or an archival file created by the Exposure.

Contained Failure

The team leaves little anomalous evidence, but Quill's work remains Fragile or disappears. Aletheia identifies a later historical point where the missing branch can still be repaired. The adventure path continues, but later missions become harder because this continuity node is absent.

Exposed Failure

The work fails to survive and anomalous evidence does. This is the worst result, but not campaign-ending. The seven-stroke mark does not appear; instead, Aletheia detects a new archival investigation into impossible technology. The path's opposition gains leverage before Mission 02.

Debrief Prompts

  • Was Silas Venn wrong about the ledger—or only wrong about what to do?
  • Which choice increased the Carrier most? Which choice increased Exposure most?
  • Did the team preserve Quill's work, Quill's reputation, Quill's life, or some combination?
  • What future technology was almost worth leaving behind?
  • Who does your Vector trust more after the mission: Aletheia, the Covenant, or neither?
  • What remembered piece of 1906 now feels personal because your team helped cause it?

12 Adventure Path Continuity

P7-AP01 — The Continuance Files

Path premise: Aletheia has identified a cluster of apparently unrelated Historical Carriers whose survival greatly improves humanity's resilience centuries later. Each operation places the Vectors invisibly inside remembered history: they do not replace its celebrated people, but cause a route, phrase, photograph, discovery, or “coincidence” the players already recognize. Together those Carriers form a civilization-spanning engineering inheritance that eventually makes the Sanctuaries possible. The Covenant claims the cluster is not neutral: it also creates the institutions and infrastructures that make Aletheia's emergency authority possible. Across the missions, a seven-stroke annotation begins appearing in records that neither side admits altering.

Path Question Is Aletheia preserving humanity's ability to survive—or preserving the historical conditions that make Aletheia indispensable?
