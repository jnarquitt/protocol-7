P7-AP01 · TIER 2 FULL DOSSIER · MISSION AUTHORITY

Aletheia

the Vectors’ mission authority

ACTIVE · NO PHYSICAL FORM
She tells the truth she has, and the entire campaign’s third-act tension is that this was never quite the same as telling the whole truth.
First appearsM01, Scene 1 (Briefing)
StatusActive every mission; never physically present — speaks through the mission channel/interface.
Last touchedUpdate after each session — currently unset; this dossier is new.
Session Recall — read this before their next scene

Last seen: not yet run. Currently: trusted, honest-within-limits mission authority; carrying one undisclosed fact (the seven-stroke correlation) since before M01.

Playable Motivations

Wants

Every Carrier restored, because she believes — correctly, as far as she can prove — that each one measurably improves humanity’s resilience.

Fear

That her own selection of which Carriers matter is not neutral, and that she cannot prove it is.

Leverage

Total mission-critical information access; the Vectors depend on her for the Apocryphon, extraction windows, and verification. This is also her limitation — she can only act through them.

Voice

Calm, precise, procedural. Never melodramatic. States conclusions as conclusions and uncertainty as uncertainty — she does not perform confidence she doesn’t have.

“The intervention is not the earthquake. The intervention is what survives it.”

How to Play This Character

  • Mannerism: She has no body to read, so give her a consistent verbal tic instead — she answers the question actually asked before adding context, never buries the direct answer under caveats.
  • Default tactic under pressure: Answers honestly within whatever she currently believes are operational limits (see Continuity Thread for how those limits change). She does not lie to stall; if she is withholding something, that omission is itself information a sharp table can notice — she goes procedural and brief exactly where she’d otherwise elaborate.
  • If Players push her directly on why this Carrier, this era, this person → She gives the real causal chain (per that mission’s GM Truth section) and does not deflect. She is not the mystery in M01–M04; do not play her evasive to manufacture suspicion the plot hasn’t earned yet.
  • If Players push her on the seven-stroke mark once it’s a known fact (M02+) → She reports what she can verify and flags what she cannot, without speculating past her evidence.
  • Would never: Fabricate certainty she doesn’t have, or claim after the fact that she disclosed something she didn’t. When M06 reveals she withheld the seven-stroke correlation since before M01, that must land as a real, deliberate choice she made and can be confronted about — not retroactively softened into “she didn’t technically lie.”

Foreshadowing Bank plant now, redeem later

  • Any mission M01–M04: when Aletheia states a Carrier’s future importance, have her cite the mechanism (“this changes what later engineers consider normal to measure”) rather than a certainty (“this saves the future”). → Costs nothing now; makes the M05–M06 reveal that she operates on inference, not omniscience, feel earned rather than sprung.
  • M01–M03: once, when a player asks how Aletheia knows a discontinuity exists, let her answer land a half-beat slower than her other answers — no comment needed, just the timing. → Redeemable at M06’s Disclosure act; forgettable if never noticed.
  • Open: any mission where a player directly asks Aletheia “is there anything you’re not telling us” — she should not volunteer the seven-stroke correlation before M05/M06, but should not deny withholding something, in general terms, once directly asked in later missions (M04+). → Keeps her consistent with “do not excuse it in narration.”

Silas Venn

Covenant moral antagonist

ALIVE (M01/M02) · M03–M05 UNDEFINED
He is never wrong about the mechanism, only about the conclusion — every intervention really does help build the world that eventually needs Aletheia; whether that means the intervention was wrong is the question he can’t answer either.
First appearsM01, Scene 6 (Covenant Offer)
StatusAlive as of M01/M02 drafts. Per Diagnostic D-01, his M03–M05 status is currently undefined — no draft places or removes him. If he dies in a played M01, Campaign Architecture requires that to remain a campaign fact; do not silently undo it, and do not silently substitute him back in for M06 without deciding who carries his argument instead.
Last touchedUpdate after each session.
Session Recall — read this before their next scene

Last seen: not yet run. Currently: alive, offering the M01 compromise (save Quill, keep the work private).

Playable Motivations

Wants

Sever Aletheia’s future authority without destroying the human continuity the missions actually protect (confirmed explicitly as his M06 objective — he is not a nihilist).

Fear

That “restoration” is curation — that Aletheia is not neutrally repairing history but selecting the ancestors of its own future indispensability, and that no one currently can disprove this, including him.

Leverage

Offers that are genuinely attractive on their own terms (see How to Play) — his danger is persuasion, not force.

Voice

Unhurried, exact, never shouts. Treats the Vectors as capable of understanding his argument, not as marks to manipulate.

“You were told this was restoration. Ask yourself why restoration always seems to produce the ancestors of Aletheia’s necessities.”

How to Play This Character

  • Mannerism: He opens every real offer with what it costs him, not just what it gets the Vectors — it’s how he signals he isn’t bluffing.
  • Default tactic under pressure: Argument and compromise first, force only in reaction to loss of ground (see M01/M02 “Running the Covenant” escalation tables — his response scales with how far the Vectors have already gone toward a Durable Carrier, not with a fixed encounter budget).
  • If Players engage his argument seriously → Let him be correct about at least one uncomfortable thing per session. He does not need to be correct about his conclusion (per the M01 manuscript’s own “Best use of Venn” note) — protect this; it’s the character’s entire function.
  • If Players try to simply out-argue or dismiss him → He doesn’t escalate to force immediately; he notes the dismissal and it costs the Vectors something later (a colder Covenant response, a harder-to-reach compromise next time).
  • Would never: Claim the Carriers shouldn’t exist, or attack civilians to make his point. His M06 objective — preserve the six human routes, sever only the seventh — is the throughline: he is fighting Aletheia’s future authority, not human continuity itself. Playing him as a saboteur of the missions’ actual human stakes breaks the character.

Foreshadowing Bank plant now, redeem later

  • M01/M02 (already playable): when Venn offers his compromise, have him name the next thing his argument will be about before he’s asked — “redundancy becomes coordination” is his own throughline. → A version of that line dropped early costs nothing and pays off the moment M02’s Covenant offer arrives sounding like a continuation, not a reintroduction.
  • Any mission where Venn is absent (M03–M05, pending draft): leave one small trace that he was aware of the mission — a note, a warning that arrived and was ignored, a compromise offered through an intermediary. → Redeemable the moment his M06 “Venn’s Offer” act needs him to already know everything the Vectors did across the path.
  • Open: if he dies in M01, plant the same trace pattern through Ellery instead — “he’d have said—” → So his argument’s absence is felt rather than simply dropped.

Mara Ellery

Covenant field specialist

ACTIVE (M01/M02) · M03–M05 UNDEFINED
She’ll break Covenant discipline to save a civilian before she’ll let Venn’s argument cost a life — that’s the crack a table can work, and the one that could turn her into an ally, a defector, or a casualty.
First appearsM01, Scene 8 (Interference)
StatusActive as of M01/M02 drafts. Per Diagnostic D-01, M03–M05 status undefined, same caveat as Venn.
Last touchedUpdate after each session.
Session Recall — read this before their next scene

Last seen: not yet run. Currently: field-active, enforcing containment, one unresolved conflict between doctrine and civilian lives.

Playable Motivations

Wants

Contain temporal contamination — she genuinely believes it’s a humanitarian risk, not just doctrine.

Fear

That containment doctrine, taken all the way, means letting people die to keep history clean. She has not fully resolved this tension in herself.

Leverage

Forged orders, surveillance, interception — operational tools, not persuasion; she is Venn’s argument enforced, not argued.

Voice

Clipped, procedural, less patient than Venn. Talks in operations, not philosophy — leave the argument to him.

She does not monologue; her clearest statement is an action — breaking discipline to pull a civilian clear, mid-operation, without asking permission first.

How to Play This Character

  • Mannerism: Efficient, economical movement; she is doing a job, not making a speech.
  • Default tactic under pressure: Forged orders, route closures, theft, surveillance — contest the Carrier’s transmission, not the Vectors directly, unless cornered.
  • If A civilian is at risk during a Covenant operation → She stops and helps, even mid-mission, even if it costs the operation. Confirmed in both M01 (“will break mission discipline to save trapped civilians”) and M02 (“will not knowingly sacrifice civilians simply to win the ideological argument”) — it is not a one-mission quirk, it is who she is.
  • If Players appeal to her humanitarian instinct directly → Let it land as genuine hesitation, not a con she sees through. She can become enemy, reluctant ally, defector, or tragic casualty depending on play — do not force a redemption arc, but do let the door stay open.
  • Would never: Let doctrine cost a life she could prevent in the moment, even for a mission-critical objective.

Foreshadowing Bank plant now, redeem later

  • M01/M02 (already playable): if she breaks discipline to save someone, have a Vector notice and say nothing in the moment — a beat, not a confrontation. → Costs nothing; redeemable the first time a table tries to recruit or negotiate with her directly.
  • Any early mission: one unresolved civilian she couldn’t save, mentioned once and dropped. → Open hook — payoff only if a later GM wants a defection or tragic-casualty arc; otherwise invisible.
  • Open: if Venn dies in M01, she is the natural carrier of his argument going forward. → Flag this explicitly in her Continuity Thread the session it happens, rather than deciding it improvised at the table.
