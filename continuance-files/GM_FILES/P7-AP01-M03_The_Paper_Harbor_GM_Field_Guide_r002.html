The Paper Harbor

Marseille, France · November 1940 · GM Field Guide r002 · Identity, bureaucracy, rescue, and the danger of being remembered

Run This Tonight — Two-Minute Brief

The whole story: Marseille is crowded with refugees trying to leave Vichy France through a maze of visas, exit permissions, transit documents, sponsors, consular requirements, aid offices, and unofficial escape routes. The Vectors are sent to preserve what appears to be a single rescue registry containing identities that history loses. That is the false objective. One master list is too dangerous and too fragile. The true Carrier is a compartmented verification method: several independent records, none sufficient to expose the whole network, that can corroborate a person's identity when one office, passport, or file disappears.

Players initially think

Protect a list of endangered people and get it into safe hands.

GM knows

The list must be divided. The Carrier is the relationship between partial records, not any one ledger.

Covenant argument

Burn centralized lists because records that save refugees can also become records used to find them.

Dramatic question

Is a person safer when institutions remember them—or when institutions cannot?

Five beats: queue and confusion → protect one vulnerable identity → discover the master list → realize it is dangerous → divide verification across independent hands → verify the mark predates the team's local intervention.

Historical Background That Matters

Marseille in 1940 was a major refuge and transit point for people trying to escape Europe. American relief organizations operated there, the American consulate remained important to visa efforts, and refugees often needed multiple documents and permissions to move legally. Real rescue workers—including Varian Fry and colleagues of the Emergency Rescue Committee—helped people obtain documents, money, routes, and in some cases forged papers or clandestine passage. This adventure does not replace their work or assign their achievements to the Vectors.

The setting matters because identity itself has become logistical. A person's name, nationality, sponsor, visa status, address, family relationship, and travel permissions may exist in different offices that do not reliably share information. A destroyed passport can strand someone. A perfectly complete registry can save them—or condemn everyone on it if seized.

Historical boundary: Real historical refugees and rescuers retain agency and credit. The fictional Carrier operates beside documented rescue networks. Do not turn real persecution into backdrop decoration or make future operatives the secret authors of historical rescue.

Cast You Can Play Immediately

Leonie Marchand — Fictional Aid Clerk

Wants: people to stop disappearing between offices. Fear: her records becoming an arrest list. Voice: brisk, tired, unsentimental. Key line: “A perfect list is perfect for whoever takes the office.”

Yakov Sorel — Fictional Refugee Bookkeeper

Wants: proof that his wife and daughter exist in the same bureaucratic world he does. Use: makes identity verification human rather than abstract.

Madeleine Roche — Fictional Consular Assistant

Wants: documentation she can defend to superiors. Use: legitimate institutional route, but only for partial corroboration.

Marcel Venn — Covenant Advocate

Wants: every endangered person aided and every central list burned. Argument: memory creates rescue and surveillance with the same machinery.

Mission Walkthrough

Scene 1 — The Queue Without a Door

Open at a relief office with too many people and too few answers. Someone's papers have become inconsistent after an office closure. Let the Vectors help before they understand the larger plot. Purpose: show that identity is a practical survival resource.

Scene 2 — A Person Who Exists in Three Places

Sorel's family can be verified only by comparing fragments held by different people. The group experiences the logic of cross-reference before seeing the master registry. Purpose: teach the future Carrier through play.

Scene 3 — The Harbor Book

Marchand reveals a compact registry connecting names, addresses, sponsors, and status. It seems like the answer to everything—and therefore looks exactly like the Carrier. Purpose: create the false objective.

Scene 4 — The Search Order

A legitimate or quasi-legitimate search is coming. The book can save people today but expose many more if seized. Give the team several ways to respond: hide it, move it, forge a decoy, divide it, or negotiate access. Purpose: turn preservation into risk.

Scene 5 — Venn's Offer

“I will help you get every living person on that book out of danger. Then we burn the book. You call it memory because you imagine the right people will always control the archive.”

Venn's proposal is humane in the short term. He will help with an immediate rescue. Purpose: make the ideological choice credible before escalation.

Scene 6 — Divide the Harbor

The Vectors can create independent partial records: an aid-office index, a consular verification card, a household receipt, a transit note, or another historically plausible route. No single copy should expose the whole network. Two independent routes plus a reproducible reconciliation method make the Carrier Established; wider institutional adoption can make it Durable.

Scene 7 — Raid, Fire, or Closure

Use the pressure appropriate to the table: a search, office closure, document seizure, street pursuit, or hurried evacuation. Combat is optional and objective-driven. Purpose: force the team to choose what record survives where.

Scene 8 — The Card That Was Already Marked

Before extraction, or during verification, the team authenticates a seven-stroke notation on a partial record whose date and custody clearly predate their insertion. This is the M03 campaign revelation.

Scene 9 — Verification

Aletheia shows people whose identity later remained provable despite lost primary papers. Then show the darker side: some descendant institutions also become better at identifying populations. Do not announce a moral verdict. Let the table own it.

Outcomes

Clean Success

Carrier Established/Durable; Exposure 0–2. Verification shows ordinary paper trails preserving identity without a central master archive.

Exposed Success

The method survives, but an anomaly file, impossible document, or conspicuous intervention survives with it.

Contained Failure

Immediate people are helped, but the verification method does not propagate. Later history must solve identity loss another way.

Exposed Failure

Carrier fails and the intervention leaves evidence useful to the Covenant or another investigator.
