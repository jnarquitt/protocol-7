JADE LION STUDIOS • DESIGN SAMPLE

RUSTWIND

A COMPLETE SETTING ON THE PROTOCOL DICE ENGINE

Designed on spec, the way I've built the last dozen of these — engine first, then find the genre that makes the engine's own math mean something. Built from the reskinning toolkit, following its own rules on itself.

The Pitch

Ten years after the war, the railroad hasn't reached Rustwind County yet, and something in the ground doesn't want it to. Prospectors dig up things that shouldn't be buried that shallow. Preachers hold services for congregations half of whom stopped breathing years back. The dust settles wrong more nights than it settles right. You're not chosen. You're not special. You're just still standing, still owed money, and the only doctor in three counties just asked you to escort a coffin that keeps trying to open itself.

Dramatic question: when the frontier itself is the thing getting stranger, does a person get harder, or get out?

Tone: Deadwood's grime, not superhero-with-a-hex. The supernatural is rare, costly, and never fully under a character's control — that belief drives three separate mechanical decisions below, and I'll flag each one when we get there.

Omens & Reckoning

Design call #1. Protocol 7's VAMs are a loadout you swap between missions. Rustwind's supernatural isn't a loadout — it's a debt. So Omens load the same way VAMs do (a capacity budget, explicit dice only, never an implied bonus), but the budget is deliberately small and the fiction is explicit that every Omen came from somewhere you'd rather not explain at Sunday service.

Capacity budget: Reckoning, cap 14 at Rank 1 (Protocol 7's BAR ships at 16 — I shaved two off on purpose; this frontier doesn't hand out power easily, and a tighter cap makes every loaded Omen a real choice instead of a shopping list).

Nine Omens, catalog cost 26 against a 14 cap — same over-subscribed catalog shape as Protocol 7's 84 VAMs against a 16 BAR. Nobody's meant to carry the whole shelf.

Design call #2. A Skill trained past d12 doesn't get a bigger die anywhere in this engine — there isn't one. It unlocks a second identical die, gated behind something explicit. In Rustwind that gate is called Marked, and I made a hard rule about it: a player cannot buy it. It's a scar the GM hands out, tied to a specific bargain, moment, or loss in that character's own story. I've watched every system where the ceiling-breaker was purchasable get optimized into "step one of chargen," and it flattens exactly the thing that made the ceiling interesting. Gate the power behind fiction the player doesn't control, and the ceiling stays a story instead of a shopping decision.

Advantage, Disadvantage, and the Dust Settling Wrong

Same mechanic as baseline — add or remove one die, cancel 1-for-1, cap at one effective side per roll. Only the trigger list is local:

AdvantageDisadvantage
High ground or solid coverPinned down, no cover
You drew first / caught them flatSpooked or unfamiliar mount
Home turf, trail you knowOn cursed or wrong-feeling ground
An ally's covering youOut of ammo, gear half-broken
You read the weather/terrain right beforehandBlind panic — something got under your skin

Blank faces (1–3, unchanged from baseline) get one line of local flavor per band so a GM never has to invent it cold at the table:

Grit & Steel blank"Your shot goes wide, or the hammer just clicks."
Parley blank"The words come out flat — no offense given, none taken, nothing gained."
Reckoning blank"Whatever you reached for isn't listening tonight."

Character Creation

  1. Assign d8, d8, d6, d6, d4, d4 across Brawn, Quickness, Grit, Savvy, Cool, Charm.
  2. Spend 20 Ranks across the seventeen Skills.
  3. Load Omens up to Reckoning 14.
  4. Pick two items of Kit.
  5. Hide Ritual — roll each Attribute die once. If any face is 4+, starting Hide (this setting's HP-equivalent) is 15 + the highest qualifying face. All six blank: flat 24. (Baseline runs 18+face / 28-flat; I dropped both numbers a few points on purpose — Rustwind is meant to run a notch grittier, and starting toughness is the cheapest lever that actually delivers that at the table without touching a single formula.)
  6. Pick a Calling (below) as a shortcut through steps 1–4, fully rearrangeable afterward — same non-binding-preset principle the base engine uses.

A Word From the Design Chair

I've shipped systems where the fun was in the exception — the one feat that broke the rule, the one spell that ignored the math. Those are fun to design and miserable to balance, and after forty years I trust a table that can see every die in the pool more than I trust one that has to take my word for what a +3 quietly means. Every die in Rustwind's pool has a name. That's not a style choice. It's the whole reason a new player can sit down, watch one roll get built out loud, and understand the entire game before anyone hands them a rulebook.
The two numbers I actually changed here — the Reckoning cap and the Hide Ritual's baseline — I changed for a reason I could say out loud in one sentence each. That's the bar. If you can't say why a number is different in one sentence, it isn't a design decision, it's a hunch, and hunches are how you end up with a rulebook nobody can defend at the table three years later.

— Jade Lion Studios, Design Chair, on Rustwind's first pass
