What the Dice Mean

Protocol 7 treats the dice in your hand as a physical description of the character attempting the action. A roll is not one abstract number followed by a pile of anonymous bonuses. The Skill die tells us how practiced you are. Three Ability dice tell us which parts of you matter to this particular task. Gear, a VAM, Advantage, and eventually Mastery can each enter the pool for their own identifiable reason.

That means the GM can look at a handful of dice and understand something about the person acting. Strength may be carrying one action while Dexterity dominates another. Intelligence can matter twice in a deeply technical Skill. A useful tool is visible as a Gear die. Preparation can be visible as Advantage. The dice are doing part of the descriptive work that other games often hide behind modifiers.

Protocol Dice: roll the complete pool together. Faces 1–3 are blank. Every face of 4+ contributes its visible value. Add the contributing faces.

The Vitality Ritual

Hit Points are not simply flesh. They are the accumulated resources that let a person remain effective when events turn dangerous: toughness, reflexes, awareness, judgment, confidence, experience, luck, positioning, and the countless small choices that turn a potentially decisive hit into something survivable.

Because no single Ability owns all of that, Protocol 7 asks all six Abilities to speak.

Roll STR. Write down the face. Roll DEX. Write it down. Continue through CON, INT, WIS, and CHA.

As with all Protocol Dice, 1–3 are blanks. If any die shows 4+, find the highest face. Starting HP is 18 + that face. With the Level 1 Ability array, ordinary starting HP is therefore 22–26.

If all six dice are blank, you have hit the statistical oddball—the draft's Mr. Irrelevant moment. Instead of being punished, the Vector gets the special result: 28 starting HP.

If two or more Abilities tie for the highest qualifying face, choose one of them as the Vector's Vitality Origin. It tells you something about why this person survives. It does not add another modifier.

Build a Roll

The ordinary pool is one Skill die + the three Ability dice printed for that Skill. Repeated Abilities are intentional: a Skill showing DEX / DEX / WIS rolls Dexterity twice.

  • Add one primary relevant Gear die when applicable.
  • Add a VAM die only when the VAM explicitly says it does.
  • Resolve Advantage and Disadvantage.
  • Add one Mastery die only when the Skill is Mastered and explicit Mastery access is active.

There is no generic bonus-die slot and no routine +1/+2 modifier layer.

Advantage & Disadvantage

Advantage: add one d6. Disadvantage: remove the smallest Ability die in the pool. Reasons cancel one-for-one; after cancellation, a remaining side applies once. Multiple same-side reasons normally do not stack.

Players should actively use tactics, preparation, teamwork, knowledge, positioning, and resources to create Advantage. The GM decides when the fiction supports it; specific rules can trigger it automatically.

Combat

3 AP per turn.
Attack: 1 AP.
Move: 1 AP per 30 feet. Spend 2 AP for 60 feet or 3 AP for 90 feet. There is no universal Run multiplier.

Protocol 7 uses active defense. The attacker rolls the appropriate Combat Skill pool. The defender rolls the appropriate Defense Skill pool. Relevant armor normally contributes its Gear die to the defense pool.

If the attacker does not exceed the defense total, normal damage is not inflicted. If the attacker wins, damage equals the winning margin. Armor normally does not then subtract damage a second time.

Reaction

You normally have one Reaction between turns. Unspent AP from your turn remains banked to pay for it. Spend banked AP first. Only when none remains may you borrow at most 1 AP from your next turn. Unused banked AP expires when that next turn begins.

v0.188 deliberately does not add a universal critical-hit subsystem. Explicit weapon, VAM, Adventure, or special-action triggers still work when their rules say they do.

VAMs, BAR & Gear

BAR is capacity, not fuel. Loading into unused BAR costs 0 AP. During active play, replacing or unloading already-loaded VAMs costs 1 AP for the entire field swap, not per VAM.

The playtest catalog contains 84 VAMs in Combat, Defense & Mobility, Recon & Investigation, Social & Cover, Technical & Medical, and Command & Temporal families.

Gear is concrete capability. Normally only one primary relevant Gear die applies to a roll. Gear is not Advantage. Weapons contribute their Gear die to appropriate attacks; they do not carry a second universal damage die. The v0.188 purpose-built catalog includes weapons, armor, field tools, medical and technical equipment, recon devices, identity/cover tools, and historical-support equipment.

Conditions

The six core conditions are Prone, Grappled, Suppressed, Stunned, Disoriented, and Restrained. Conditions that create Disadvantage provide a named reason for the universal Disadvantage rule; they do not invent their own penalty dice.

Level 2 Edges are Driven, Inspired, Prepared, Connected, and Durable. Mastering a Skill does not automatically add a die: a Mastered Skill also needs explicit Mastery access. When active, its Mastery die equals the current Skill die, with at most one Mastery die per roll.

Historical Operations

Carrier and Exposure are competing objectives. Carrier states are Fragile → Established → Durable. A mission specifies how strong the Carrier must be to survive Historical Verification while the team tries to contain Exposure.

Mission outcomes are Clean Success, Exposed Success, Contained Failure, and Exposed Failure.

The operating rhythm is: Briefing → Insertion → Establish Cover → Investigate → Identify Leverage → Intervene → Establish Carrier → Contain Exposure → Extract → Historical Verification.

v0.188 Playtest

This release is meant to be played, challenged, and changed. The goal is a coherent system, not a claim that every Gear die, VAM cost, or high-level interaction is already perfectly balanced.

Matched Blanks / Dice Tricks are deferred. A universal critical subsystem is deferred. Those ideas can return through deliberate testing rather than accidentally entering the core rules.
